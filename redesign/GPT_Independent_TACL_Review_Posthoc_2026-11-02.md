# Independent review of revised TACL manuscript after post hoc checks

**Date:** 2026-11-02  
**Manuscript:** `tacl/main.pdf` (current Google Drive version, modified approximately 06:30 UTC), source `tacl/main.tex`, titled *Detecting Shared Ancestry Among Language-Model Reports*. Twelve PDF pages, with main text and reproducibility statement ending on page 8.  
**Evidence read:** current PDF and TeX, `build/runs/full/analysis/review_checks.md`, November 2 entries of `build/LOG.md`, prior independent review, and TACL's official aims and submission instructions. I did not rerun the pipeline or audit the code of `analyze_review_checks.py`. The source report gives results, intervals, and analysis status, not a full independent reproduction.

## Decision on venue

**Retain TACL for one submission, but classify the contribution as borderline on significance, not on correctness. Do not change venues solely because the untrained score nearly saturates the binary task. Equally, do not invest in another prolonged prose-polish cycle before settling the contribution statement.**

TACL expressly welcomes empirical/data-driven and resource/evaluation papers and judges originality, technical strength, significance, correctness, and relevance, not novelty of a detector alone (Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics [TACL], n.d.). ACL-family reviewing guidance also cautions against treating a simple solution, an intuitive result, or a non-SOTA method as automatic grounds for rejection (ACL Rolling Review [ARR], n.d.). These policies establish eligibility, not a favorable predicted decision.

The publishable empirical question is narrower and more consequential than 'can a classifier detect common ancestry?': **when terminal reports are generated through matched procedures from the *same* event facts, and no single-output distribution is designed to differ, how much output-only information about a shared upstream text survives one rewrite?** The controlled independent-versus-out-star comparison, the equivalence checks on nonshared terminal texts, the partial Y task, and producer comparisons address that question. Near-ceiling performance of an inexpensive score is a result, not a falsification of the research question. It demonstrates that a standard lexical overlap measure suffices for two producers under this especially constrained observation condition.

However, the case for **TACL-level significance** is fragile for reasons that the score makes harder to ignore. The main phenomenon is a predictable consequence of a common sampled parent inducing between-report dependence. The paper estimates its **observable magnitude in one short-report, one-rewrite synthetic task** but does not explain a surprising mechanism, establish robustness to deeper rewriting or mixed-producer sets, provide a reusable public evaluation resource, or show operational deployment. Related work has long used shared variants, shingles, and text reuse to infer copying relationships. An NLP referee may reasonably ask what new principle is established beyond 'rewrites of one source share more rare phrases than independent paraphrases of the same record.' The present answer, a matched design and quantified boundary, is legitimate but not yet decisive.

My venue advice is therefore **a calculated TACL attempt if field-specific recognition justifies a potentially unfavorable novelty review**. It is not a claim the manuscript comfortably exceeds the journal's bar. If efficient acceptance is the overriding objective, the existing Scientific Reports fallback has a more compatible emphasis on scientific validity and technical soundness rather than perceived importance (Scientific Reports, n.d.), although suitability and acceptance remain editorial judgments. More stylistic polishing will not change this tradeoff. A genuinely stronger TACL case would require a broader scientific conclusion or additional, discriminating empirical variation, not a more elaborate detector.

## What the new checks establish

### 1. Nonshared single texts

`review_checks.md` retains reports 2 and 3 from each independent and out-star set, removing terminal texts that are identical across those structures by construction. It analyzes 720 reports per producer and states that no texts coincide across the compared structures. Both tested learners meet the existing equivalence rule (90% world-cluster interval contained in AUC 0.43–0.57):

- Gemini: feature AUC 0.527 [0.496, 0.559], embedding 0.490 [0.469, 0.509].
- Haiku: feature 0.511 [0.485, 0.538], embedding 0.513 [0.494, 0.533].
- Luna: feature 0.538 [0.512, 0.566], embedding 0.486 [0.473, 0.497].

This is an appropriate diagnostic response to a consequential reviewer concern: the original single-report baseline involved literal duplicate texts assigned different structure labels, which mechanically dilutes discriminability. The new restriction prevents that particular dilution. It does **not** show that *all possible* single-text functions are uninformative, nor is it a fresh independent sample. The marginal equality is a property of the generating design, while equivalence is an empirical conclusion restricted to these feature and embedding learners, this selected subset, and the stipulated equivalence band. That separation must govern the abstract and discussion.

### 2. Untrained scores

Using each pairwise statistic directly as a score, without label-trained weights, the largest rare-word-sequence Jaccard overlap yields AUC 0.863 [0.829, 0.895] for Gemini, 0.985 [0.975, 0.993] for Haiku, and 0.984 [0.972, 0.994] for Luna. The trained relational model yields 0.908, 0.982, and 0.973, respectively. The manuscript now presents this in the abstract and §4.1, with all nine inspected scores in Appendix B. That is the right priority and the right scientific concession.

Two distinctions matter. First, **untrained means no supervised classifier fitting**, not an absolute external-corpus-free score. Rare n-grams are defined using document-frequency tables from fold-specific training texts, with the count threshold specified in Methods. Clarify this in one phrase, preferably 'without supervised fitting,' because the score still depends on a corpus-derived rarity definition. Second, 0.985 versus 0.982, and 0.984 versus 0.973, are **descriptive comparisons on the same sets**, not established performance advantages. The separate confidence intervals do not provide paired uncertainty for their differences. Among nine post hoc statistics, the maximum was also selected after observing the data, so intervals for that selected statistic do not incorporate selection uncertainty. No separate replication is reported. Appendix B makes the comparison transparent, but the text should not infer that one procedure statistically outperforms another.

The simple-score result addresses **binary independent-versus-out-star discrimination**. It is not a validated substitute for three-structure classification, five-configuration assignment, pair localization conditional on Y, or producer-transfer tasks. The paper should preserve those distinct inferential tasks instead of implying that the trained models have no further role.

## Consistency of the new framing

**Introduction (§1, PDF pp. 1–2): largely consistent.** It now states the matched marginal construction, differentiates separate drafting from independent acquisition of facts, defines the pooled and relational representations, identifies the held-out AUC gain as an incremental feature comparison rather than an information-theoretic bound, and names the bundled observer-call count. The identification logic is accessible within two pages. The title uses 'Detecting' in the ordinary sense of a task and does not by itself promise a novel detector.

**Results (§4.1, PDF pp. 4–5): the most successful revision, with two residual problems.** The untrained overlap result follows the supervised estimates immediately, and both checks are called review-triggered, post hoc analyses. But the sentence 'only for the primary producer is the trained model clearly higher' needs either a paired comparison or a more descriptive construction. Later, 'the trained models matter mainly for the producer whose rewrites overlap least' extends a binary-task result to an unspecified range of tasks. Limit both statements to the binary task and to observed AUC ordering. The explanation that 'training is not needed for most of this discrimination' is defensible within this sample, but not yet as an out-of-sample conclusion about a preselected statistic.

**Three-way and configuration results (§4.2, PDF pp. 5–6): still defensible, but underleveraged for the venue argument.** These are harder tasks than the binary contrast. The conditional 88.3% odd-report identification is not 88.3% exact configuration recovery. The manuscript correctly reports 64.1% unconditional five-configuration accuracy and the decomposition of Y errors. It should not abandon these findings when conceding that binary ancestry detection is easy for a lexical statistic. The best novelty argument concerns *which* ancestry relationships can be recovered under the controlled observation design, and where performance fails, not building a classifier.

**Ablation (§4.3) and boundary (§4.4): properly calibrated.** The ablation is treated as redundancy among correlated features, not causal isolation of a semantic channel. Partial access remains explicitly confounded. Prompted readers are described as a different supervision/evaluation regime. The scope is consistent with the earlier critique.

**Discussion (§5, PDF p. 8): one sentence still overstates the empirical check.** 'Information about a shared parent is carried by the joint distribution of the reports and not by any single report' follows as a statement about the *designed marginal distributions*, but the immediate shift to tested predictive results may make it read as a universal claim about classifiers. Distinguish the formal design fact from the observed equivalence for the two learners. The claim 'what the design contributes is not a detector but a controlled comparison' is a correct reframing, though it would carry more argumentative weight if followed by the genuinely nontrivial discrimination boundary, including the constrained depth and conditional Y performance, rather than by another restatement that reports are relational.

**Limitations (§6): adequate and appropriately early.** One rewrite, common synthetic worlds and genre, single-producer sets, nonadversarial paraphrase, no graph reconstruction, conditional inference, and post hoc status are all declared. The restriction to one rewrite is not a generic future-work caveat. It is the central limitation on the paper's substantive generality and hence its TACL significance. There is no need to hide or repeat it more often.

**Abstract: one material correction required.** It foregrounds the selected untrained overlap statistic and the 'equivalent to chance' result without labeling either as post hoc or identifying the two single-report learners. The Results and Limitations do label them, but an abstract reader could mistake these for prospective tests and read 'single reports' as the full class of possible isolated-text measurements. A compact repair: 'In post hoc checks, a score based on maximum rare-sequence overlap ...; two single-report learners, tested on texts not shared across structures, met a predefined chance-equivalence band.' This recognizes that the *comparison and selected subset* are post hoc even if the equivalence band itself predates them.

## Post hoc labeling and inferential language

**Overall verdict: substantively adequate in the body, incomplete in the abstract, and not quite adequate for comparative superiority.** Methods §3.4 says analyses other than the primary tests were exploratory and identifies the planned transfer and replication. Results §4.1 specifically states that the new checks were added in response to review. Limitations §6 calls them post hoc. The appendix table caption identifies the untrained-score check as post hoc. These are appropriate disclosures and do not retroactively promote analyses into prespecified findings.

Two short clarifications would make the reporting resistant to review: (i) state that nine unsupervised statistics were inspected and that maximum rare overlap was highlighted after inspecting their performance, and (ii) characterize all intervals as conditional on the score and samples actually examined, without multiple-comparison or score-selection correction. The report's result tables already supply the requisite disclosure data. Do not call the post hoc AUC a replication. Do not call an untrained score *pretrained*, *untuned*, or *parameter-free*: the rarity frequency threshold and reference construction remain analytic choices. If a statistical claim that untrained overlap *outperforms* a trained model is intended, a paired uncertainty analysis would be necessary, and any test added now would also be post hoc.

## Main likely TACL referee objections and whether the manuscript answers them

1. **'This is ordinary shingling of closely related paraphrases, not a new NLP contribution.'** The manuscript partially answers: controlled same-facts comparison, matched marginals, three producers, Y structures, pair localization, transfer. The answer is credible but does not eliminate the significance concern. A referee can accept the evidence while judging the generalizable conceptual advance too small.
2. **'Why study only one rewrite of 220-word synthetic incident reports?'** The limitation is candidly stated, but its effect is not answered by existing evidence. The small single-report trace weakening after additional relays does not establish that the relational signal persists or disappears at greater depth. This is the most substantive unresolved boundary.
3. **'You selected the strongest simple score after looking at nine.'** The nine are displayed, and the post hoc status is disclosed in the body. Add the selection sentence and abstract flag. The full sample performance is descriptive; there is no independent confirmatory test of the highlighted score.
4. **'Is the single-report result an artifact of duplicate texts assigned conflicting labels?'** The new nonshared-text check directly answers this for the two tested learners and the predefined equivalence criterion. This is a material methodological strengthening.
5. **'Do the trained models add anything if rare overlap is enough?'** They add less for Haiku and Luna in the binary task and more for Gemini. The three-way and configuration tasks remain distinct, but the manuscript does not compare a corresponding simple rule for them. It should not claim training is necessary for those tasks without an appropriate comparator.
6. **'Could shared assigned facts rather than shared parent text explain the score?'** The full-access condition fixes the event record across all roots, which is a strong response to that specific confound. The manipulation still bundles the shared text with one versus three observer calls. The manuscript states both facts. The partial-access arm is only a contrast boundary.
7. **'Can these findings be independently evaluated or reproduced?'** Methods and logs describe strong internal provenance, but the current reproducibility statement makes no release commitment. TACL strongly encourages release and asks authors to disclose availability (TACL, n.d.). That is a plausible weakness under a methodological-contribution framing. Do not create a public-release promise without approval. A permissible alternative is a confidential reviewer-access or sanitized artifact plan if feasible and allowed by journal procedures, subject to the journal's restrictions on supplemental links.

## Recommended changes before a TACL submission

These are **substantive precision edits**, not another general prose pass:

1. Add 'post hoc' in the abstract immediately before the rare-overlap and nonshared-single-text findings. Qualify single-report equivalence as applying to the two tested learners and the specified 90% AUC band.
2. State explicitly in §4.1 that nine simple statistics were assessed and the highlighted maximum rare-sequence score was selected after inspection. Replace 'untrained' where necessary with 'not fitted to structure labels, with rarity defined on fold-specific training text'.
3. Remove statistical overtones from the 0.985 > 0.982 and 0.984 > 0.973 contrasts. Report them as observed AUCs and say that paired differences were not estimated. Scope 'trained models matter mainly...' to the **binary** task.
4. In the Discussion, separate the equality of single-report marginals **by construction** from equivalence of **these two learners on post hoc nonshared texts**. Do not translate that into a no-single-text-information theorem.
5. Decide whether the editorial thesis is **a matched-marginal measurement of one-step paraphrase ancestry** or **a more general evaluation problem**. The current manuscript supports the former. If the broader contribution is required for TACL, more independent conditions, such as deeper shared ancestry or different genres, would be scientifically more informative than polishing prose. No extra model calls are necessary for the narrower claim.
6. Do a short final PDF check. The main text does end on page 8, with references beginning immediately afterward. Appendix B's simple-score table and the other supplementary analyses remain present in the same file. The visual format looks consistent with the TACL template. Final release and anonymization compliance are separate checks, not covered by this review.

## Bottom line

The new checks **improve inferential validity and reduce methodological novelty simultaneously**. They close the specific duplicate-text objection and show that a conventional similarity statistic captures most of the separability of one-step shared-parent paraphrases. They do not invalidate the primary AUC estimates. The manuscript's strongest claim is now an empirically quantified, matched-marginal boundary on what can be inferred from several final reports, rather than an algorithmic advance.

I would **keep TACL as a defensible first attempt**, make the four high-yield reporting corrections above, and submit without another expansive polish cycle. I would not claim the paper plainly clears its significance threshold. If the objective instead is to maximize acceptance probability and minimize review delay, the existing Scientific Reports fallback is a rational immediate switch. Its soundness-centered policy is more congruent with this carefully controlled but scientifically narrow empirical result.
