# Independent review of the first full TACL draft

**Date:** November 2, 2026  
**Manuscript reviewed:** `tacl/main.pdf`, compiled November 2, 2026, with `tacl/main.tex` as the line-level source.  
**Other materials consulted:** `redesign/TACL_Claim_Ledger_Outline_2026-11-01.md`, `redesign/Claude_Citation_Verification_2026-11-02.md`, and the project Writing Canon.  
**Review position:** An independent, technically informed TACL reader. This is an assessment of the argument presented to a reviewer, not an attempt to preserve the authors' intended interpretation. I inspected the rendered 10-page PDF, including the figures, tables, bibliography, and appendices. I did not independently rerun models or reverify every primary citation. The citation-verification file is an audit record, not independent confirmation by this reviewer.

## Assessment and decision

**Major revision before submission.** The principal empirical result is intelligible and comparatively well delimited: on world-held-out, full-access, same-producer report sets, explicitly relational features improve IND-versus-DEP AUC from 0.699 to 0.908, a gain of 0.209 with a refitted interval of 0.156 to 0.287. The methods separate this primary estimate from exploratory extensions, use world-grouped splits, and disclose the conditioned nature of most intervals. The draft also handles the cross-producer transfer asymmetry rather than selecting only favorable directions. Those features make an evaluation of the claim possible.

The remaining problems concern **what the estimand establishes**, **what “independent” denotes**, and **where a measured difference is converted into a causal or mechanistic statement**. The draft would be considerably more defensible if it presented itself as identifying *experimentally assigned sharing of an upstream text among separately generated endpoints*, rather than as directly adjudicating independent factual corroboration. The main AUC gain is an incremental improvement over a **specified low-dimensional pooled representation**, not proof of information that cannot be extracted by any model using the three reports as a set. These are not cosmetic adjustments to terminology. They define the target construct and the comparative claim.

## Major findings in priority order

### 1. “Independent sources” is not independent factual evidence (high priority)

**Location:** Abstract, PDF p. 1; Introduction, pp. 1–2; Design §3.2, p. 3; Discussion, p. 7. Source `main.tex` lines 20, 25, 29, 84, 184–188.

The motivating contrast between corroborating independent accounts and copied accounts is understandable. Yet all full-access observers are generated from the **same 20-fact record**, with its same named sources and disputes. The IND condition contains three independent *observer-model calls or parent texts*, not three independently acquired bodies of event evidence. Three reports generated separately from one event dossier are not three independent confirmations that the event facts are correct. A reviewer may reasonably interpret “independent sources,” “independent observations,” and “corroboration” as asserting the latter.

The distinction is useful to the identification design. By assigning everyone the same record, the study holds factual inputs constant while varying whether terminal reports share an **intermediate drafted report**. Make this explicit in the first paragraph or two, then use “independently generated parent reports” or “distinct upstream report texts” where independence refers to the generation lineage. The abbreviated IND label can remain. In the Discussion, the potential application is diagnosing shared *report production*, not measuring independence of underlying factual corroboration. The latter would require a different study with separately obtained evidence.

This correction does not undermine the P3 contrast. It prevents the motivating construct from exceeding that contrast.

### 2. The pooled comparator does not identify a general information-theoretic boundary (high priority)

**Location:** Abstract and Introduction, pp. 1–2; §3.3, p. 3; §4.1, pp. 4–5; Discussion, p. 7. `main.tex` lines 20, 29, 31, 92, 149–151, 184.

The abstract says marginal matching “places the information about shared ancestry in the relations among reports.” The Introduction says “any recoverable difference must lie in the relations among reports,” then says the relational gain measures what the third information tier adds to the second. The first proposition, if “relations” denotes dependence in the **joint distribution**, is defensible under the matched-marginal design. The stronger reading, that explicit pairwise features recover information unavailable to a model that collectively sees all three texts, is not established by the proposed learner comparison.

The pooled model uses the mean and maximum of 12 handcrafted individual-report features. The relational model adds rare n-gram overlap, embedding similarity, and sentence-order alignment. Those relations are deterministic functions of the **same three observed texts**. A sufficiently flexible permutation-invariant learner over the raw texts could in principle compute them. Even within hand-engineered features, pooled means and maxima discard distributional and lexical information. AUC improvement therefore estimates the incremental predictive value of the **specified relational feature families over the specified pooled summary with a specified logistic learner**. It does not identify a universal ceiling on pooled or set-based modeling, nor a unique quantity of ancestry information intrinsic to a separate “tier.”

This is probably the hardest TACL objection because it concerns model comparison rather than merely prose. The present paper answers it adequately **only if the claim is narrowed**. Define the estimand formally as a performance difference between two feature maps, with identical held-out rows and fitting protocol. Keep the matched-marginal design result separate from the statistical comparison. The paper should not imply that this selected pooled model exhausts nonrelational evidence. A richer Deep Sets or permutation-invariant text baseline might be requested by a reviewer. The computational work can remain closed if the manuscript accepts the narrower feature-comparison claim. A general representational claim would require additional evidence.

The above-chance pooled AUC of 0.699 is not a defect. It illustrates how equal one-report marginals coexist with structure-dependent joint variation. That point should be stated before the contribution is summarized, rather than seeming to qualify an overly categorical initial statement.

### 3. Structure-level causal attribution is defensible with a precisely defined intervention (high priority)

**Location:** Introduction, p. 2, “Because the structures are experimentally assigned with everything else matched, a difference between them is attributable to shared ancestry”; Discussion, p. 7. `main.tex` lines 31, 184–186.

I accept the intentional departure from the earlier ledger **in its narrow form**. The experiment intervenes on whether endpoint rewrites draw on one or several upstream report texts while fixing the event record, producer, role instructions, and nominal terminal rewrite depth. Differences in the joint distribution of terminal outputs can therefore be attributed to the **assigned lineage construction** within that system. “Experimentally assigned” is accurate. The paper need not say “randomized” if the structure allocation was not a randomized trial.

“Everything else matched,” however, hides a constitutive difference: IND generates three upstream observer texts, whereas DEP generates one upstream text that is reused. That change is precisely what shared ancestry entails in this implementation, but the study does not separate intermediate-text sharing from the resulting distribution of upstream lexical choices or the number of realized observer calls. The causal object is a **bundled structural intervention**, not an independently estimated causal effect of a unique linguistic mechanism. Recast the sentence to identify the assigned transformation and its outcome, for example that changing the sharing of upstream report texts changes the joint distribution of the endpoints. The ensuing feature ablations do not mediate that effect.

The causal language is thus defensible in §1 if locally bounded. It becomes less defensible in §5, where “the relational models succeed by detecting the excess similarity that one shared rewrite step adds” states a particular operational mechanism. In the DEP and Y motifs, **terminal rewrites are separate calls**. What they share is the upstream observation report, not a shared rewrite step. Replace that inaccurate description with the shared parent text. Also avoid implying that the ablations identify the sole channel of transmission.

### 4. §4.4 crosses its own confounding boundary (high priority)

**Location:** “Boundaries of the set result,” PDF p. 6; continuation of implication on p. 7. `main.tex` lines 175 and 188.

The paragraph correctly says partial access “confounds shared ancestry with shared assigned content by construction.” It then concludes that the arm shows “common information, when present, is a stronger signal than common ancestry.” That causal comparison is **not identified**. The high partial-access AUC estimates a combined change in the distribution of assigned facts and report ancestry. It does not estimate the relative contribution of shared assigned facts against inheritance. The greater AUC could reflect changed task difficulty, the altered fact packages, their interaction with paraphrasing, or other distributional shifts. The accurate observation is that the combined partial-access contrast is **more readily discriminated** than the matched full-access contrast.

Similarly, removing the visible `Sources:` line after generation while preserving much of the classification performance shows that **the line is not necessary for the observed discrimination**. It does not show that the readers “were not relying on attribution lines” to any degree. The concealment instruction alters generation behavior, not just a final field, and descriptive improvements over the neutral arm do not identify a negative effect of concealment. State those results as robustness observations, not mechanism-specific exclusions. These edits are text-only and should be made before circulation.

### 5. Ablations establish feature dependence, not the source or type of the inherited signal (high priority)

**Location:** §4.3, pp. 6–7; Discussion, p. 7. `main.tex` lines 170 and 186.

The opening sentence of §4.3 is well calibrated: the feature families correlate, so leave-one-family-out changes measure how much other features fail to replace. The paragraph then asserts that shared ancestry is recoverable “without rare shared wording, through sentence order and similarity of meaning.” Removing the **explicit rare-n-gram feature** does not remove rare wording from the underlying reports, from sentence alignments, or from embeddings. A positive residual gain is evidence that the remaining engineered feature map retains predictive ability. It is not an identification of semantic inheritance as a distinct causal channel. Likewise, the sentence-order feature uses embeddings, which the paragraph correctly acknowledges.

Use language such as “the residual relational feature families retain predictive value after explicit rare-sequence overlap is removed.” The sentence about “excess similarity” in §5 should be tied to the observed feature contrasts, not presented as a demonstrated transmission mechanism. The example sentences in Figure 2 are illustrative and cannot establish which feature family accounts for aggregate performance.

### 6. Single-report conclusions sometimes convert weak discrimination or nondetection into knowledge (moderate to high priority)

**Location:** Abstract and Introduction, pp. 1–2; §4.5, pp. 6–7; Discussion, p. 7. `main.tex` lines 20, 33, 178–184, 195.

The study distinguishes design matching from an empirical equivalence decision and reports the inconclusive single-report diagnostic, including the prompt-family stratum. Keep that. Elsewhere, “a single report reveals how many relays it passed through” overstates macro AUC 0.599 on the tested discrete depths. This is **weak ranking discrimination**, not accurate inference of an arbitrary rewrite count. The Discussion’s statement that a single-output analyst can say little “beyond how many times it was rewritten” amplifies the error. “A five-relay chain cannot be told from a diamond followed by three relays” is a claim of nondetectability that does not follow from AUC 0.489 with the reported interval. Say the tested learner **did not establish discrimination** in that contrast.

P1 is properly represented in Results as a primary dead-end control rather than relegated to an implementation check. Yet “the negative controls behave as the design requires” overgeneralizes across feature and embedding learners. The D0/NC0 embedding result is inconclusive under the equivalence rule, and the underlying refitted permutation p-value is 0.015, despite the fresh-seed result returning to equivalence. Because the positive P2 p-value is printed while this anomalous P1 p-value is not, an attentive reviewer may ask whether this is selective reporting. State the two different decisions, equivalence and the chance-null permutation, rather than suppressing one. Replication does not diagnose the first result’s cause.

### 7. First-two-pages identification: conceptually visible, operationally deferred (moderate priority)

**Location:** First two PDF pages, with Figure 1 and feature-tier definitions appearing on p. 3.

A fluent NLP reviewer can identify the intended contrast by the end of the first Introduction page: all terminal reports have one rewrite, the same full-record factual inputs and nominal role templates, and only upstream-parent sharing varies. The three information tiers are named on p. 1. This is the first version of the argument in which the source-sharing contrast can be reconstructed without the project dossier.

However, the crucial representation distinction does not become concrete until §3.3 on p. 3. Meanwhile p. 2 is heavily occupied by Related Work, and the design graph is also delayed to p. 3. A reader can encounter “the main estimate is what the third tier adds to the second” without knowing that tier two is **mean/max of 12 features**, or that the feature families added at tier three include embedding and n-gram comparisons. Give the pooled-baseline definition in one sentence in the Introduction and, if layout permits, move the design figure earlier. Replace the autobiographical “An earlier version of this problem...” with the general confound it describes. The prior rejected design is not an evidentiary premise and requires no manuscript biography.

The first sentence of the abstract, “a reader ... cannot tell which,” also reads as literal non-identifiability, which contradicts the result. Say the history is not **directly observed**. The abstract’s “same distribution” should specify **each terminal report’s marginal distribution under the matched design**, not equality of the three-report joint distributions.

## Additional reviewer pressure and whether the draft answers it

**Why is this more than detecting text similarity?** Partly answered. The full-access common-facts comparison and inclusion of a pooled baseline are the response. The baseline limitations in finding 2 must be disclosed, and “independent source” must be defined as lineage, not fact acquisition. The related work accurately credits text reuse and stemmatics.

**Could language-model source or prompt leakage generate the signal?** Partly answered. Producer-specific fits, matched terminal roles and templates, excluded source lines for single-message features, world-grouped folds, and the source-stripping reader analysis help. However, the manuscript should specify whether all pairwise lexical and sentence-similarity features also operate on the body with the `Sources:` line excluded. It currently says this unambiguously for single-report features but not for the relational features. That implementation detail is material to provenance claims. It should also name the embedding model and version and specify how sentence matches are obtained, rather than only saying “general-purpose text-embedding model.”

**Is there genuine held-out generalization rather than memorization of event facts or rare sequences?** Substantially answered by world-grouped outer folds and world-excluded rarity construction. The refitted bootstrap's fixed rarity reference is disclosed. A reviewer might request a schematic or pseudo-code showing when rarity tables are constructed, especially because the corrected rarity fit is a consequential methodological change. The present compact description may suffice, but the exact reference units and source-line handling should be unambiguous in Appendix A.

**Do outputs generalize across producers or only within producer?** Answered to the scope tested. All six ordered cross-producer pairs are reported, with conditional world-bootstrap intervals and the same test-world comparison to within-producer fitting. The text should retain direction-specific losses. “Loses nothing detectable” for Haiku↔Luna means failure to detect a paired loss, not equivalence or absence of one. It is acceptable only with that interpretation. The present main text reports the worst direction, which is appropriate.

**Are pair identification and exact structure recovery conflated?** Mostly answered. The text distinguishes known-Y odd-member accuracy (88.3%) from unconditional five-configuration accuracy (64.1%) and explicitly displays the 66/180 exact-Y count. The 28 Y cases demoted when the Y probability is divided among positions reveal that the five-way construction is a composite decision rule, not a separately calibrated five-class learner. A TACL reviewer may ask how the probabilities of the three-class model and pair model are combined and calibrated. Give the exact rule in Appendix A or §4.2. The current prose gives only a verbal approximation.

**What does the prompted-reader result add?** Its main-text placement is defensible as a distinct inference procedure, not independent confirmation of a shared causal mechanism. The paper correctly says readers received 18 examples, saw randomized input order, and were evaluated on the first replicate only. It should specify how examples were chosen, that they came from disjoint training worlds, whether the same example sets were used across reader models, and the role of source lines. Do not juxtapose their AUC with the classifier's as though supervision, evaluation populations, and ranking tasks were controlled head-to-head.

**Is the statistical inference confirmatory?** Partly answered. The P3 refitted bootstrap, P2 refitted permutation, Holm family, and exploratory status are disclosed. The phrasing “only the cross-producer transfer and single-report replication were specified in advance” is appropriate. Clarify that the transfer plan preceded **fitting of that transfer analysis**, not the original experiment, and do not call any of the study preregistered.

**Does the six-order invariance result cover the final rarity implementation?** Not fully established by the supplied audit trail. The earlier claim ledger says the six-order diagnostic was performed under an earlier rarity fit, while the principal numbers use the corrected world-excluded rarity construction. The structural symmetry of minimum/median/maximum pair summaries makes invariance very plausible, but §4.2's claim that all models were **refitted under all six orders with identical predictions** needs a version-specific audit statement. If the same test was not rerun on the final feature construction, describe it accurately as a prior diagnostic plus an implementation symmetry property. The unkeyed “below 10^-15” in the source should be tied to the actual verified check, rather than presented as a new canonical estimate.

## The three intentional departures from the ledger

**Structure-level causality:** Accept with the intervention and scope repair in finding 3. The label “causal” attaches to manipulating the parent-sharing construction, not to n-grams, sentence order, semantic similarity, omission inheritance, or a general communication-network topology.

**Prompted readers in the main text:** Accept, provided their different supervision and test population remain explicit and the source-line and example-selection details are documented. Their result makes clear that the distinction is not wholly specific to the handcrafted-feature logistic model. It does not establish comparable performance under matched inference budgets.

**“Experimentally assigned” instead of “randomized”:** Accept and retain. The former accurately describes implemented experimental conditions without asserting a randomization mechanism that the manuscript does not document. Avoid weaker substitutes that unnecessarily erase the designed intervention.

## Writing Canon and exposition

The opening begins with a concrete evidentiary situation, defines the operative comparison, and credits adjacent literatures without asserting first discovery. The prose is predominantly direct, with little ornamental throat-clearing. The claim discipline is uneven at a small number of consequential verbs: “cannot tell,” “reveals how many,” “cannot be told,” “were not relying,” and “is a stronger signal than” convert missing observability, moderate discrimination, non-detection, or a compound treatment into categorical inferences. These should be corrected on evidentiary grounds, not merely softened with generic “may” language.

The Introduction's final paragraph (“The results establish four things...”) repeats content already carried by the abstract and preceding Introduction paragraph. In a short TACL paper, remove or compress it in favor of the one precise contribution definition. The Discussion's opening (“The asymmetry is the main result”) similarly names the result rather than adding an inference, and the following sentence turns a classifier's discrimination into a broad analyst capability. Retain the causal and observation conditions, delete the slogan-like restatement, and use the space to delimit what analysts actually observe and what their model is trained to predict.

The Related Work section is compressed enough that distinctions risk becoming too categorical. Historical text reuse and computational stemmatology are not limited to literal near-copies, and some genealogical methods explicitly address paraphrase, contamination, and uncertainty. Replace “The texts in those settings are copies or near-copies of one another” with a more particular distinction in the observation model and baseline: here independent *root drafts* condition on the same record and upstream intermediate texts are withheld. This avoids manufacturing a false contrast against the cited literature.

Small but material edits include specifying that “shared rewrite step” in §5 means “shared upstream observation report,” avoiding a general “code ... will be released” commitment until the responsible author approves the release, and checking the source note that the six-order claim corresponds to the canonical rarity version. I did not find a formatting defect that competes with these scientific issues. The 10-page PDF has the article, references, and in-PDF appendix. Figures and tables are legible, although the full transfer table occupies most of the last appendix page and the font is dense.

## Minimal revision sequence before another independent review

1. **Repair the target construct:** define IND as independent upstream text generation conditional on the same event record, not independent factual evidence. Correct the corroboration analogy and “independent sources” references throughout.
2. **Narrow the core estimator:** make the matched-marginal design a statement about one-report distributions and the P3 gain a comparison of two explicitly specified feature representations. Do not claim an information-theoretic impossibility for pooled set models.
3. **Retain only warranted causality:** attribute joint-output changes to the assigned sharing of upstream report text. Remove unproved mechanistic decomposition, especially §4.3, §4.4, and the shared-rewrite-step sentence in §5.
4. **Repair boundary language:** partial access estimates a combined difference, source-line stripping shows non-necessity rather than nonuse, and negative AUC results do not prove indistinguishability. Include the anomalous P1 embedding permutation result alongside the equivalence interpretation.
5. **Resolve methodological specifics:** identify the embedding model, source-line treatment of pairwise features, five-configuration probability combination, prompted reader example selection, and version of the six-order audit. These are the strongest requests for appendix detail.
6. **Compress repetition:** remove the Introduction's recap and the Discussion's broad generalization, making room for the construct and feature-comparison qualifications without weakening the empirical results.

**Bottom line:** The paper supports a **controlled, model-specific predictive claim about shared intermediate-text ancestry**. The main empirical contrast and its uncertainty are already present. The current draft occasionally expands that into independent corroboration, a universal relational-information distinction, and identified linguistic mechanisms. Those expansions are not required by the findings and create avoidable vulnerabilities at TACL. I would request revision rather than accept the present framing.
