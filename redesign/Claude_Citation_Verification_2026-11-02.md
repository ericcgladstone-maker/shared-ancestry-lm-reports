## Closest prior work: the two 2026 preprints

### Nian et al. (2026), Implicit Execution Tracing (arXiv 2603.17445)

Verified with corrections.

- **Title and authors depend on the version.**
  - Versions 1 to 4 are titled "When Only the Final Text Survives: Implicit Execution Tracing for Multi-Agent Attribution", by six authors.
  - The current version 5 (1 August 2026) uses "Auditing" in place of "Attribution" and adds Yudi Zhang between Qingqing Luan and Yue Zhao.
  - Cite version 5 with all seven authors: arXiv:2603.17445v5.
- **Peer review is not established.**
  - The version 3 PDF header reads "Published as a conference paper at COLM 2026", but version 4 reads "Preprint" and version 5 has no venue line.
  - OpenReview lists it only as CoRR, and no COLM 2026 record was found.
  - Treat it as a preprint.
- **What it requires.**
  - It embeds "agent-specific, key-conditioned statistical signals into the token generation process at generation time", which requires cooperating, instrumented agents.
  - At audit time it needs a registry that maps each agent to its key and tokenizer.
  - It attributes segments within one final text, not relations among several separate outputs.
- **What it claims.** Version 1 claimed interaction-graph reconstruction. The version 5 abstract narrows this to segment attribution and "reliable transition recovery". Describe it in version 5's terms.

### Tang, Barke & Agarwal (2026), Tokengeist (arXiv 2607.22610)

Verified with corrections.

- **Citation details.** Title and authors are correct. Only version 1 exists. It appears as a NeurIPS 2026 workshop poster and as ACL Rolling Review submissions, with no archival version.
- **What it requires.**
  - It works on the full completed transcript ("post-hoc over completed transcripts").
  - It does not need the generator, but its attribution backends need "access to model internals (attention weights or hidden states)" of an open-weight model.
- **Corrections.**
  - "Provenance over an agentic interaction graph" overstates it. The graph is over the turns of single-agent tool-use dialogues, and the paper says its data "does not cover ... multi-agent transcripts".
  - The benchmark is announced but not released.
  - The contrast the file draws, that upstream turns are accessible to the method, is correct and is the key distinction from our setting.

## Additional works found and verified in this pass

- **Broder, A. Z. (1997). On the resemblance and containment of documents.** Compression and Complexity of SEQUENCES 1997, 21–29. https://doi.org/10.1109/SEQUEN.1997.666900. This is the shingling estimate of document resemblance.
- **Potthast, M., Stein, B., Barrón-Cedeño, A., & Rosso, P. (2010). An evaluation framework for plagiarism detection.** Coling 2010: Posters, 997–1005. https://aclanthology.org/C10-2115/. This is the PAN plagiarism-detection evaluation framework. If GPT's "Barrón-Cedeño et al. (2010)" refers to a different paper, that paper is still unverified.
- **Barbrook, A. C., Howe, C. J., Blake, N., & Robinson, P. (1998). The phylogeny of The Canterbury Tales.** Nature, 394, 839. https://doi.org/10.1038/29667. A phylogenetic tree of 58 manuscripts.
- **Roos, T., & Heikkilä, T. (2009). Evaluating methods for computer-assisted stemmatology using artificial benchmark data sets.** Literary and Linguistic Computing, 24(4), 417–433. https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqp002. It evaluates against artificial traditions with known genealogy, which is the closest methodological analogue to this study. Only one abstract sentence was verified verbatim.
