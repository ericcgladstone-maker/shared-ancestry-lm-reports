# Recommendation to the editor: major revision, assuming predicted results

The redesigned study directly responds to the rejection’s most serious identification failure. It restricts the target to interventions on a declared language-model message pipeline, matches producer functions and prompt pools across many comparisons, introduces non-ancestral negative controls, and treats the world rather than an individual generated message as the unit for generalization. Those changes are substantial. They are not sufficient for acceptance as a methodological contribution until the claims, primary inferential targets, mechanism measures, and statistical testing plan are more tightly specified. In particular, the design is best understood as testing recoverability of specific ancestral procedures in synthetic production systems, not graph structure in general. Its most distinctive claim, that several texts reveal shared ancestry through inherited deviations, currently depends on an unusually fragile proposition-matcher validation plan. I would invite major revision before data collection rather than reject the research program outright.

# I. Response to each concern in the rejection review

The statuses in this section assess whether the redesigned \*protocol\* would persuade the original reviewer. They do not certify new results, because none exists. References to sections are to the v5 design unless otherwise indicated.

## R1. Topology versus role, protocol, transformations, and genre: answers in part

Parts II–IV distinguish effects of non-ancestral changes, rewrite depth, partition-and-synthesis procedures, independent roots at fixed assigned input, and access-plus-ancestry total effects. Shared functions, matched prompt families, and fixed assigned packages eliminate several earlier alternative paths. The author also expressly concedes that F1–F3 does not isolate fan-in from root multiplicity, and SI-div/SD-div deliberately bundles access with ancestry. Thus the reviewer’s request for interpretively valid contrasts is substantially met, but the general claim that graph topology alone causes recoverable residue is not established. The reviewer should be persuaded about the narrower procedural estimands, not a topology-invariant graph signature.

## R2. Independent corroboration despite unused branches: answers for the new design

The original reviewer singled out 96.4% motif recovery where two of three independent branches could not causally reach the observed terminal. Part IV’s NC0/D0 and NCR1/R1 negative controls now intervene on unused branches while holding the terminal ancestry and call inputs fixed, and Part V’s H1 states a testable no-difference expectation. Part I recognizes the old result as protocol prediction rather than evidence of invisible sibling ancestry. This is a direct response. It cannot make the original positive result a valid ancestry finding, and passing a reproducibility check is not, by itself, a discovery about communication topology.

## R3. Convergent-discriminant validation using alternative instructions and matched structures: answers in part

Part III’s A/B prompt-family transfer and same-function pools, together with Part V H6 and the second producer, test sensitivity to at least one instruction-family and generating-model change. The evidence is conditional on the selected variants and producers. Only R1/R3 and SI-same/SD-same are crossed with the second genre, and Study 2 lacks a specified cross-producer replication. A reviewer seeking robustness across independent generating environments will still regard the validation as limited.

## R4. Prompted reader versus supervised classifier conflates training, architecture, and access: answers in part

Part IV restricts the method comparison to message-text access and gives the prompted reader labeled training-world examples in context. That repairs the clearest information-access imbalance in the submitted manuscript, which compared a trace-informed reader with a supervised surface-form model. The difference between a fitted supervised model and in-context demonstrations, as well as representation and parameter budget, remains. A performance difference may be reported descriptively but not attributed uniquely to residue-method alignment or algorithmic superiority.

## R5. Production-mechanism narratives inferred from accuracy alone: answers in part

Parts II and IV now specify realized omissions, conflict resolution, input similarity, and downstream transformation as candidate mediating pathways. Part V H5 distinguishes declining discriminability from direct evidence that inherited deviations disappear. However, reporting correlations between intermediate features and successful classification does not by itself identify mediation. Mechanism language should remain conditional unless an intervention or properly defined causal decomposition distinguishes those pathways.

## R6. Undefined semantic, formal, and trace residue and ambiguous entity/source markers: answers in part

Part VI labels feature families as operational groupings rather than pure latent constructs, acknowledges that named entities carry content as well as form, and proposes feature-family ablations. Part XIII explicitly removes the metadata/trace-observability construct from the new paper. This is more coherent, but “residue” remains broadly defined as any artifact property varying by production graph, and no unique construct-validity link is shown for the formal or relational indicators.

## R7. Length-only baseline and incremental feature contribution: answers in part

The original 14 features are retained as a baseline, with formal, attributional, propositional, and relational group ablations and flexible classifiers as leakage stress tests (Part VI). These moves address the length-only objection and go beyond a single fixed feature list. V5 does not yet identify a primary family contrast or a bounded sensitivity set of reasonable alternative features, so a favorable ablation could still be one of many correlated comparisons.

## R8. Accuracy, label entropy, fidelity confidence, source posterior, and probability calibration serve different claims: answers in part

Part VIII includes proper scores (multiclass Brier score and log loss), while Parts V and XIII narrow the outcomes to structural predictions. The origin posterior and fidelity-confidence task are explicitly dropped, with the original manuscript’s claim about fidelity confidence withdrawn. That is defensible for a replacement study, not a comprehensive validation of every earlier diagnostic. The scoring target for each prompted reader task still needs a fully specified label space, input representation, and invalid-output policy.

## R9. Four test worlds, pseudoreplication, and trial-level intervals: substantially answers

Part VII supplies 60 confirmatory worlds with three replicates each, and Part VIII specifies world-grouped evaluation, nested selection, world-clustered intervals, and per-world/per-condition summaries. These respond directly to the reviewer’s principal statistical objection. The selection law for authored worlds and the exact clustered bootstrap/retraining estimand nevertheless need specification for valid generalization claims.

## R10. Uneven motif results and length-subgroup swings without counts or interaction inference: answers in part

The new paper no longer tests the eight original motifs as fixed labels, and Part VIII requires subgroup counts and intervals. Part VI retains length and formal measurements. But the analysis plan does not prespecify which interactions with message length or other covariates are confirmatory and which are descriptive, so the old reviewer’s concern about interpreting heterogeneous performance remains applicable to new subgroups.

## R11. Cross-family robustness based on 89 observations: answers in part

H6 tests A/B prompt-family transfer and a second producer on the new 60-world design, with same-test-world comparisons. That is substantially stronger than the submitted 89-trial slice. Still, the reader’s own cross-family robustness and Study 2’s shared-ancestry recovery across producer families are not specified as parallel confirmation. The conclusion must remain bounded by model, task, and genre.

## R12. External validity and substantive applicability: answers in part

Part X explicitly confines claims to tested local language-model pipelines, and Part XI treats a documentary field anchor as optional and permission-dependent. This is properly cautious. Yet the journal’s substantive-interest question remains: a positive result about deterministic routing among local model calls does not show that similar signals distinguish independent human reporting, institutional copying, or natural information cascades. The original reviewer could reasonably see a stronger computational benchmark but not yet a general complexity-science finding.

# II. Ranked, numbered prospective objections

Severity meanings: fatal means that the promised central inference would not be supported without remedy, major means that a skeptical referee could withhold acceptance even if the reported statistical results are favorable, and minor means a correction required for replicability, calibration, or presentation but unlikely by itself to determine disposition. These rankings assume predicted results unless otherwise stated.

## 1\. MAJOR. No single confirmatory estimand or decision hierarchy governs eleven hypotheses

Part: Parts V, VI, and VIII. H1–H11 encompass equivalence, above-chance prediction, structural contrasts, differences in discrimination after rewriting, two transfer domains, relational classification, access interactions, stripping effects, and a world-free-to-anchored performance ratio. Each entails multiple outcome scales, model classes, conditions, and strata. The protocol describes nested tuning but no ordered primary question, primary contrast, primary statistic, or familywise decision hierarchy. Favorable fragments from a large result space could appear decisive without strong joint evidence. Smallest change: designate one primary Study 1 positive contrast (preferably SI-same/SD-same), one primary Study 2 relational contrast (full-access neutral IND/DEP, with MIX secondary), and one negative-control criterion. Specify their label spaces, statistic, effect threshold, interval, and whether multiplicity is controlled by a testing hierarchy or by explicit exploratory labeling. Keep the remaining contrasts as prespecified secondary estimates without implying that all eleven are independent confirmatory discoveries.

## 2\. MAJOR. Proposition-matcher validation as specified is likely unable to validate the mechanism

Part: Part VI, with consequences for Parts V H4, H8, H10, and H11. The validation sample is approximately 170 messages across seventeen Study 1 conditions, two producers, Study 2, and stripped pairs. A condition receives only six primary-producer and two second-producer Study 1 messages. Yet balanced accuracy of at least 0.85 is required for each of four judgments in every condition, and each class with fewer than twenty annotated proposition-by-message examples is unvalidated. With approximately twenty propositions per world and high preservation in the earlier experiment, some classes such as alteration or rare conflict resolutions may occur fewer than twenty times in a condition. No sample-size or prevalence demonstration establishes that the declared threshold is even attainable. The predeclared fallback would then withhold the anchored and world-free methods and claims about inherited omissions and errors, eliminating an important mechanism test from the successful-results paper. Smallest change: before data collection, provide a label-count feasibility calculation and a class-enriched, blinded validation sample or an independently generated, prespecified error-challenge set large enough to include at least twenty instances of each judgment class within the level at which validation is claimed. Define scorer performance separately for uncommon positive events. Retain one locked fallback, but do not let a predictably undersampled validation gate nullify the principal mechanism analysis.

## 3\. MAJOR. The proposed construct “production structure” remains broader than the identified treatments

Part: Parts II–IV, H2–H4, and X. R1/R3/R5 vary rewrite depth and cumulative generation noise. F1/F2/F3 jointly vary independent root count, fan-in, and assignment of evidence. SI-div/SD-div varies independent sourcing together with whether a single producer receives the union of evidence. These are well-defined interventions in a generative pipeline, but not invariant graph-topology effects. Even SI-same/SD-same changes the number of independent source realizations. The proposed contribution risks returning to the rejected submission’s claim if “recovering hidden communication structure” is used as shorthand for arbitrary graph-class identification. Smallest change: state the formal treatment and estimand for each positive claim in a concise preregistered estimand table, separate general ancestral properties from bundles of instructions and information allocation, and reserve “structure” for the actual ancestral and shared-root relations. This narrows inference without discarding experiments.

## 4\. MAJOR. Incremental relational prediction is not sufficient proof of inherited deviations

Part: Part IV Study 2, Part V H8, and Part VI. In the full-access arm, randomizing common versus independent roots while holding source information fixed can identify an effect of source sharing on the joint text distribution. Yet an AUC increase when pairwise lexical or embedding features are added to a mean/max pooled baseline is partly a representational fact about the two classifiers. It does not establish that rare shared mistakes or omissions, rather than normal semantic similarity or stylized rephrasing, carried the gain. The independent full-access IND reference is a useful null, but the validation of “excess” shared deviations depends on the matcher and its conditioning. Smallest change: designate a primary shared-deviation statistic with an explicit independent same-world null, compare it with generic lexical/semantic similarity on the same held-out worlds, and report the inherited-error explanation as supported only when the measured deviation channel accounts for observed gain. An alternative pairwise-feature success alone supports relational prediction, not the named mechanism.

## 5\. MAJOR. AUC equivalence margins may be too precise for the fixed number of independent worlds

Part: H1, H7, H10, Parts VII–VIII. H1 and H7 stipulate AUC equivalence to \[0.43, 0.57\] and require a two-sided 90% world-cluster interval entirely inside the band. H7 does this within access × attribution × prompt-family strata, leaving roughly 90 sets per structural class per stratum before dependencies. Even under ordinary independent-observation approximations, the null AUC uncertainty at this scale is near the width of the full tolerance half-band, with clustering potentially widening it. H10 additionally uses a ±0.05 margin on a paired AUC difference. The most likely outcome may be “inconclusive” even when the population effect is negligible, limiting the force of the negative controls and singleton-leakage claim. Smallest change: report a prospective precision analysis using simulated world-level correlation, conditional imbalance, and multiclass scoring, and make the three-way decision labels explicit in the abstract and claims. If there is no power to establish the chosen equivalence at the fixed size, characterize H1/H7 as bounds on detectable leakage rather than as a demonstrated absence. Do not reinterpret non-significance as equivalence.

## 6\. MAJOR. Comparability and inference for prompted readers versus trained classifiers remain incomplete

Part: Part IV method comparisons, Parts V and VIII. Matching message-text access while providing training-world examples in context is an improvement, but supervision intensity, number of examples, fitted parameters, output constraints, and evaluation tasks may remain radically different. The design does not fix the prompted reader’s exact input representation, number and selection of examples, label instructions for Study 1 versus Study 2, or aggregation of multiple reader calls in the same trial. The requested interpretation is descriptive, yet the submitted manuscript’s headline depended heavily on a method gap. Smallest change: preregister separate fully specified reader protocols for single-message and set-level tasks, matched test inputs and labeled example inventories, and a primary within-method accuracy estimand. Treat cross-method differences as comparative prediction performance only, not an explanation based on model family or residue alignment.

## 7\. MAJOR. World generation and sampling define a narrow, underspecified target population

Part: Parts III, VII, and VIII. Seventy new approximately 20-proposition fictional worlds are authored under a schema with two conflict pairs, and the researcher reviews them. The world is correctly used as the inferential cluster, but the distribution from which worlds are sampled is not specified, nor the distribution of proposition difficulty, adversarial similarity, entity density, and conflict plausibility. Bootstrap intervals across 60 authored worlds are conditional on these worlds and do not automatically yield population uncertainty about news events, organizational reports, or arbitrary graph-generating tasks. Smallest change: publish the world-construction generator and acceptance rules, freeze the full world bank and world-level holdouts before outcomes, and report results for the finite authored world distribution rather than natural events. Document any hand edits and their timing.

## 8\. MAJOR. The most consequential transfer claim is not tested on the second study

Part: H6, Parts VII and X. The second producer receives the full Study 1 generation program, but Study 2’s three-structure relational recovery is apparently run only with the primary producer. Prompt-family A/B within Study 2 is helpful, but the inherited-deviation mechanism could be highly sensitive to the primary model’s characteristic error modes and rewriting behavior. The manuscript’s most distinctive claim concerns hidden dependence among multiple artifacts, the part with no explicitly budgeted second-family test. Smallest change: either restrict the relational claim unequivocally to the single producing model or use a fixed, limited full-access IND/DEP/MIX replication under the second producer before claiming generator robustness. This is one targeted robustness extension, not a new topology design.

## 9\. MAJOR. Mechanism pathways are described as mediators without a causal mediation design

Part: Part IV SI/SD, decay, and measurement. Intermediate similarity, length, drift, and conflict-resolution differences arise after root assignment and can be affected by other post-treatment features. They are useful diagnostic measures, but regression adjustment or feature ablation cannot identify natural indirect effects without assumptions about post-treatment confounding, measurement error, and downstream dependence. Smallest change: call these post-treatment process measures and explanatory correlates unless a specific mediation estimand and sequential identification assumptions are prespecified. Report their temporal ordering and associations, while keeping the randomized total treatment effect as the causal quantity.

## 10\. MAJOR. The statistical analysis is not fully executable as preregistered

Part: Parts V, VI, and VIII–IX. The protocol allows either world-grouped nested cross-validation or a final untouched world holdout, as alternatives. It does not specify a definitive outer-fold rule, the primary classifier family/hyperparameter space, features permitted in each hypothesis, the construction of pairwise shared-source labels and pair dependence, bootstrap algorithm, or handling of a training fold that lacks a rare class. The reader invalid-output policy is also to be “prespecified,” rather than stated. These choices can move results materially when world count and class prevalence are modest. Smallest change: attach a frozen machine-readable analysis configuration and run synthetic-data unit tests before any full-run output is available. Choose one outer evaluation design and lock the complete prediction, tuning, and resampling pipeline. A registration of broad intent without this specification does not prevent analyst degrees of freedom.

## 11\. MAJOR. The source-attribution manipulation may alter more than an attribution cue

Part: Part IV Study 2 attribution field and H10. The neutral model generates an attribution line selected from an in-world source inventory, while the concealment prompt asks it both to omit this line and to remove source cues in prose. Thus the generative concealment comparison changes discourse and potentially propositional content, not simply visibility of the same metadata. The design acknowledges a content effect, and the stripped arm is derived from neutral outputs. However, marker absence, false source inventory entries, and content-dependent stripping compliance can differ systematically by structure, and the complete-case secondary analysis is selected after generation. Smallest change: keep the intention-to-generate full-set comparison as primary, report field presence/validity and source-specific errors by structure, and clearly separate the effect of removing the delimited field from the total effect of a concealment instruction. Do not call the generative contrast an isolated attribution effect.

## 12\. MAJOR. The experimental graph vocabulary underrepresents network complexity

Part: Parts II, IV, XII, and X. The experiments involve one to three roots, short rewrite chains, and single synthesis nodes. They exclude circulation, overlapping copying trees, feedback, and heterogeneous node functions over larger networks. The null that non-ancestors do not affect a terminal artifact is an expected property of correctly implemented isolated DAGs, not a novel law of network diffusion. The positive results may therefore establish signatures of specific text-generation workflows without identifying broader graph motifs. Smallest change: frame the intended contribution as a controlled identifiability benchmark for bounded message-production DAGs, describe its limits in graph size and type, and avoid general “hidden network recovery” claims. No modest change to this design can establish generic topology recovery, so the claim must be narrowed rather than inferred.

## 13\. MINOR. Paired call reuse complicates the estimand and effective sample count

Part: Parts III–IV, VIII. Shared canonical keys intentionally cause exact source and rewrite reuse across SI/SD and IND/DEP/MIX. This is an efficient paired design and the manuscript correctly calls for world-level splits. However, some apparent condition replicates are identical messages, and shared synthesis descendants create further correlated observations. If ordinary independent-item bootstraps are used inside folds, intervals will be too narrow. Smallest change: specify resampling at the world level, preserving entire world × block clusters and all descendants of shared keys, and distinguish the number of unique generated messages from the number of labeled trial records.

## 14\. MINOR. Reliability and reproducibility controls are necessary but not scientific findings

Part: H1, Part IV replay, Part IX. Per-call seeds, exact prompts, lineage audits, and strict replay standards are appropriate engineering controls. A difference between a terminal-first and concurrent schedule could reflect Ollama runtime or hardware nondeterminism rather than an informational path in a social graph. Similarly, identical D0/NC0 outputs after enforcing identical keys demonstrate pipeline isolation under tested scheduling, not a broadly estimated probability of zero social influence. Smallest change: separate engineering validation outcomes from social-scientific hypotheses in the eventual paper, and report execution constraints as a boundary condition. A failed control should qualify structural claims but should not be recast as a novel diffusion mechanism.

## 15\. MINOR. Stratum-specific and length interactions need a prespecified evidentiary status

Part: H2–H5, H7–H10, Part VIII. V5 promises per-world, per-condition, and worst-stratum findings but does not define a primary interaction model for how length, assigned access, genre, or attribution change recovery. Selecting large subgroup swings after seeing outputs would repeat the rejected manuscript’s length-stratum overinterpretation. Smallest change: designate a bounded set of secondary interactions with contrasts and cluster-aware intervals, report remaining subgroup displays descriptively, and show denominators before inferring heterogeneous mechanisms.

## 16\. MINOR. Fixed thresholds are operational decisions, not theoretically calibrated effects

Part: H1, H7, H10, H11, Part VI. AUC bands 0.43–0.57, ±0.05 for H10, a 0.25 world-free retention ratio, and 0.85 class-balanced annotation agreement are declared as choices rather than estimated from a pilot, which is transparent. Yet their relation to practical information value is not provided, and binary support labels could exaggerate a continuous gradient. Smallest change: give a short consequence-based rationale for each threshold, report estimates and intervals irrespective of pass/fail, and distinguish a scientifically meaningful minimum effect from the limit of statistical detectability.

## 17\. MINOR. Unvalidated feature families limit the proposed construct decomposition

Part: Part VI, with reference to Reviewer 1’s measurement concern. Formal, attributional, propositional, and relational families overlap by design, and a flexible text classifier can reconstruct the same information from multiple families. Incremental ablation is sensitive to collinearity and model capacity. The results can show predictive importance conditional on a feature specification, not that one component is a separable social construct. Smallest change: present family ablations as robustness and operational attribution analyses, not as evidence of distinct latent residue types, and release the exact feature extraction definitions.

## 18\. MINOR. A field anchor is optional, but the application claim should not overreach

Part: Parts X–XI and XIII. The design prudently rejects direct transfer to human communication without labeled documentary ancestry. The field anchor remains an open choice, with candidate corpora providing different labels and uncertain licensing. The resulting article could therefore be entirely synthetic despite examples drawn from news, organizations, and intelligence analysis. Smallest change: decide before submission whether the paper makes only an in-silico methodological claim. If no suitable field corpus with independently established source relationships is available, remove implied empirical validity for human communications. A weakly matched external corpus is worse than a transparent synthetic-only boundary.

## 19\. MINOR. Protocol correction and original-result replication need distinct labels

Part: Parts I, IX, and XIII. The original paper’s figure and causal discussion attributed the compound failure to masking, whereas the new code diagnosis says the first stage was never transmitted to the second. V5 plans to carry the old figures only as a protocol-confound demonstration without rerunning them. The correction needs explicit provenance and acknowledgement of which reported quantities are numerically reproduced and which conclusions are withdrawn. Smallest change: include a short correction audit in the methods supplement and avoid describing the old compound as a genuine negative example of composition recoverability.

# III. Results that would most seriously undermine the proposed paper

The damaging joint pattern is feasible without violating any stated engineering specification: H1’s terminal-pair replay passes because the dispatcher is isolated, but the H1 and H7 distributional intervals are too wide for practical equivalence; the primary SI-same/SD-same and R1/R3 discriminators are at chance or fail prompt-family/genre transfer; H3 predicts only partition-related differences and not reusable ancestral signatures; H8’s relational classifier improves little over the information-matched set baseline or improves only in partial access where shared packages already explain similarity; H9 is weak or inconsistent; H10 is dominated by field-presence effects; and the anchored H11 method has insufficient signal for the world-free retention ratio. If the proposition matcher fails its prespecified class-balanced validation, the shared-deviation analysis that could interpret a relational gain is withheld as unvalidated. Under that combination the project would establish reproducible code isolation and document why protocol templates had once been mistaken for structural information, but would not demonstrate positive recoverability of hidden ancestry from message content.

That result could still justify a short, carefully framed negative-identifiability or research-design note if the nulls were sufficiently precise to rule out meaningful effects and the full-generation archive were released. With the present equivalence precision and matcher feasibility, however, an inconclusive positive result would be confounded with low information content and noisy measurement. The contribution would be closer to a transparent failed benchmark and correction of an earlier synthetic protocol than to the general substantive finding promised for a complexity journal. It would not support an article claiming recoverability, and a standalone article in a broad journal would be unlikely without a sharper theoretical bound or a demonstration of generality.

# IV. Contribution if predictions hold (three sentences)

If the preregistered positive contrasts replicate across held-out worlds and prompt families while non-ancestral changes remain indistinguishable, the paper would show that selected text-production histories leave predictable information in terminal artifacts under matched local language-model instructions. If full-access sets additionally reveal shared-source deviations beyond independent same-event similarity, it would establish a controlled method for detecting common ancestry from multiple generated reports without privileged access to the production graph. This would interest computational-social-science and machine-learning methods readers as a bounded identifiability benchmark, but a general complexity-science audience would need a stronger connection to heterogeneous networks or a validated real-document case before the result could support a broad account of hidden communication topology.

# V. Recommendation

Major revision, assuming the predicted results are obtained. The highest-priority preregistration repairs are a primary-inference hierarchy (Objection 1), a feasible and genuinely informative matcher-validation design (Objection 2), an exact analysis configuration (Objection 10), and claim language that distinguishes ancestral intervention effects from general graph topology (Objections 3 and 12). The remaining issues concern robustness, calibration, and interpretation. These could be addressed without rebuilding the two-study experiment, but acceptance should depend on what the verified design and full results actually establish rather than on the presence of statistically positive classifiers.
