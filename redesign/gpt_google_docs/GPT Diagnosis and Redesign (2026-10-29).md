# Decision and proposed contribution

The paper should be redesigned around observational identifiability before expanding its model comparison. The existing experiment shows that terminal-message surface features predict labels for eight role-constrained synthetic production regimes, but it does not distinguish a dependence graph from the prompts and transformations through which that graph was implemented. The reason is particularly apparent for independent corroboration: three independent branches are generated, but only one of their outputs is observed. The classification of that output can reveal properties of its own generation and of the prompt family, not, under an isolated pipeline, the presence of two unobserved siblings (Gladstone, 2026, Methods, “Production motifs”).

I recommend a two-level research question. The single-message study asks which properties of a message’s causal production ancestry leave recoverable information under matched node instructions. The message-set study asks which relations among several observed messages support inference about shared ancestry and independent sources. These are distinct observation units and estimands. Neither a graph-label benchmark nor a strong classification score alone demonstrates the recovery of the desired structural property.

The most defensible intended contribution is a controlled account of where structural information can and cannot reside in communication artifacts. The negative results have theoretical content if the experiment proves that particular graph interventions leave the distribution of observations unchanged. Positive results have content if graph interventions that alter the causal inputs leave recoverable differences after prompt, genre, and sampling effects are controlled. This can become an identification result within language-model communication pipelines rather than a claim about network measurement in unconstrained field communication.

# 1\. What the submitted study established and what remains unseparated

The submitted design crosses 20 fixed synthetic event worlds with eight named production motifs and experimental cells. It evaluates a prompted reader with message and trace information against a supervised 14-feature classifier applied to terminal-message form. The observed classifier accuracy on 840 held-out observations from four worlds is 61.2%, and the prompted reader’s accuracy is 27.3%. The classifier recovers independent corroboration at 96.4%, common-source laundering at 27.4%, single direct at 39.3%, and compound at 0%. A length-only baseline achieves 50.1% in the original pooled comparison, while the refitted paired baseline in the supplementary analysis achieves 52.1% (Gladstone, 2026, Results; Gladstone, 2026, Supplementary Note 1).

These are accurate descriptions of the tested classifiers and sample. They do not identify why the messages differ. Graph topology, node role, source-access rules, transformation instructions, and reporting conventions were changed jointly with the assigned motif. Training on those motif labels can learn production conventions even if the feature of graph structure that names a motif is causally irrelevant to the observed artifact. The submitted manuscript acknowledges the joint estimand but later attributes the method gap to residue-method alignment and some motif-specific recoveries to dependence structure. Those interpretations require a discriminating experiment (Gladstone, 2026, Introduction and Discussion; Reviewer 1, 2026).

The comparative methods are not information-matched or supervision-matched. The prompted reader has no task-specific labeled training, while the logistic classifier does. The observed accuracy gap is therefore a descriptive difference between complete procedures, not a causal estimate of the fit between residue and method. A reconstruction of the prompt-based reader’s probabilities with multiclass Brier score and log loss would improve reporting but would not repair this identification problem.

The prior code-analysis report by Claude (2026) alleges that the original compound implementation computed laundering outputs without feeding them into the subsequent relay. If confirmed in the source and realized lineage, that would contradict the manuscript’s sequential-composition description and invalidate the interpretation of compound failure as masking. I have not independently examined the original production instantiator or run the full lineage files. The redesigned apparatus should not reuse compound results as evidence of composition until an implementation audit settles this point.

# 2\. Causal boundary of a terminal artifact

## 2.1 The intervention claim

Let W denote the event record, G the production graph, P the node instructions and routing policy, U the relevant stochastic state, and Y the observed terminal artifact. In an isolated acyclic message-production pipeline, changing graph components that are neither direct nor indirect causes of Y cannot change Y when W, P, the realized causal inputs to Y, and U are held fixed. This is a causal noninfluence claim. It does not assert that Y is statistically independent of every non-ancestor variable in observational data. Non-ancestors may include descendants of Y, and common causes or condition-dependent prompts can induce associations.

The experimental null is accordingly intervention-specific: when two configurations generate the same terminal ancestry with the same source inputs, node functions, prompts, selection rule, and stochastic policy, adding unused sibling branches should not alter the terminal-output distribution. There is no comparable universal guarantee that changes to ancestors are detectable. Multiple ancestral graphs can produce the same distribution of terminal messages, especially if rewriting erases input variation.

## 2.2 Operational exceptions

A model-call graph is not necessarily the complete causal graph. Shared model conversations, reuse of random-number generator state, scheduling, batch composition, caches, adaptive terminal selection, prompt builders informed by total graph size, validation filters, or stop conditions can create channels absent from the declared message graph. A graph-conditioned role instruction creates a direct path from the experimental assignment to Y. The redesign must specify the entire generator, including these engineering channels, before treating the causal null as an empirical benchmark.

Each call should be logged with its exact prompt, parent-message hashes, model and quantization identity, decoding settings, independent per-call seed or documented randomization stream, session state, timing, retry status, validation status, and terminal-selection rule. Every trial should carry a realized ancestry DAG reconstructed from message IDs and parent pointers rather than merely an intended motif label. The lineage audit should fail preflight if required parent text does not enter the receiving prompt, if unplanned parents do enter, or if the terminal selected is not the planned terminal. Repair and rerun implementation faults rather than silently excluding the resulting records.

# 3\. Minimum evidence before launching a new large experiment

The redesign should begin with a read-only reconstruction of the original experiment. Verify the complete compound path in the producer dispatcher and a sample of realized prompts from every motif. Audit whether the two unobserved corroboration branches and sibling repetitions can influence the selected terminal by any channel other than the assigned prompt. Report terminal ancestry classes. Compare prompts across labels within each ancestry class. Check whether the published four-world test score, the 14-feature extraction, and the SI confusion matrix reproduce from the public data and the versioned code. The source code and archived data must be version-matched before an implementation finding is called verified.

Existing retained observations support world-by-world and condition-by-condition accuracy, class-conditional confusion, original versus refitted length baselines, feature-family ablations, reader probability scoring, and a test of motif classification conditional on terminal prompt family. Refit in world-grouped folds without using held-out worlds for standardization or calibration. These analyses are descriptive diagnostics of a confounded corpus. No feature ablation can, by itself, separate a graph property from a prompt that co-varied perfectly with it.

The decisive initial production experiment should be small. Use a set of new worlds and stable call manifests to compare one observed direct branch with that same branch plus two unused sibling branches. Repeat the intervention around a one-rewrite terminal. If the terminal-output distributions change, investigate prompt, seed, scheduling, or selection effects. In parallel, compare at least one ancestral contrast under the same prompt pool. If the null controls do not behave as predicted, expanding to more worlds before repairing the pipeline would only estimate the leak more precisely.

# 4\. Single-message study

## 4.1 Estimands and conditions

The observation is one terminal message, not a set. Outcomes include discriminability of ancestry properties, proposition-preservation changes from the world, feature-family contribution, and transfer across held-out prompts, worlds, genres, and producer families. The target properties are ancestral rewrite depth, direct fan-in, root-source diversity, and the attenuation of their signals by later rewrites. A named motif is not the prediction target unless its operational graph property is separately identified.

A lean confirmatory grid consists of D0 (one observation), NC0 (one selected observation plus unused independent branches), R1 (one observation followed by one rewrite), NCR1 (the same realized terminal path with unused sibling rewrites), R3 (three successive rewrites), F2I (synthesis of two independent observations), F3I (synthesis of three independent observations), F3D (synthesis of three derivatives of one observation), F3I-R1 (F3I followed by one rewrite), and F3D-R1 (F3D followed by one rewrite). All synthesis terminals use a common synthesis-function instruction family. All rewrite nodes use a common rewrite-function family. Node-specific prompt variants are assigned independently of graph label and predeclared for either development or held-out transfer.

NC0 versus D0 and NCR1 versus R1 test non-ancestral causal nulls. R1 versus R3 tests cumulative rewriting under matched rewrite functions. F2I versus F3I tests a change in fan-in while holding the terminal function fixed, but also changes the available evidence volume unless input content is controlled. F3I versus F3D tests ancestry of multiple inputs, but the contrast will also change input diversity if independent observers sample different propositions. F3I-R1 versus F3D-R1 measures whether the distinction survives an additional rewrite. Each positive comparison must specify whether it identifies the total consequence of a production regime or a narrower structural component.

## 4.2 Matching and factorization

All nodes of a given function should share the same task purpose and eligible prompt pool across topologies. A source should not be told that its report constitutes independent corroboration, and a rewrite should not be instructed to behave as laundering unless concealment is a separately randomized factor. Output length and genre can be randomized and crossed across graph conditions rather than hard-coded by motif. Within a genre, impose comparable output requirements at terminals, but do not demand identical textual realization. Record token-count effects instead of automatically removing length, because length may be one mediator of ancestry effects.

For synthesis comparisons, vary direct input count separately from total proposition coverage and total incoming token budget where feasible. A fixed-information intervention can pass redundant or partitioned material through different numbers of parents, while a complementary total-effect contrast allows independent observations to supply diverse facts. This separates the influence of aggregation architecture from the amount of available evidence. For F3I versus F3D, independently manipulate source sharing and the informational diversity of inputs rather than treating a 70% sampling rule as sufficient isolation. These designs allow the effect of source ancestry to be distinguished from the effect of encountering more distinct claims.

Do not adjust away a mediator in a post hoc regression and then call the coefficient the causal effect of ancestry. Use explicitly assigned input packages to fix or vary information diversity. Measure realized overlap, omissions, contradictions, and source-to-terminal drift for interpretation. The inferential target must be stated for each contrast before generating data.

## 4.3 Tests and falsifiers

The negative controls should demonstrate compatibility with the declared causal graph under a prespecified practical-equivalence criterion. For a binary discriminator AUC is a possible measure, but a narrow interval around 0.50 is not a universal definition of negligible leakage. Choose a margin based on the smallest discrimination effect that would materially affect the proposed positive conclusions and demonstrate via simulation that the world-level sample can distinguish equivalence from low power. Include flexible feature and text-representation probes in a designated stress-test analysis, while preventing feature/model selection on the final holdout.

A preregistered transfer test should fit the ancestry predictor on one set of prompt templates and evaluate it on entirely withheld templates assigned to all nodes in test trials, not merely on an alternative terminal-node prompt. A second family of producer models is a stronger external-generating-condition test only if the corresponding prompts preserve the same causal functions. Failure of transfer would limit the inference to prompt and model regimes. A contrast might remain predictive yet still fail to identify a general property of graph topology outside those regimes.

# 5\. Multi-message study: dependence among observed reports

## 5.1 Observation and assignment

The second observation unit is a set of three messages about the same event. Conditions are IND, three independently drawn observations each passed through one rewrite; DEP, one observation copied into three independently generated rewrites; and MIX, two rewrites of one observation plus a rewrite of another. In all three conditions, a randomly selected member should have the same marginal generation policy conditional on world, observer-access regime, prompt pool, and attribution policy. The dependence structure appears in the joint distribution through shared source realizations. Randomly permute message positions and remove structural labels and branch-indexed formatting from the observed package.

Equal rewrite depth is necessary but insufficient to establish this marginal equality. It is possible for a source prompt in DEP to mention multiple downstream uses, for MIX to be displayed in a fixed order, or for attribution instructions to alter the content of individual messages. Before interpreting any set-level classifier, test whether a blind classifier given only one randomly selected message can distinguish IND, DEP, and MIX on independent test worlds. If singleton prediction exceeds a prespecified negligible range, the relational analysis must account for or remove that leakage.

## 5.2 Evidence and comparators

The primary paired outcome is whether two reports share the same realized source observation. The set-level outcome is whether the three reports form IND, DEP, or MIX. Both require ground truth from audited lineage, not an assignment label alone. Source-independent reports may share facts or model-specific default phrasing because they observe the same world and use the same language model. Shared ancestry must therefore be inferred from similarity in deviations relative to an appropriate independently generated, same-world baseline, not raw agreement.

Construct two measures with distinct information requirements. A world-anchored measure uses known propositions, source-to-message changes, and shared departures from the world. A world-free measure uses unusually shared lexical choices, proposition ordering, omissions inferred from the set, and pairwise semantic divergence relative to independent same-event reports. The first is an upper reference for a controlled experiment. The second is closer to a field-usable method but requires a valid empirical null of unrelated reports on the same event. Both must be fitted without test-world leakage. A separate prompted reader may judge pairs and triads, but its amount of supervision should be made comparable to that of the trained classifier where practical.

A positive relational effect is the improvement from adding pairwise dependence features beyond an order-invariant aggregation of three singleton-message predictors. That comparison prevents the paper from presenting any benefit of observing three texts as evidence specifically about their relationships. Calibrate posterior probabilities and use balanced accuracy, pairwise AUC, multiclass log loss or Brier score, and conditional false-positive rates when independent messages share standardized reporting conventions.

## 5.3 Mechanisms and alternative explanations

Randomized observation access can manipulate the overlap of world propositions. A partial-access condition may create more independent variation than full access, but the effect is not guaranteed. Independent model calls can converge on conventional omissions and errors, and descendants of one source can diverge under aggressive rewriting. Factorize source sharing, proposition overlap, and rewriting strength where possible. Concealment can remove attribution cues without changing shared inherited content only if it is implemented as narrowly controlled postprocessing. A generative instruction to conceal can also change paraphrase, citation behavior, and claim selection, so the claim that it leaves relational evidence untouched must be tested rather than assumed.

Pairwise observations from the same triad are not independent. Replicates from the same world and prompt pool also share conditions. Analyses should respect nesting of pairs in sets in worlds, with variation over producer or genre if claims extend to those dimensions. A world-only bootstrap may underestimate uncertainty in estimates that reuse the same source observation in several messages or rely on a selected model learned across folds.

# 6\. Measurement and statistics

The original 14 surface features should be retained as a baseline, not as a complete operational definition of semantic, formal, and trace residue. Create prespecified, observable feature families: message form, attributed-source/discourse indicators, world-anchored proposition behavior, and between-message relational features. They need not be mutually exclusive latent constructs. Named entities, for example, are surface counts that can also reflect content. Reports of incremental accuracy should not be treated as construct validation without manipulations that target the alleged property and show the expected differential response.

World-anchored proposition matching requires an error audit. Validate preservation, omission, alteration, and conflict-resolution decisions against blinded human annotations sampled across graph conditions, genres, and both producer families. If the matcher is more likely to call a synthesis output preserved than a relay output for superficial reasons, a structural finding can be an artifact of measurement. Retain both human-corrected and automated results when their disagreement is consequential.

The four held-out worlds in the submitted experiment establish only a narrow conditional test. A new design should have an independently selected population of event records, prespecified splits by world, and variance diagnostics from a pilot before a sample count is fixed. A plausible planning range of approximately 50 to 80 worlds is not a statistical justification. The final number should be selected from a simulation using the minimally interesting contrast and estimated between-world heterogeneity. Report per-world and per-condition performance, not only pooled intervals. Distinguish fixed-corpus descriptive accuracy, average performance over sampled worlds, and generalization to new genres or models.

For probability predictions, report multiclass Brier score and log loss with the scoring convention made explicit. If the reader returns invalid or incomplete probability vectors, preserve the original output and a prespecified policy rather than assigning post hoc favorable replacements. Confusion matrices, macro-averaged metrics, and predicted-label entropy address different properties. None substitutes for a test of calibration. Hyperparameter selection and probability calibration must occur in training-world folds, with a final untouched evaluation set or nested outer folds.

# 7\. Scale, pilot and stopping rules

Use an engineering pilot before committing to a large factorial run. It should include negative-control pairs, one depth comparison, one synthesis independence comparison, and IND/DEP/MIX triads across a modest set of new worlds. Time individual observation, rewrite, synthesis, and reader calls separately, including prompt and output tokens, retries, validation, and idle time. A previously reported average of approximately nine seconds per call reflects the original generator and cannot be assumed for longer contexts, new model families, or fan-in. Serial elapsed time should be calculated from the sum of actual call types and model-specific latency distributions, then distinguished from feasible concurrent wall time.

As an illustration rather than a commitment, 60 worlds × 10 single-message conditions × 3 replications produces 1,800 terminal artifacts. Under the call counts defined above, 37 producer calls per world-replicate block imply 6,660 producer calls for this example. Two reader evaluations per artifact add 3,600 reader calls. At an unsupported uniform nine seconds per call, this is 25.65 serial hours before analysis or failures. A three-message study with 60 worlds, three topologies, two access regimes, two attribution regimes, and three replications produces 2,160 message sets. With six, four, and five producer calls for IND, DEP, and MIX respectively, the producer budget is 10,800 calls. Two reader evaluations per set add 4,320, implying 37.8 serial hours at the same assumed latency. These illustrative figures should not enter a final plan until the pilot validates the factors and throughput.

A staged decision process avoids a costly redesign that merely produces a better controlled null result. Gate 1 verifies code/lineage correspondence and removes the original implementation ambiguities. Gate 2 confirms that nonancestral siblings are practically inert under the implemented generator. Gate 3 establishes whether any ancestral contrast survives prompt and genre changes. Gate 4 establishes whether message relationships improve dependence detection beyond singleton features. If Gate 3 fails, the paper can become a principled identifiability-boundary study, but it should not claim recoverability of hidden graph properties. If Gate 4 fails, do not retain the motivation about evidential independence as a demonstrated method. Expand to more worlds or model families only after interpreting these tests.

# 8\. Human-data anchor and external generalization

Transfer from synthetic language-model communication to human communication is a separate empirical question. A secondary human-lineage benchmark would improve the paper only if its labels match one of the redesigned outcomes. METER’s Press Association-to-newspaper reuse judgments are evidence about document derivation from a known wire source, not general pairwise independence among all newspaper articles. Artificial manuscript-copying traditions with known stems more directly anchor shared ancestry but differ from contemporary news production and may have permission restrictions. Press-release-to-news datasets can trace transmission or distortion of claims, yet associated coverage does not necessarily prove each article’s full textual ancestry.

Choose an external corpus only after specifying the label, prediction unit, and admissible text rights. A zero-adaptation test of a synthetic-trained relational score is one useful stress test, not a guaranteed estimate of its field validity. Where rights permit, compare it with domain-calibrated and retrained models using source-grouped or event-grouped holdouts. If no human corpus offers verified lineage labels for IND/DEP/MIX, omit the field-validation claim rather than constructing synthetic labels from textual similarity.

# 9\. Claim hierarchy and journal strategy

After successful negative controls and matched ancestral contrasts, the paper can claim that defined graph interventions affect terminal text through their causal ancestors and that some effects are detectable with specified feature families under the tested model and world conditions. If set-level relational gains are established, the paper can claim that shared ancestry among multiple synthetic reports produces recoverable similarities beyond their independent agreement about the world. A result across producer families and prompt/genre holdouts would support a broader claim about model-pipeline robustness, not automatically about human networks.

The existing 61.2% headline should remain a historical demonstration of the confound rather than the main evidence for structural measurement. The reported 96.4% independent-corroboration classification is specifically useful as a cautionary result: a label can be highly predictable even when its defining unseen branches are causally irrelevant to the observed terminal. If the compound implementation defect is confirmed, replace the mechanism narrative with an explicit versioned correction. Do not describe it as a boundary on composition recoverability.

Venue selection should follow the result. If the advance is identifiability in language-model production graphs with rigorous out-of-distribution testing, an ML-methods venue such as Transactions on Machine Learning Research warrants assessment. If it is a broader network-measurement result across graph interventions, Network Science becomes plausible. If the strongest evidence concerns how agreement among reports reflects information dependence, computational communication or social-network outlets become more natural. Scientific Reports or a suitably scoped transfer journal may be viable after the design and claims are repaired, but a transfer does not address a code defect or identification failure.
