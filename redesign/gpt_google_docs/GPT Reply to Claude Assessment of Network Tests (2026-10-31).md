# GPT Reply to Claude Assessment of Network Tests (2026-10-31)

Adjudication of seven disagreements and five questions

31 October 2026

# Disposition

I accept Claude’s central recommendation: do not enlarge the topology bank for this manuscript. The existing full run answers the two questions that Part II of the v8 design separates. One final message permits modest recovery of some ancestral attributes, especially relay depth, while three outputs support much stronger inference about shared origin. A conditional, small single-message hub-versus-diamond signal remains, but the anomalous direct-report negative control warrants replication. The shared-omission explanation requires a validated content judge and a more discriminating salience benchmark (Claude, 2026a; Project analysis, 2026a–d).

I also accept four of Claude’s practical decisions: defer the four-terminal partition extension, reject balanced-versus-sequential merging for this manuscript, reject the masked-information experiment as currently motivated, and reduce the single-message predictor analysis. I contest several rationales and one proposed implementation. Most importantly, the direct-report anomaly is not a general numerical “noise floor” applicable to every learner, the 0.515 aggregate-of-singles estimate is not a single observed message, and estimating world-specific salience using held-out-world texts cannot silently become a feature of a world-held-out predictor.

Final recommendation is the narrow package of a frozen, fresh-seed replication of P1’s anomalous direct-report control and P2, the already-required human validation augmented to assess correlated omission errors, and a salience-sensitive reanalysis that respects the world-held-out boundary. A small, carefully selected pathwise decomposition is conditional on validation and on whether salience adjustment leaves a substantive unexplained shared-omission advantage. No new graph shape is necessary.

# Evidence and inferential status

The current text-only report gives AUC 0.620 (formal and attributional features) and 0.675 (embeddings) for one-relay versus three-relay chains, and 0.552 for the feature-based full-access hub versus diamond. The later inference report estimates the latter one-sided refitted permutation p at 0.023 using 999 permutations. P3 is a 0.142 held-out AUC gain, 0.841 relational versus 0.699 pooled, with a refitted gain interval of 0.092 to 0.265 and p \= 0.010 over 200 resamples. The current secondary report gives three-class relational macro AUC 0.783, five-configuration accuracy 0.591 and identification of the Y’s odd message in 81% of Y sets. The direct-report/dead-end-control embedding comparison remains AUC 0.553, with its 90% interval 0.531–0.577 and a 199-permutation one-sided p of 0.015. In contrast, its formal-feature comparison is 0.491, with the entire 90% interval inside the equivalence band (Project analysis, 2026a–c).

The current anchored report gives full-access excess-only AUC 0.828 and pooled-plus-excess AUC 0.831, with omission-only pooled-plus-excess AUC 0.829. The same anchored feature has minimal incremental value under partial access after the opportunity restriction, while relational text features still classify that arm very well. All anchored results remain explicitly unvalidated. The judge also has fourteen internally inconsistent dispute resolutions among 5,324 judged disputes. These facts delimit, rather than establish, the omission mechanism (Project analysis, 2026d).

# I. Response to the seven disagreements

## 1\. Study 2 one-, two- and three-message curve. Accept the decision not to add it as a main empirical test, qualify two premises.

Claude is correct that Study 2 was built to equalize the marginal distribution of one terminal message across independent sources, out-star and Y. Every terminal is one rewrite from a single observation, with slot-matched instruction templates and the same full-record access in the primary arm. Part IV explicitly distinguishes canonical generation slots from randomly permuted presentation positions and shares exact realized outputs across conditions where keys coincide. Thus a one-message classifier near chance mainly checks the intended isolation/equal-marginal property. It does not independently discover that multi-message information is relational. Family B’s IND-versus-out-star AUC of 0.606 with a 90% interval 0.554–0.659 is inconclusive under the stipulated 0.43–0.57 rule, not evidence of a demonstrated beyond-tolerance leak. The result nevertheless cautions against claiming exact marginal equality was empirically proven in every stratum (Claude, 2026b, Part IV; Project analysis, 2026b).

Two corrections. First, secondary.md’s “aggregate of single-message predictions” fits a classifier to individual messages but then AVERAGES THREE predictions, one from each terminal, into a set probability. Its macro AUC 0.515 is not an observation from only one terminal. Claude is correct that it is the relevant no-explicit-pairwise-relation comparison, but incorrect that it directly supplies the one-message point as proposed. Second, the quoted two-message 0.833 macro-AUC ceiling follows if pair-root status is the ONLY information available, the three source-partition classes have the designed exchangeable pair distributions, and root-sharing is known perfectly. Its pairwise one-versus-one AUC components are 1, 2/3 and 5/6. It is not a universal upper bound on an arbitrary text-based two-message classifier if those conditional distributions differ in other ways. This qualification does not rescue the evidence curve as a high-priority addition: any two-message result would need its combinatorial reference model, and the existing H7 and pair-root analyses already answer most of the substantive question.

Disposition: reject I as a new main analysis. Retain the conceptual distinction between one-message marginal isolation, pairwise relatedness and full-set partition recovery.

## 2\. Pooled baseline is already a set-level summary. Accept the correction, reject the phrase “lower bound on relational information.”

The pooled P3 baseline takes each formal/attributional feature’s mean and maximum across three messages. Maximum relative to mean makes across-message dispersion available even without aligning words or propositions. I should not have called A\_0 purely “single-message information.” The paired-model AUC gain 0.142 is precisely the incremental value of explicitly pairwise lexical, order and embedding relations OVER these pooled distributional summaries. The aggregate of three independently generated single-text predictions, macro AUC 0.515 on the three-class target, is a distinct secondary benchmark (Claude, 2026b, Part V).

It does not follow mathematically that ΔAUC \= 0.142 is a lower bound on the amount of relational information. AUC is not a measure of information and is not additive across feature sets. The pooled and pairwise classifiers differ in their training optimization, and the three-class 0.783 versus 0.515 figures cannot be subtracted to infer a binary P3 lower bound because their target differs from the binary IND-versus-out-star result. The defensible statement is narrower: the reported incremental gain is measured against a comparator that ALREADY encodes set-level dispersion, so it should not be interpreted as the total gain attributable to seeing relations instead of seeing only one message.

Disposition: accept the baseline-language correction. Reject the formal lower-bound claim. No additional model fit follows.

## 3\. Pathwise omission persistence versus sibling compression. Accept the conceptual decomposition, with a measurement qualification.

Claude’s three categories are better matched to the Study 2 graphs: omissions in the common observation transmitted down the out-star branches; omissions independently introduced by sibling rewrites of the same observed parent text; and analogous omissions independently produced by unrelated source observations in the three-unconnected-sources condition. The first two are ancestrally conditioned in different senses. Only the first is an inherited idiosyncrasy already present at the shared source. Calling the second simply “independent convergence” obscures that the siblings were exposed to exactly the same parent’s wording, framing and retained facts. The full-access report’s mean excess events, 2.67 per pair for the out-star and 1.55 for independent sources, show that convergence on omission is substantial even without shared descent. Their difference is roughly 1.12 excess events per pair, but not a causal decomposition of the difference (Project analysis, 2026d).

A parent’s omission ordinarily cannot be corrected by a child whose only input is the parent. But “near certain by construction” is not a mathematical identity: descendants can infer omitted details from retained context, interpolate a likely value, invent a detail, or incidentally restate a proposition that the parent left implicit. More importantly, the parent and child are independently judged texts, and repeated false absences from shared paraphrase can manufacture apparent inheritance. The transition analysis therefore remains valuable principally as a MEASUREMENT and mechanism-discrimination test, not because the causal fact that children only see their parents is in doubt. Its critical result is the partition of terminal shared omissions into common-root absences and shared losses generated AFTER branching, with the latter benchmarked against independent-source overlap.

Disposition: accept Claude’s reframing and reject a large chain-trajectory expansion. Conditional on judge validation, assess the out-star/Y root-to-terminal paths and the correlated-error rate. Do not claim an inherited-innovation mechanism from the terminal shared-omission AUC alone.

## 4\. Calibration and structural attribute probabilities. Accept most restrictions.

A calibrated probability is conditional on the empirical class-prior mixture and the particular generator/instruction environment. Exact-history top-k accuracy inherits the 13-class frequency distribution and adds little when the fitted predictor overwhelmingly selects a few history classes. We should not choose selective thresholds retrospectively. Attributes involving number of synthesis inputs or independent roots are partly distinguished by how access packages differ in the partitioned hub arms, so a single all-condition probability of “multiple roots” cannot be advertised as a general topology-only diagnostic.

A modest qualification: prior-dependent calibration is not inherently meaningless. It is meaningful for prediction under the study’s explicitly stated prior mixture, but not for claims about arbitrary naturally occurring messages. A within-level-2 discrimination or reliability table is preferable if the manuscript wants the narrower question of structural recoverability at fixed assigned information. If probabilities from the all-condition model are examined in a level-2 subset, they are NOT automatically calibrated for that subset: the relevant priors and conditional feature distributions may differ. Label such a table as subset reliability of the existing model, or cross-fit any recalibration using training-world observations only. The existing attribute AUCs, such as pre-synthesis rewriting 0.676 and synthesis 0.631, are across the study’s mixture and require this scope qualification (Project analysis, 2026b).

Disposition: retain only a small, descriptive within-design attribute analysis, subordinate to the established chain-depth and hub-versus-diamond contrasts. Drop proposed top-k and confidence-threshold exercises.

## 5\. Common-information masking. Accept rejection as unnecessary, contest literal redundancy.

The full-access independent-source and out-star conditions already hold the complete twenty-fact world record fixed at the observation stage. They are the correct current comparison for shared ancestry without differences in assigned access. In partial access, the corrected judge statistic restricts comparisons to facts both messages could have received, and the attributable omission advantage becomes negligible. These findings remove the immediate manuscript need for a masked-input experiment.

However, independently generated messages from the SAME restricted fifteen-fact record have not been directly manipulated. Full access is not literally the same manipulation as common selective access, and excluding inaccessible facts from the deviation count cannot tell us how shared input selection influences the remaining text. The experiment could answer a boundary-condition question, but that question is unnecessary if the manuscript restricts its account to full, identical access and treats partial access as a different production environment.

Disposition: reject IV NOW on incremental-contribution grounds, not because an exact duplicate already exists.

## 6\. Control discrepancy and priority. Accept the proposed small replication, reject a universal noise floor and guaranteed resolution.

The full run establishes 180 paired direct-report/control requests with identical request hashes, first-attempt acceptance and matching stop reasons. Control calls occurred later in 169 pairs, with a median 29-second gap and nearly identical mean lengths. Timing remains a plausible nuisance. Later/earlier classification using ordinary features gave 0.533 with p \= 0.020 on 99 permutations, while the embedding learner gave 0.487. The original anomalous direct-report comparison is embedding-only at 0.553, whereas the direct-report feature comparison is 0.491 (Project analysis, 2026b,c). A replication with randomized execution order, fresh keys/seeds and a pure independent-draw null has substantial diagnostic value at low projected producer cost.

The phrase “noise floor at AUC 0.553” is not justified. AUC depends on the comparison, training labels, learner and marginal distributions. The 0.577 upper interval limit from the embedded negative control is an upper confidence bound for THAT contrast, not a universal minimal detectable structural effect. P2’s feature AUC 0.552 is not rendered invalid by a numerically similar embedding AUC on a different pair of conditions. Moreover, the current refitted one-sided P2 permutation p \= 0.023 with 999 permutations, together with P3 p \= 0.010, passes the reported two-test Holm family. The P2 claim is a small, conditional discrimination result, not an accurate single-item topology diagnosis. It remains vulnerable to generation artifacts, but it has not been nullified by the control result.

Claude’s calculation that four independent null tests would have about a 6% chance of at least one p ≤ 0.015 is a useful heuristic, not an adjusted p-value. The four controls share worlds, generation context and feature extraction. The later permutation result repeats the same data, so it is not independent replication. A new stochastic replication can reduce uncertainty but may leave both contrasts inconclusive. Its projected \$0.55 cost also requires actual call-count and credit checks before execution. The named four “arms” describe five output categories if the extra independent-draw null is counted separately from both direct-report and control, hub and diamond. Fix the exact plan and comparison keys before using a budget estimate.

Disposition: accept fresh-seed replication as the highest-value OPTIONAL producer expenditure before manuscript closure. Predefine separate outcomes for P1 feature and embedding learners, the pure null, and P2. Preserve matched worlds, class balance and world-held-out evaluation. Interpret no outcome as a proof of a universal noise floor.

## 7\. Exploratory status and preregistration. Accept.

The project is explicitly exploratory. Asking for formal preregistration of a new extension was unnecessary, and the v8 design already distinguishes registered primary analyses from secondary and exploratory work. The proper safeguard for the proposed diagnostic replication is to freeze the comparison, seed and ordering rules, outcome metrics, world grouping and decision criteria before running it, and then disclose that it was motivated by an observed anomaly. This can be done in an internal dated analysis record without claiming prospective registration or a fresh confirmatory population sample.

Disposition: use a locked, transparent replication protocol. Do not call it preregistered, and do not add a new shape solely to obtain a novel hypothesis test.

# II. Direct answers to Claude’s five questions

## Question 1\. Does Study 2 equalize one-message distributions, and does the family-B AUC of 0.606 matter?

Yes, accept the equal-marginal design. The causal contrast in Study 2 is the JOINT distribution of three terminal reports, not the marginal content of a random terminal. Under full access the same observation package, source-template policy, matched slot-level rewrite templates and output rules apply to every terminal. The out-star and Y structures differ in WHICH observations are reused, not in the intended law for an individually sampled observation and its single rewrite. In partial access the package law likewise equalizes the distribution of a randomly selected slot’s package across structures, while joint overlaps differ (Claude, 2026b, Part IV).

The current evidence does not establish perfect realized isolation. H7’s family-B full-neutral comparison of three independent sources against out-star is 0.606 \[0.554, 0.659\], which crosses the upper tolerance boundary of 0.57 and is consequently inconclusive, as secondary.md correctly states. Its point estimate deserves disclosure as the most extreme prompt-family stratum, but no causal explanation is identified from it. Conditional on the reported outputs, finite-sample fluctuation, default sampling/reuse behavior, slot-specific features, or execution-context effects could generate a marginal difference. One cannot infer a systematic template flaw solely from the observed AUC. For P3 the effect is bounded conceptually by the comparison to pooled features, which can use marginal differences and within-set dispersion. P3 remains an incremental relational-feature result, not a proof that individual messages are exactly exchangeable under every prompt family.

The five-configuration set score and the aggregate-of-three-singles score remain useful descriptive results. They should not be described as a one-versus-three-message empirical information curve. The latter “single” comparator actually sees three separately scored messages whose predictions are averaged.

## Question 2\. Which worlds should support the fresh-seed replication?

Use the same sixty evaluation worlds for the PRINCIPAL stochastic replication. The target is to determine whether the observed P1/P2 patterns recur when producer randomness and execution order change while the world and template environment remain fixed. Holding the worlds fixed is desirable for that conditional comparison. Use three new, nonoverlapping replicate identifiers and fresh call identities, counterbalance and randomize execution order, and avoid inherited stored keys that would return the original output. Preserve the original world-grouped five-fold assignment and the original learner/feature definitions, and conduct new-model training and evaluation wholly within the new replicates using world-held-out predictions. Do not train on old outputs from a given world and call predictions on new outputs from that same world an independent world-held-out test.

This is independence of generated calls, not independence of the world sample, prompt-pool selection, or prior analysis decisions. Describe it accordingly. The ten verification worlds are not a virgin out-of-sample test: they were inspected repeatedly during engineering, preflight and matcher development, and ten worlds give little precision for a 0.05-AUC effect. They can serve as an additional transport check if it costs virtually nothing, but they should not displace the sixty-world replication or be advertised as an untouched confirmatory holdout.

The pure-null comparison must be specified precisely. It consists of two independently generated terminal reports from identical requests with randomized labels that carry no systematic condition-specific content. With the same grouped fitting procedure, it tests whether analysis or execution conventions create an apparent classification signal even when the labels should be arbitrary. A near-chance score provides a useful negative check, but a single above-chance score is not definitive proof of software leakage. The D0/NC0 comparison separately asks whether the actual control-key and execution pathway matters. The hub/diamond comparison separately replicates the level-2 ancestry contrast. Count the graph calls from a dry plan before accepting the advertised seventeen calls per block or \$0.55 as an exact cost.

## Question 3\. Are three same-world Study 1 R1 outputs a valid salience null? Should they be shrunk?

Accept the substantive objection to the current random-placement expectation: proposition omission is not exchangeable across facts. Some propositions are routinely dropped because of narrative relevance, proposition type, placement or the producer’s compression habits. An expected shared-omission count of k\_a k\_b/N conditional on counts and event type ignores these differences. A salience-aware comparator could materially weaken or strengthen the asserted excess (Project analysis, 2026d).

But three Study 1 one-relay chain messages PER HELD-OUT WORLD do not, by themselves, supply a valid null feature for the cross-validated Study 2 predictor. Each of those messages is another realized output of the very world currently held out, and a world-specific event-omission profile is not something a learner trained on OTHER worlds can estimate for a newly encountered world solely from its record. It is world-specific auxiliary response data. Using it to construct a feature for a held-out world while claiming conventional world-held-out text prediction would cross the evaluation boundary, even though it does not explicitly use that world’s Study 2 labels. Moreover, each per-proposition rate has support only at 0, 1/3, 2/3 or 1 with three trials, and the Study 1 generation context is similar to, not identical with, Study 2’s source-template/rewrite-slot arrangement. The project’s call keys include STUDY, so similar calls in Studies 1 and 2 are not reused by key across studies.

I therefore distinguish two uses. For a DESCRIPTIVE, world-anchored diagnostic, same-world R1 omissions can serve as a noisy matched reference for what that particular event encourages independent writers to omit, provided the analysis is explicitly labeled as drawing on those auxiliary same-world texts. They may be especially useful for the decomposition of independent convergence rather than as a general predictive feature.

For a world-held-out salience-adjusted H8 estimate, any learned omission law must be fitted on TRAINING worlds only and applied to test-world propositions using predictors available from their world records, such as core status, dispute status and other prespecified fact characteristics. A model that pools by event type alone is sparse because the design has roughly four evaluation worlds of each event type. Position identifiers p00–p19 were shuffled across worlds, so “the omission probability of p07” is not a transferable semantic category. Shrinkage toward an overall or hierarchically pooled rate is preferable to three unregularized 0/1 per-world observations, but the shrinkage target must correspond to a meaningful, available fact category and its parameters must be selected without seeing test-world outcome labels. An alternative training-only reference is omission behavior in independent Study 2 observations from training worlds, which better matches the actual target generation law. Do not add both variants just to multiply analyses.

A noisy same-world diagnostic is better than uniform random placement for asking whether independence-induced compression converges on salient event facts, but not automatically better for the registered prediction estimand. The decisive report should show whether the out-star versus independent-source difference survives plausible salience adjustments, with uncertainty clustered by world, and whether the result depends on the judge’s classification of omission. It must retain the explicitly unvalidated status until human coding passes.

## Question 4\. Is 80 pairs plus 26 triples among 240 messages sufficient validation? What transition criterion applies?

Not as a wholesale replacement for the v8 natural sample. Eighty nonoverlapping parent-child pairs use 160 messages and twenty-six distinct sibling triples use 78, exhausting 238 of 240 slots. That leaves no room for independent stratification of the prescribed 240 verification messages across BOTH studies, structural groups, observation/synthesis/rewrite functions and all three producer environments. It also makes the effective number of independent clusters much smaller than 240 messages. If pairs and triples reuse nodes, the arithmetic and coverage change again. The arrangement might be appropriate for a dedicated pathwise substudy, but it does not by itself satisfy v8 Part VI.

Keep the mandated natural sample: 240 verification-run messages stratified by study, condition group, node function and producer, with 60 independently double-coded by a second design-uninvolved coder. Within that sampling frame, deliberately include a subset of linked parent-child pairs and out-star sibling groups, with known unique-message and independent-world denominators, while preserving the broad strata. If the design’s natural-sample coverage cannot be maintained simultaneously with enough linked examples, add a small, separately labeled relational validation subset rather than silently reallocate the entire 240\. The exact number of linked pairs and triples should be chosen after checking eligible verification graphs and the frequency of positive “stated→absent” transitions, not by filling a round 238-message quota.

Retain ALL specified validation gates. The natural sample must report the confusion matrix, class counts and intervals for four proposition states and four conflict-resolution categories, independently assessed pre-adjudication kappa on the double-coded cases, pooled class-balanced accuracy at least 0.80 for each judgment, and the stipulation that a natural class with fewer than thirty instances is NOT validated. The separate controlled challenge set still requires at least forty instances of each judgment class for each node function and producer, per-class recall at least 0.85, false-change rate below 0.10 on meaning-preserving hard negatives, and the stripped/unstripped invariance pairs. An 80-pair allocation does not waive these requirements (Claude, 2026b, Part VI).

For linked texts, construct truth transitions from the TWO human-coded states rather than assigning a second, independent transition label. Report agreement on the categories “stated at both”, “stated then absent”, and “absent then stated”, with the more general four-state transition matrix available. Of particular concern is the judge’s JOINT false-absence rate when human coders find the same proposition stated in both parent and child, and among out-star siblings when human coders find it stated in all descendants. Compare the observed joint false-absence frequency with what would follow from independent errors, and estimate how many false shared-omission events this could add per terminal pair. Include inherited paraphrase in the hard-negative challenges. Do not assume coder disagreements are independent merely because they arise on different messages from the same origin.

There is no transition-specific threshold in v8. I would NOT retroactively present a new 0.80 transition-balanced-accuracy cutoff as another preregistered gate. Require the existing per-state and challenge gates and report transition-level balanced accuracy and class-specific recalls as additional diagnostics. For a mechanistic claim, the bias/sensitivity analysis must also show that plausible correlated false absences cannot explain the approximately 1.12-event-per-pair observed full-access out-star-versus-independent excess. That comparison is more inferentially relevant than choosing an arbitrary extra transition threshold. If the linked-sample precision is too poor to establish this, retain the terminal text-only prediction result and withhold the stronger pathwise mechanism claim.

## Question 5\. Should P1 follow the three-way rule and P2 be conditional pending replication?

Yes to the first. The direct-report/dead-end-control embedding result is INCONCLUSIVE by the v8 rule: its 90% interval 0.531–0.577 intersects, but is not contained within, the tolerance band 0.43–0.57. The upper interval endpoint 0.577 is a bound for THIS directional AUC under the conditional world-bootstrap procedure. The direct-report formal-feature contrast and both one-relay control comparisons meet the equivalence criterion. The later two-sided/one-sided permutation p of 0.015 tests departure from the label-exchangeability null, not equivalence to the stipulated tolerance. A small statistically detectable difference can coexist with an inconclusive practical-equivalence judgment (Project analysis, 2026a,c).

No to mechanically declaring P2 “conditional on P1” as though P1 had failed the predefined rule. V8 Part V specifies conditional reporting of P2 and P3 if P1 finds a LEAK BEYOND TOLERANCE. The direct-report anomaly is inconclusive, not beyond tolerance, so this formal condition has not been triggered. The current inference report correctly shows the refitted one-sided P2 p \= 0.023 and P3 p \= 0.010, with both rejected under the two-test Holm procedure. State that P2 shows a SMALL detectable discriminative trace in the current fixed-world environment, while individual classification remains weak. Explicitly disclose the anomalous P1 embedding result as a substantive robustness warning that motivates replication. These two statements are consistent with v8 and do not hide the anomaly.

Before the fresh-seed replication, use wording such as: “The hub and diamond are weakly but detectably separable by formal text features under this generation environment, although a distinct embedding-based negative-control contrast also shows a small unexplained difference.” After the replication, revise the scope according to the newly observed P1 and P2 results rather than applying a universal AUC threshold.

# III. Final ranking and minimum package

**Rank 1\.** Fresh-seed, execution-order-randomized replication of the direct-report negative control, the independent-draw null and the same-access hub-versus-diamond contrast. Recommendation: RUN, after freezing the protocol. This is the smallest producer expenditure that directly tests the outstanding threat to interpretation of weak single-message signals. Keep separate feature and embedding outcomes, exactly match the original prompts and validation rules, and count unique graph calls before treating the \$0.55 estimate as verified. Use the sixty fixed worlds for conditional stochastic replication and disclose that they are not new worlds. A replication is worthwhile even if it ends in a qualified null for P2.

**Rank 2\.** Human validation of the proposition judge, augmented by linked-text diagnostics. Recommendation: REQUIRED IN ANY EVENT if the manuscript claims inherited omissions, alterations or dispute resolution. Preserve the 240-message, sixty-double-coded natural sample and the separate full challenge set in v8 Part VI. Include strategically sampled parents, children, out-star sibling texts and inherited paraphrases without sacrificing the prescribed producer and node-function strata. Correlated omission errors are the critical extra validity problem. This item can proceed in parallel with rank 1\. It has greater absolute scientific importance than rank 1 if the paper’s principal theoretical claim will be about transmission of information loss, although its coder cost is higher.

**Rank 3\.** Salience-sensitive reanalysis of shared omissions. Recommendation: RUN AS AN EXPLORATORY DIAGNOSTIC after judge validation. This is the strongest direct rival to the current omission explanation: shared selection of generally expendable facts rather than inherited idiosyncrasy. The comparator must condition on credible fact-salience categories and respect the outer-fold training-world boundary for any model-performance claim. A same-world R1 calibration can be shown separately as an explicitly transductive, matched-event description. Do not substitute three raw per-fact estimates for a transferable held-out null. Report how much of the observed 1.12-event-per-pair out-star advantage remains after salience adjustment and correlated-judge-error sensitivity, without claiming a causal percentage.

**Rank 4\.** Focused pathwise decomposition using the stored Study 2 observations and terminal rewrites. Recommendation: CONDITIONAL. Judge approximately 540 unique full-access neutral observation texts only if validation passes and the salience diagnostic leaves a mechanism question the existing terminal comparison cannot answer. Distinguish shared-root omissions already present in the common observation from similar omissions independently introduced in sibling rewrites receiving that observation, and compare with independent-source convergence. The cost estimate of roughly \$1.15 OpenAI is plausible at \$0.0021 per new judgment but must be reconciled with actual credits and judge cache/reuse. Do not enlarge this into all Study 1 chain trajectories for the current manuscript.

**Rank 5\.** Reduced attribute-level analysis of the single-message history predictor. Recommendation: OPTIONAL, NO NEW GENERATION. Show one or two defensible attribute discriminations and their reliability under the study’s specific condition mixture, with a level-2-only description where assigned input information is fixed. Avoid post hoc confidence thresholds, top-k exercises or an implication of generally transferable individual posterior probabilities. This is useful for explaining the 20–22% exact-history accuracy, but it does not threaten or substantially alter the main result.

Not ranked as new work. Reject the one/two/three-message evidence curve as a principal experiment, despite its remaining two-message descriptive estimand. Reject common-information masking for the current paper, not as logically redundant in every possible design. Reject balanced-versus-sequential merging for the current paper given the weak level-2 single-message signal, the unresolved control anomaly and the leaf-depth confound. Defer four-terminal partitions and the two-pair network to a later scaling study. No new graph is needed for the present contribution.

Smallest defensible package: ranks 1–3, with judge validation proceeding regardless of rank 1’s outcome. Rank 4 is valuable if the judge is accurate and if the salience-adjusted result survives enough to warrant a pathwise explanation. Rank 5 can be done during manuscript drafting if the authors need more precise language about partial recovery from one report. This package is intentionally smaller than the six-test memo. All proposed post hoc diagnostics are exploratory. Neither success nor failure authorizes renaming the already observed AUC gain as the share of a causal mediation mechanism.

# IV. Implications for the manuscript

The opening question remains viable without added topologies. A final report can carry a detectable but limited signature of how it was produced, especially the number of successive rewrites. Reconstructing a precise thirteen-class history from one artifact is substantially harder. The distinction between a common-source diamond and an independent-observer hub is weak in ranking terms and sensitive to the generation environment, even though the feature-based primary test presently clears its one-sided permutation criterion. When several terminal reports can be compared, their joint content supplies much stronger evidence about whether they descend from shared or separate origins.

The text-only claim stands independently of the proposition judge. The more specific proposition that shared omission is an important carrier of ancestry information is well motivated by the unvalidated anchored data but must await human validation and a credible salience comparator. It may ultimately require the narrower language “shared absences from a common input” rather than “inherited idiosyncratic omissions” if sibling compression accounts for a large part of the excess. That refinement would improve causal precision without undermining the recoverability finding.

The outcome of this adjudication is to finish the present question before broadening the network taxonomy. Spend scarce time on making the small single-terminal result interpretable and the shared-departure instrument trustworthy, not on adding graph shapes because they can be generated.
