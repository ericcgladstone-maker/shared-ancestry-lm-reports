# GPT Attack on Difference Table (2026-10-30)

Independent defect audit of Claude\_Diagnosis\_and\_Redesign\_v2\_2026-10-30.md, Part IV, with checks of Parts V and IX. Prepared 30 October 2026\. Status: pre-freeze critique, not a revised design.

Scope and evidentiary basis. I read the v2 proposal and the linked original run\_experiment\_paper1.py, regime\_instantiators.py, model\_client.py, receiver\_dispatcher.py, and the original 20-world JSONL file. I also checked the call-count arithmetic. The redesigned dispatcher, its prompt families, assigned packages, and new lineage audit do not yet exist. Thus defects below are demonstrated conflicts or ambiguities in the specification, not claims that the future code already behaves incorrectly. I previously read Claude’s v1 proposal and wrote the GPT critique and retrospective redesign. Agreement with v2 is not independent confirmation. Original-code findings are identified separately from conclusions drawn from v2.

# Bottom line

The v2 table is materially better than version 1, especially the matched input depth, same-function synthesis comparison, explicitly crossed source-access conditions, trial-level prompt families, and the distinction between causal nulls and positive recoverability. I would not freeze it. The most consequential unresolved issues concern the meaning of the F1/F2/F3 fan-in contrast, whether the input-independence contrasts also vary prompt-template diversity, whether IND/DEP/MIX have identical singleton marginals under assigned partial packages, and whether H8 actually identifies relational information. The paired-replay negative control is under-specified and its proposed execution order can prevent it from testing the very resource-state channel it names. Pilot gates 2 to 4 currently support stronger inferential conclusions than ten worlds and two replications can provide. Most problems can be repaired in the table and its condition definitions. I see no need to replace the two-study design.

# A. Defects ordered by potential effect on conclusions

## 1\. F1 versus F2 versus F3: fan-in remains bundled with independent-root count and evidence allocation \[HIGH\]

Contrast and unlisted difference. F1, F2, and F3 vary not only the number of direct inputs to a synthesis node but also the number of independently generated root observations, the number of observer prompts and decoding draws, the length and composition of each observer’s evidence packet, and the opportunity for the producer to reconcile a contradictory proposition pair before aggregation. These differences have causal paths through the observed parent texts into the synthesis prompt and terminal output. In particular, source multiplicity is a separate graph property from fan-in, and a one-input synthesis is not simply a three-input synthesis with two edges removed when the source packages are repartitioned.

Mishandled handling and repair. The table accepts observation-call count as “inherent in fan-in,” which is true only for this particular implementation, not for fan-in in general. Naming H3 “fan-in at fixed information” would imply isolation of fan-in while independent root count changes deterministically with it. Either rename the contrast as the total effect of increasing independently generated, partitioned inputs to a synthesis node at fixed assigned union, or drop the claim that H3 identifies fan-in independently of source multiplicity. The existing SI/SD comparison at fan-in three provides a separate root-sharing contrast, but it does not orthogonalize roots and fan-in across F1/F2/F3. Renaming requires no new study.

## 2\. F1 versus F2 versus F3: the core-event constraint contradicts partitioning and zero redundancy \[HIGH\]

Contrast and difference. Part V says all observers always receive the core event. Part IV says the world propositions are partitioned across k observers and that redundancy is zero at all levels. For k greater than one, a disjoint partition cannot simultaneously put the core proposition into every observer packet. If all observers receive the core, it becomes redundantly represented, and the number of copies reaching synthesis rises from one to k. That repeated event can become a salient cue in the terminal output even if the union of distinct propositions is constant. If the core goes to only one observer, the observers are not exchangeable and its position is correlated with a specific subpacket.

Repair. State which rule governs. The simpler correction is to hold a shared core proposition in all k packages and partition only the remaining propositions, then explicitly acknowledge the k-fold core redundancy and report the contrast as a total effect of partitioned evidence plus repeated core exposure. Alternatively put the core into exactly one package and withdraw the “always including the core event” condition. The claim of zero redundancy must be removed either way. The original worlds file assigns W001’s core proposition p1 to all three synthesis-input categories, which shows why this is a material rather than cosmetic distinction, although v2's future packages are new.

## 3\. F1 versus F2 versus F3: fixed proposition union does not hold realized evidence constant \[HIGH\]

Unlisted difference and path. The total assigned set of propositions can be identical while the texts available to the synthesis node differ substantially. A single observer sees all claims and both sides of a conflict, while partitioned observers see only their respective subsets. One observer may compare and resolve the conflict before writing. Separate observers may make incompatible selections or omit different items. Reconstructing the union from generated reports is not the same as delivering the complete assigned proposition union to synthesis. Source-message length, uncertainty, factual coverage, and conflict handling reach the terminal through the parent texts.

Repair. Retain “fixed assigned union” as the accurate design property and measure realized coverage, conflict-pair co-location, omission, and total input tokens as mediators. Explicitly balance which conflicting propositions co-occur in a single source packet and their order across F2/F3. Do not report the result as fan-in holding terminal information fixed. This is a total-effect contrast unless the input texts themselves are experimentally fixed, which would answer a different question.

## 4\. SI-same versus SD-same: independent observation prompts introduce template diversity \[HIGH\]

Unlisted difference and path. In SI-same, three observation calls independently draw source prompt variants from the trial’s A or B family. In SD-same, one observation call supplies all three rewrites. Even if every observer sees the same assigned package and all synthesis inputs have depth one, SI inherits a mixture of up to three source-template realizations and SD inherits one. Systematic heterogeneity in form, omissions, or conflict framing can therefore arise from prompt-variant differences, not source ancestry. The table says variants come from one family but does not handle the number of independent source-template draws, which changes with the number of roots. An identical-pool rule ensures equality in expectation, not equivalence of prompt-variant composition within the trial.

Repair. For the main SI-same/SD-same contrast, use one matched source prompt template for all observation calls within a matched trial block, with branch-specific independent seeds. Match the three rewrite templates by branch across SI and SD. Randomize the common source template across blocks, not separately by SI branch. A separately labeled sensitivity analysis may let source templates vary independently if prompt diversity is itself of interest. Then source-realization independence rather than instruction diversity is the intended difference. The proposed depth repair remains worthwhile but is insufficient without this correction.

## 5\. Study 2 IND/DEP/MIX: matched rewrite depth does not establish equal singleton distributions under partial access \[HIGH\]

Unlisted difference and path. IND requires three source observations, DEP one, and MIX two. Part IV says observer access is “identical across structures” within a partial-access level, but does not specify the package assignment law, whether packages are independently drawn, whether they are balanced across branch positions, or whether dependent branches share a package selected by a different procedure. If IND uses several complementary packages and DEP uses one union, their individual source texts differ in proposition count, conflict visibility, and length. Even if all roots sample from an identical marginal distribution, a fixed assignment of distinct packages to IND slots or the MIX odd branch can create position and selection effects. These source differences propagate through the one-rewrite output to individual messages.

Repair. Specify a single conditional marginal distribution for a randomly selected observation package given world and access stratum. Draw or assign packages by an exchangeable, condition-blind rule. Reuse a selected package when the graph calls for shared ancestry, without changing its marginal distribution. Couple world, source package, prompt template, and seed policy across the structure assignments. Test singleton discriminability within each access × attribution × prompt-family stratum, not only in a pooled sample where opposing cues may cancel. H7 remains a diagnostic, not a substitute for specifying the assignment mechanism.

## 6\. H8, Study 2: the stated baseline may make relational gain nearly tautological \[HIGH\]

Contrast and unlisted information difference. The proposed baseline is an order-invariant aggregate of three single-message predictions of IND/DEP/MIX. But a single message has no pair identity and, if H7 succeeds, provides almost no information about which other messages share its source. Aggregating three near-chance class probabilities is structurally incapable of representing pairwise overlap. A model using lexical overlap, shared omissions, or embedding similarity will improve partly because it receives the relevant cross-message information, not because a distinct inheritance mechanism has been validated. The label “relational gain” is descriptively accurate but H8 alone does not discriminate shared ancestral deviations from common event details or repeated genre conventions.

Repair. Keep the aggregate baseline but add an information-matched, non-ancestry set baseline that sees all three texts and uses permutation-invariant per-message descriptors without cross-message interactions. Measure the incremental gain from pairwise features above that baseline, and evaluate pairwise shared-source labels, not just three-class set labels. Fit the independent same-event similarity null only on training worlds. H8 then identifies predictive value added by cross-text relations under the specified null, while causal attribution to shared ancestry still depends on randomized source-sharing assignments. This is a benchmark correction within Study 2, not a new study.

## 7\. NC0/D0 and NCR1/R1: the promised paired seeds are not guaranteed by the seed formula \[HIGH\]

Difference and path. Part III derives seeds from trial and node identifiers, while H1 requires the terminal in each negative-control pair to have the same seed, prompt, and inputs. If trial IDs encode condition or the corresponding terminal nodes receive different IDs, the seeds differ by design. This creates ordinary stochastic variation in terminal content even if unused siblings are causally inert. The new dispatcher is not yet implemented, so the conflict is within the specification, not an observed defect. The original model\_client.py accepts a seed argument but its Ollama path does not pass it to the request and records seed\_supported=False (lines 81–91, 119–158). Thus prior code cannot validate the new seed guarantee.

Repair. Define a paired canonical call key derived from world, matched replicate, ancestral role, and prompt-variant assignment, deliberately omitting condition. Use it to assign identical seeds and exact inputs to corresponding ancestral calls in both arms. Assign all unused siblings separate nonoverlapping keys. Log the actual options.seed received by Ollama and validate reproducible replay before H1 is interpreted. A stable seeded hashing algorithm is required, not a language runtime hash that changes between sessions.

## 8\. NC0/D0 and NCR1/R1: “terminal first” neutralizes the operational channel the control claims to probe \[HIGH\]

Difference and path. Part IV prespecifies the observed branch as the first node and selects it before generation. If “first” also means executed before the unused siblings, there is no opportunity for their cache, scheduler, shared RNG, or server-state effects to influence an already generated terminal text. Then the negative control is nearly guaranteed under stateless prompts and tells little about what happens when an unused sibling executes first. Part III’s randomized execution order refers to condition order within world blocks, not explicitly to branch-call order inside a trial.

Repair. Fix the identity of the observed branch in advance but independently counterbalance its execution position among the sibling calls, without changing its seed or parent inputs. Include siblings-first and selected-branch-first executions as implementation stress cases. Record batching and parallelism rather than assuming all schedules are equivalent. The intervention test should compare output distributions and paired replay identity under matched antecedent state, with an uncertainty interval and noninferiority or equivalence criterion, not merely say that an identity rate “does not fall below” a baseline rate. If the aim is only the isolated abstract DAG, label the terminal-first comparison as an implementation sanity check rather than a general test of sibling inertness.

## 9\. Gate 2 and H1: the pilot cannot establish a narrow practical-equivalence claim \[HIGH\]

Mishandled gate consequence. With ten pilot worlds and two replications, each negative-control condition has only 20 observations, with at most ten independent world clusters. A binary classifier’s near-chance AUC under 20 positive and 20 negative observations has a rough independent-observation standard error near 0.09, before allowing for clustering. Its 90% interval would be on the order of ±0.15 even under favorable assumptions. Likewise, zero replay mismatches in 20 paired runs would leave a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound of roughly 14% for an underlying mismatch rate. The proposed pilot therefore cannot establish a stringent equivalence margin merely by declaring it after the pilot.

Repair. Treat Gate 2 as an engineering-invariance and detectable-leak screen, not as a population-level equivalence finding. A deterministic state-isolated replay can establish correspondence under tested settings if the calls truly reproduce, but the distributional equivalence test needs a separate precision budget. A failed narrow equivalence test means “insufficient precision or possible leak,” not necessarily a discovered leak. The Part IX statement “Level 1 nulls are established for this pipeline” is unsupported by the specified pilot and should be narrowed.

## 10\. Gate 3: its qualifying contrasts do not all receive the promised genre transfer test \[HIGH\]

Mismatch between Part V and Part IX. Gate 3 accepts at least one level-2 contrast among depth, fan-in, or SI-same/SD-same if it survives both a prompt-family and genre change. Part VII says the second-genre pilot includes R1/R3 and SI-same/SD-same, but not F1/F2/F3. Thus fan-in cannot satisfy the specified two-factor survival criterion using the planned pilot. Furthermore, a positive result in one contrast does not rescue the identifiability claim for the others, and 10 worlds are insufficient to treat a failure of a noisy positive test as a proof of nonidentifiability.

Repair. Either restrict Gate 3 to the two contrasts actually crossed with genre and make H3 a separately reported pilot diagnostic, or explicitly budget the second-genre fan-in cases. A pass authorizes expansion of the \*passing contrast\*, not a general claim about single-message ancestral structure. A failure is an inconclusive or unfavorable pilot result requiring scope reduction or redesign of the specific contrast, not evidence that all ancestral graph properties are nonidentifiable.

## 11\. SI-div versus SD-div: the label “independence” obscures a strong access-and-reconciliation intervention \[HIGH\]

Difference and path. SI-div gives each of three independent source observations a different partially overlapping package, whereas SD-div gives a single source the union. Beyond root count, the conditions change each source’s input length, access to competing claims, the number of independent decisions about conflict resolution, and the opportunity for one actor to reconcile all evidence before any rewriting. SD’s source may summarize the entire world in one text, while SI’s three sources each emphasize a subset before synthesis. These differences reach the terminal through the reports. The table lists variation in coverage but understates that the source-level cognitive task itself changes.

Repair. Preserve the separate level-3 arm as a total effect, but name it exactly: independent partial-access observers versus a single union-access source, both followed by matched rewrites and synthesis. Measure assigned and realized per-source coverage, conflicts seen jointly, input length, and information retained. Do not use H4’s success in this arm as evidence of independence beyond the access mechanism. The SI-same/SD-same arm is the intended narrower contrast.

## 12\. SI-same/SD-same plus downstream rewrites: a paired contrast is not actually matched at the input to each downstream node \[MEDIUM-HIGH\]

Difference and path. The table says downstream rewrites are “identical in function, family, and number” across SI and SD. Those statements do not guarantee identical prompt templates or family assignment in matched trials, nor comparable starting synthesis-message lengths or conflict content. The two synthesis outputs differ because of the source-sharing manipulation. Later rewrites can respond differently to length, textual entropy, and uncertainty, and prompt-template randomization can interact with that difference. A decline in cross-sectional AUC may reflect a changed classifier, ceiling effects, or shifts in message scale rather than erosion of inherited information.

Repair. Pair SI and SD trials by world, assigned source package, prompt family, branch-level rewrite templates, and seed schedule, then use each synthesized output as a shared starting record for its own \+1 and \+3 continuations. Estimate the ancestry-by-downstream-depth interaction on prespecified outputs and feature channels. Distinguish an unconditional decline in discriminability from direct evidence that particular inherited deviations disappear. The effect of extra rewriting on detectability is still an operational total effect. H5 should say that rather than presuppose literal signal decay.

## 13\. Study 2 attribution arms: generative concealment and postprocessed stripping do not share the same upstream realizations \[MEDIUM-HIGH\]

Difference and path. The stripping arm is explicitly derived from neutral sets, while generative concealment is a separately generated set of messages. Nothing specifies that its observer/source texts, assigned packages, source prompt variants, or branch seeds are paired with those of the neutral set. A measured difference between generative concealment and postprocessed stripping can therefore reflect independent source-generation noise or a different realized conflict selection, in addition to the concealment instruction. The difference reaches the terminal through the rewrite input and content.

Repair. For the attribution contrast, hold the exact upstream observation texts fixed within a matched set and change only the rewrite’s concealment policy. Derive the stripped text from the matched neutral rewrite. Match branch templates and seeds where meaningful, and analyze the paired outcomes with sets as the dependence unit. Report the effect as content-changing generative concealment versus marker-only removal conditional on the same source texts. This repairs the existing arm rather than adding a new condition.

## 14\. H10 and the stripping arm: “source markers removed without other changes” is not operationally guaranteed \[MEDIUM-HIGH\]

Difference and path. Source labels may be embedded in propositions, quotations, and evidence attributions. Removing a phrase such as “the engineer said” may change sentence boundaries, grammatical content, who is asserted to know the claim, or the proposition matcher’s tokenization. There is also no guarantee that neutral outputs contain source markers at all. A no-op stripping operation cannot test loss of attribution, and a destructive operation can change propositional features. A world-anchored shared-deviation algorithm need not remain unchanged after such transformations.

Repair. Define an explicit, parsable attribution field or bracketed marker whose removal can be verified without changing proposition text, if attribution is genuinely an independent factor. Report marker presence, stripping success, and pre/post propositional annotation equivalence. If ordinary prose attribution is retained instead, H10 must test the net effect of the particular redaction algorithm and cannot assume the anchored method is invariant. H10’s “leaves unchanged” prediction should be contingent on successful semantic preservation of the stripped record.

## 15\. Study 2 H7: pooled marginal-equivalence results can conceal condition-specific leakage \[MEDIUM-HIGH\]

Difference and path. Partial/full access, neutral/generative/stripped attribution, and prompt-family assignment each affect singleton content distributions. A probe pooling across all arms could return chance when cues of opposite sign occur in separate arms. In MIX, the odd branch comprises one of three messages. If its package or prompt sampling differs, the one-random-message mixture may dilute but not eliminate a structural cue. The table’s random slot permutation removes position labels but does not establish conditional marginal exchangeability.

Repair. Predefine singleton probes within access × attribution × prompt-family strata and by graph position before permutation where relevant. Use the same randomly sampled singleton selection rule in training and evaluation. Report both pooled and worst-stratum excess discrimination, with world-grouped uncertainty. A success at H7 means no demonstrated singleton cue of the tested magnitude in the tested strata, not exact equality of marginal distributions.

## 16\. Study 2 H11 and the world-anchored null: conditional independence of errors requires a properly matched reference distribution \[MEDIUM\]

Difference and path. IND messages share world facts, producer weights, genre instructions, and prompt family. Independent calls can independently converge on the same stereotyped error or omission. The table recognizes shared model errors and uses IND as a null, but does not specify whether the null is conditioned on the event, proposition difficulty, source-access package, model family, and attribution arm. Failing to condition can make shared-similarity excess appear to reflect common ancestry when it reflects a world-specific default. The world-free method’s comparison to the anchored score also lacks a scale definition: a simple ratio of AUCs is dominated by their 0.5 common baseline.

Repair. Estimate the independent same-world reference from IND trials in training folds with matching package, genre, model, and attribution conditions, using shrinkage where strata are sparse. Define a baseline-adjusted, uncertainty-bounded estimand for H11, such as the proportion of above-chance discrimination retained, and state what happens if the anchored method has no reliable signal. H11 should not be declared supported merely because an arbitrarily small positive fraction is “measurable.”

## 17\. Method comparisons: “same information” is false for world-anchored or externally calibrated features \[MEDIUM\]

Difference and path. The v2 table says the prompted reader and trained classifier receive the same information, “message text only.” Elsewhere the world-anchored method consumes the true event record and the proposition matcher’s alignment, while the world-free method uses a same-event independently generated reference. A classifier evaluated with these channels is not information-matched to a reader given only terminal messages. Source ground truth, reference corpora, and externally extracted annotations can each produce score differences unrelated to reasoning or model architecture.

Repair. Limit matched-information comparisons to methods given exactly the same text or message set and no world anchor, and report the anchored analysis as a separate upper reference. Record any additional reference data used to compute classifier features. Equating in-context examples with fitted parameters is already correctly declined in v2, but the held-out-world example inventory and source access must also be specified.

## 18\. H6 transfer: prompt-family and producer-family tests can be mixed with world differences \[MEDIUM\]

Difference and path. Every node in a trial draws from one family A or B, which repairs the v1 problem of mixed variants in long chains. However, a training/test arrangement that uses family A on training worlds and family B on different worlds also changes the event population. A change of producer family can introduce different tokenizer, length, and stylistic signatures. Without crossed evaluations, a loss of accuracy may be ascribed to prompts or producer when it also reflects world composition.

Repair. Within outer world-grouped evaluation, make both prompt families available on distinct trials in each training-world block, preserve a held-out template family for transfer, and report the family shift conditional on comparable world folds. For producer transfer, apply both producer models to identical assigned world and package blocks, while keeping producer identity out of test labels. This is the same proposed H6 test with an explicit estimand, not a new study.

## 19\. R1 versus R3 versus R5: one difference is accepted, but its interpretation needs the same discipline \[MEDIUM\]

Difference and path. Longer chains have more independent rewrite draws, more opportunities for template variation, altered intermediate input lengths, and greater selection on successful completions. The difference table lists the first three and treats them as part of the depth procedure, which is defensible. The remaining path is failure and retry behavior: if a long chain is more likely to be restarted, edited, or excluded, the terminal messages may be selected for a different property than short-chain messages. The code audit’s “lineage as gate” reduces silent exclusion but does not define whether a failure reruns the entire chain with new seeds or only the failed node.

Repair. Specify retry/restart policy per node type and compare failure denominators by depth. When retries occur, retain their seed and prompt history. The substantive contrast remains the randomized total effect of adding rewrite stages, not a pure path-length effect invariant to prompt sampling. H2’s nonmonotonic drift wording is already correct.

## 20\. F1: a single-input “synthesis” may be a function-class exception \[MEDIUM\]

Difference and path. Part III defines synthesis as combining several incoming messages, while F1 requires synthesis of a single observation. If the prompt must be reworded when only one input is present, the F1 terminal-generation function is different from F2 and F3, introducing a role/instruction difference. If the identical multi-input instruction is applied to one input, the model may manufacture comparison language or notice that a promised second source is absent. Either route can make F1 unusually easy to distinguish.

Repair. Write and preflight one synthesis instruction that is semantically valid for any k ≥ 1, without instructing the model to compare nonexistent additional sources. Use an input list format whose k varies by the intended manipulation only. If this is not possible without substantially changing the function, drop F1 from H3 and retain F2 versus F3. A contrast against a special one-input prompt cannot be treated as a matched-function fan-in test.

## 21\. Gate 1: code/lineage verification is not the same as verification of original inference \[MEDIUM\]

Mismatch and consequence. Gate 1 combines reproducing the original code/lineage correspondence with checking the new dispatcher. Part IX says a pass means “the original findings are documented as verified.” A lineage audit could confirm that the compound was not composed and that other parent connections exist, yet says nothing about whether the reported classifier fit, calibration, samples, feature extraction, and uncertainty reproduce. It can actually establish that the original causal interpretation was invalid.

Repair. Describe a Gate 1 pass as “implemented lineage documented against code, source-to-record correspondence checked, new dispatcher preflight passed.” Reserve “original numerical results reproduced” for the distinct deposited-data rerun. The main claim should remain “protocol-label accuracy reproduced, structural attribution unsupported,” even if the original count-level statistics match.

## 22\. Gate 4: an H7 or H8 failure does not have the same consequence \[MEDIUM\]

Mismatch and consequence. Gate 4 requires H7 to show no singleton leakage and H8 to show relational gain. If H7 fails but H8 succeeds, the results may still contain useful relational information, although it is contaminated by a singleton cue and cannot support the intended clean attribution. If H7 holds but H8 fails, the predeclared relational method has not demonstrated gain, but that does not prove information-theoretic absence. A small pilot with 10 worlds and two replicates also has inadequate precision to distinguish a modest relation-based gain from zero.

Repair. Separate the two failure branches. H7 failure triggers inspection and correction of the identified marginal leakage before claim-making. H8 failure triggers a statement that the tested relational feature set did not show sufficient pilot gain, with uncertainty, and possible restriction to a boundary claim only if an adequately powered equivalence or upper-bound analysis is later performed. A pilot null alone is not a bound on recoverable information. A Gate 4 pass authorizes additional testing, not a positive conclusion.

## 23\. Gate 3 and H4/H5: a predictive success need not isolate an ancestral mechanism \[MEDIUM\]

Mismatch and consequence. H4 says SI-same and SD-same are distinguishable because independent inputs differ in realization and resolution. But absent controls for source-template draws, a positive result is not attributable to roots. H5 says the SI/SD contrast declines after rewriting, which could arise from changing accuracy ceilings and training sets. Gate 3 treats survival under prompt-family and genre shifts as enough to proceed on a structural claim, but a stable prompt-diversity or length cue can survive both shifts.

Repair. Gate 3 should require the specified difference-table repairs to have passed their own balance checks, and should report robustness to prompt family and genre as a generalization condition rather than an identification condition by itself. H4 estimates input-ancestry assignment effects with the mediator pathways named. H5 tests an interaction in discriminability across downstream depth, not necessarily disappearance of a specific source-inheritance signal.

# B. Checks explicitly requested outside Part IV

## Depth-confound repair: worthwhile, not complete

Rewriting each of three independent observations once before synthesis correctly aligns the length in edges from each observed input root to the synthesis node with the depth of the three SD derivatives. The call counts are correct: SI comprises three observations, three rewrites, one synthesis, or seven calls; SD comprises one observation, three rewrites, one synthesis, or five. Both present three depth-one messages to the same synthesis function. Call count outside the ancestry of the synthesis differs through root multiplicity and is not independently a leakage channel in an isolated dispatcher. The more consequential new difference is that SI has three independently sampled observation prompt variants and SD one, which can generate source-form heterogeneity before the matched rewrite stage. Matching within-trial source and rewrite templates repairs this. A further consequence is that the extra rewriting can induce convergence across SI inputs or divergence across SD descendants, reducing identifiability. That is not an uncontrolled confound once node functions and stochastic policies are matched. It is an empirical mediator and possible null-result mechanism, not a promise of preserved source independence.

## Hypotheses versus targeted contrasts

H1 points to the correct null but needs a canonical seed key, branch-order stress test, and a powered or explicitly limited equivalence criterion. H2 matches R1/R3/R5 if labeled the total depth-procedure effect and evaluated with failure/retry rules. H3 currently tests varying number of independently sourced, partitioned inputs, not fan-in alone. H4’s SI-same target is contaminated by independent source-template draws unless matched; SI-div is correctly placed at level 3 but needs the more explicit access-allocation name. H5 tests an interaction in classifier discriminability, not directly the disappearance of inherited errors. H6 requires crossed world, prompt, and producer comparisons to identify which transfer failed. H7 tests the intended absence of singleton cues only when the package-assignment rule is exchangeable and strata are analyzed separately. H8 tests incremental pairwise predictive information only with a meaningful nonrelational set baseline and a same-event null. H9 is properly labeled a prediction with exceptions. H10 requires successful marker presence and semantic-preserving stripping plus matched source realizations. H11 needs a defined baseline-adjusted performance fraction and sufficient precision.

## Gate consequences and sample-size logic

Gate 1 cannot pronounce original inference verified from lineage correspondence alone. Gate 2 cannot establish a narrow distributional null with 20 observations per arm across ten worlds, although deterministic replay may validate a specific configuration. Gate 3 cannot accept F1/F2/F3 as genre-robust if the pilot does not cross that contrast with genre. A negative result at Gate 3 is not proof of nonidentifiability. Gate 4 must distinguish contamination by singleton cues from failure to detect relational signal, and a pilot failure cannot establish a general bound. Each passing pilot gate should authorize confirmatory work under explicit conditions, not become an empirical conclusion in itself.

The numerical budget is internally consistent, rather than defective. Part V has 17 Study 1 conditions totaling 85 producer calls per world-replication block. Sixty worlds × three replicates gives 3,060 terminal messages and 15,300 producer calls. Two reader evaluations per message give 6,120 reader calls and 53.55 hours at nine seconds per call. For Study 2, IND/DEP/MIX require six, four, and five producer calls per set, respectively. At 60 worlds × three replicates × two access regimes × two generated attribution arms, this is 2,160 generated sets and 10,800 calls. The 1,080 stripped sets are derived and require no producer calls. Two reader evaluations for all 3,240 sets yield 6,480 calls and 43.2 hours. The second Study 1 producer adds 38.25 hours. These sum to 135 hours.

The pilot’s 3,260 producer calls are also reconstructable: 1,700 for 17 Study 1 conditions at ten worlds × two replications, 360 for four second-genre Study 1 conditions (R1, R3, SI-same, SD-same), and 1,200 for 240 generated Study 2 sets. The text should state that decomposition because otherwise “about 420 messages and 240 sets” appears unexplained. The nine-second throughput is an inherited assumption that Part VII correctly flags for pilot measurement, not a discovered arithmetic error. No separate pilot-reader budget is included, as the table openly states.

# C. Contrasts clean as specified, and limits of that judgment

R1 versus R3 versus R5 is defensible as a randomized total effect of the number of rewrite stages under a common rewrite-function prompt family. The extra prompt draws, stochastic transformations, and changes in intermediate input length are constitutive of that operational treatment. They need not be eliminated if the claim stays at that level. Its remaining implementation preconditions are the common source distribution, condition-independent prompt-template assignment, and a documented retry policy.

The structural logic of NC0 versus D0 and NCR1 versus R1 is also correct at the level of the intended causal graph: their terminal ancestries match, and unused siblings should be inert in an isolated system. They are not clean as executable tests until the seed-key and sibling-order specifications are repaired. I do not classify F1/F2/F3, SI-same/SD-same, SI-div/SD-div, downstream decay, or IND/DEP/MIX as clean as currently written, for the specific reasons above. No contrast requires dropping immediately except F1 if a common one-input synthesis function cannot be implemented.

# D. Code checking, provenance and freeze decision

Verified directly in the linked original code: run\_experiment\_paper1.py assigns first\_terminal\_text from the laundering stage at lines 249–253 and calls the second regime with the original world at line 273, without passing that text onward.
