# GPT Critique of Claude Redesign (2026-10-29)

Independent assessment of Claude\_Diagnosis\_and\_Redesign\_2026-10-29.md. Prepared 29 October 2026\. The Task A GPT review was confirmed saved before the Claude redesign was opened. This document applies the scientific-manuscript provisions of Projects/Claude Logistics/writing\_canon.md. It is a critique, not an authorization to implement or modify the original study.

# Decision

Adopt the two-observation-unit architecture from Claude’s proposal, together with its lineage audit, controlled prompt pools, and source-inheritance tests. Do not adopt the design verbatim. Its causal statement about non-ancestors is too broad, its negative-control tolerance is insufficiently justified, and several proposed contrasts identify information diversity or prompt-context differences rather than the named graph property. Study 2 is conceptually better aligned with evidential independence, but matching one rewrite per observed text does not itself prove the absence of single-message cues. The core production-call arithmetic is correct under a fixed nine-second latency assumption, while the extensions are underbudgeted or unspecified. For external validation, prefer a properly licensed lineage dataset whose labels answer the proposed task. Do not treat METER, stemmatological copying, and university press-release coverage as equivalent ground truth.

Claude’s report is also stronger than my Task A assessment because it reports a code-level defect in the compound instantiator and analyzes the existing trial records, whereas my earlier report was bounded to the submitted DOCX and SI. The distinction matters. A compound stage whose output is computed but never passed to the relay stage is a factual implementation failure, not merely an interpretation problem. I inspected Claude’s supplementary analysis scripts and the source-linked review, but not the original local Python instantiator or the JSONL data needed to reproduce their findings. The code-derived values below therefore retain the status of Claude-reported analyses until the underlying code and data are independently rechecked.

# 1\. Where Claude’s diagnosis is stronger

## 1.1 The original implementation and its consequences

Claude reports that the original compound routine generated a laundering output into first\_terminal\_text and then initiated a new chain directly from world facts without using that output. Its analysis script 04\_compound\_message\_flow.py would test this by checking the source node’s parents and whether any laundering text occurs in the later source prompt. The scripts are present and readable in claude\_review\_analysis\_2026-10-29/, but the original run\_experiment\_paper1.py and lineage records were not accessible to me in this review. If the reported evidence is verified, the submitted manuscript’s description of compound as sequential composition is false. Neither the 0% compound accuracy nor the 65/84 predictions of chain relay can establish masking by a later stage, because no informational connection between the stages was implemented (Claude, 2026a, “Two implementation findings”; Gladstone, 2026, Results and Table 1).

Likewise, the claimed collapse of eight named motifs into a smaller number of terminal-ancestry classes should become an explicit structural audit. The relevant equivalence is not nominal motif label but realized causal ancestry of the observed artifact, including its functions and inputs. Claude reports 100% leave-one-world-out discrimination between single direct and independent corroboration despite both having one model call from world facts, and strong prompt-variant prediction within motifs. That directly demonstrates protocol information in the form features if the source analyses are reproduced. The 96.4% result cannot be read as recovery of the independence of unused branches. Claude’s stronger code diagnosis supersedes any proposal, including my earlier one, to retain the compound mechanism story without a lineage correction.

Two qualifications matter. First, the redesign says “In five motifs” and then names four parallel-sibling motifs: independent corroboration, dependent repetition, common-source laundering, and clustered reinforcement. The compound defect represents an additional noncontributing stage, but it is not a fifth instance of the described three-parallel-messages pattern. The count and categories should be corrected. Second, even when compound and a three-hop chain use the same ancestry and templates, calling their outputs “one distribution” presupposes equivalent stochastic call conditions, initialization, and selection. Different random-state histories or hidden contextual state can create distributional differences despite identical explicit prompts. This is precisely what a pipeline audit must test.

## 1.2 Observation units and hypotheses

Claude’s distinction between the observable history of one message and the relations among several observed messages is a substantive improvement. An unobserved sibling cannot be causally informative about a terminal message unless it changes that message’s production or selection. By contrast, the dependence among three observed reports can leave shared textual deviations that provide evidence of common ancestry. My Task A report proposed matched protocols and a negative control but did not specify a separate set-level experiment with pairwise detection as a primary outcome. I would take the two-study architecture, subject to the corrections below (Claude, 2026b, Parts II, IV, and V).

The proposal is also better at identifying testable boundaries: equivalent terminal ancestry as a structural null, attribution concealment crossed with graph configuration, audits for actual parent-text passing, and prompt-variant shifts as an out-of-distribution check. These should remain, although the planned contrasts and criteria need repair. Its world-level cross-validation, raw subgroup counts, and calibration diagnostics are appropriate follow-ups to the reviewer and are better specified than my original brief proposal.

# 2\. Conditional independence and the causal graph

## 2.1 The valid statement is about causal noninfluence, not universal statistical independence

Claude writes that a message “is conditionally independent of every non-ancestral part of the graph, given those ancestors.” As written, this is false without additional restrictions. In a structural causal model, a terminal output Y generated as Y \= f(Pa(Y), UY) cannot be changed by an intervention confined to nodes outside An(Y), if the graph is acyclic, structural functions and exogenous state remain fixed, and there is no unmodeled channel into Y. This is an absence of directed causal influence from those nodes to Y. It does not imply statistical independence between Y and every non-ancestor variable. Descendants of Y are non-ancestors yet may depend strongly on Y. Siblings can remain associated through shared unconditioned causes, and dependence can enter through common random states, model sessions, or selection.

If N denotes the outputs of sibling branches, A the realized preterminal ancestors and their inputs, and W the world, a restricted conditional independence of Y and N given A and W requires independent terminal and sibling disturbances, invariant prompts and sampling policies, and no shared omitted generator state. Conditioning on observed ancestral texts alone may not suffice. A terminal text may also predict the existence of siblings in an observational dataset when graph type determines the terminal node’s role prompt, even though intervening to remove unused siblings would leave an otherwise matched terminal generation unchanged. Prediction of a correlated graph label and causal influence of that graph component are distinct targets.

## 2.2 Plausible violations in implemented pipelines

Relevant channels include persistent conversational context across model calls, prompt builders that condition on total graph size or role, shared random-number generators whose state advances as unused siblings are sampled, batch-level scheduling, caching or mutable tool state, context-length truncation, resource contention that changes decoding, output selection that depends on the other branches, and evaluation that conditions on acceptance or length after all branches are produced. A “randomly selected” terminal is nonadaptive only if the selection distribution is independent of sibling texts and any downstream filters. Shared weights and a common world can create similar errors across independently called observers even when no message is passed between them. That dependence is common-cause dependence, not shared message ancestry.

The correct preanalysis statement is narrower: under specified isolation, prompt, and selection conditions, intervening on non-ancestral siblings cannot affect the distribution of the terminal output. This should be a falsifiable property of the particular producer dispatcher, not a declaration based only on the diagram. For negative controls, log an immutable call-state manifest: model and quantization hashes, temperature and seed policy, prompts, world input, node order, parent-text hashes, conversation/session IDs, batch settings, terminal-selection index, retries, and rejection rules. Test both a deliberately paired terminal draw and an independently resampled draw. Compare their conditional feature distributions and classification results.

## 2.3 Identifiability within ancestry is not guaranteed

Claude is correct to bound possible single-message information by the realized ancestors, but the phrase “only properties of the ancestral subgraph are identifiable” can suggest the converse, which is not true. Ancestral depth, fan-in, and root multiplicity may be observationally equivalent after transformations erase distinctions. A classification estimate is not identification unless the mapping from target property to the distribution of observables is sufficiently discriminating under controlled nuisance factors. The redesign should state equivalence classes explicitly: which graph changes have zero effect by construction, which change ancestral inputs but are empirically indistinguishable, and which generate discriminable distributions under stated prompts and world populations.

# 3\. Negative controls and equivalence testing

NC-IND versus D0 and NC-DEP versus C1 are well-chosen graph interventions if their terminal-generating ancestries, prompts, selection rules, and model state are actually matched. The matched-protocol claim requires each control pair to have an exchangeable terminal observation. It is not enough that the two conditions have the same count of ancestral calls. A three-branch condition that uses extra calls before drawing the terminal may consume a shared random stream differently from a one-branch condition. Define terminal selection before generation, or run selected terminal branches with independent per-call seeds and immutable context. Randomize graph labels and execution order, and use a paired world/prompt/seed design when possible.

The proposed H1 equivalence rule requires the world-clustered 90% interval for binary AUC to lie within 0.45 to 0.55. A two-one-sided-tests logic is appropriate for equivalence, and AUC is a coherent discrimination target. The ±0.05 tolerance, however, is not justified by an effect-size argument. An AUC of 0.55 is still exploitable discrimination in a sufficiently large corpus. A single benchmark should report both the point estimate and an interval, predeclare the largest substantively negligible signal, and include a power calculation with world clustering and repeated generations. The procedure may either fail to establish equivalence despite an isolated pipeline or pass while allowing leakage that matters downstream. These are different errors.

A more informative primary negative-control estimand is the paired change in a prespecified feature vector and terminal-distribution behavior under addition of non-ancestral branches, together with a world-held-out discriminative probe. Use equivalence margins tied to the detection effect the substantive claims require, not an arbitrary AUC interval. Report multiple probes, including original 14 features, length, textual embeddings, and a flexible classifier as a stress test. Keep training/test separation by world and prompt variant. Because a failed equivalence test can reflect limited precision, it should lead to diagnosis or more precision, not automatically to a conclusion that the pipeline leaks. Conversely, a successful test cannot establish the universal absence of leakage.

Do not automatically exclude lineage failures. A broken parent-text assertion is a study-validity failure to be reported and repaired before confirmatory collection. Excluding failed trials after seeing output text can create selection bias correlated with graph complexity or output form. State preflight audit gates, rerun policies, and the complete failure denominators. Analyze an intention-to-generate set and a valid-realization set where appropriate. Preregister confirmatory rules only after a pilot resolves the engineering assumptions. If an initial preregistration is written before the pilot, its amendments must be versioned and all pilot runs withheld from final testing.

# 4\. Study 1: strengths, unresolved confounds, and corrected contrasts

## 4.1 Depth and fan-in

The depth series D0, C1, C2, C3, and C5 is interpretable if terminal functions and instruction pools do not vary with depth and the relay prompt receives precisely one parent. Even then, increasing depth changes both the number of transformations and accumulated model-decoding opportunities. That is an expected part of the operational mechanism, not an independent causal effect of abstract path length. Proposition drift need not increase monotonically. Intermediate rewrites can delete a false addition, compress inconsistent statements, or return to canonical wording. Treat monotonic drift as a hypothesis with possible plateaus and reversals.

H3 proposes distinguishing synthesis from chain structures of equal call count. Equal total calls does not match total information entering the terminal, number of direct observations, token context, or the terminal-node function. A synthesis node necessarily receives several inputs while a relay receives one, so a discriminable difference could be induced by input volume and the synthesize instruction. If the target is fan-in as implemented, these factors are mediators of the graph’s effect, not contamination, but the result must be called recoverability of fan-in under this family of node functions rather than abstract topology alone. Add controls that concatenate independent inputs under a neutral instruction, match total input-token budgets, or vary fan-in with the same terminal synthesis function using padded or redundant inputs. Each control isolates a different component and should be selected only if it answers a stated mechanism question.

## 4.2 Input independence is confounded with input diversity

SI3 versus SD3 compares three independently sampled observation records with three rewrites of one observation. Under 70% proposition sampling and independent conflict resolution, the former differs in information coverage, sampling variability, and frequency of opposing claims. This may be the mechanism of interest, but it is not a clean effect of independence as such. An independent set can share exactly the same fact subset, and descendants of one ancestor can diverge through stochastic rewriting. Measure the actual overlap of inputs, the number of distinct roots, and the number of distinct propositions before synthesis. Cross ancestry with controlled information overlap wherever feasible. At minimum, stratify or standardize on input diversity and report the residual ancestry contrast separately from the total designed contrast. Do not call the latter recovery of source independence without naming its mediated content differences.

The same issue constrains H4’s interpretation. If independent inputs contain mutually contradictory claims by construction, the terminal may identify diversity, not a lineage property. That is still an informative generative mechanism for why some dependence graphs become observable, but it should be distinguished from a topology-invariant signature. For H5, use paired sequences with matched initial synthesis outputs and fixed relay prompts so that changes in recoverability across depth can plausibly be attributed to additional rewriting. Otherwise decay is mixed with initial sampling variation.

## 4.3 Circulation and prompt transfer

CIRC is not cleanly part of the same design. Each new message receives two other nodes’ previous messages, which makes its terminal function a two-input recombination rather than the one-input rewrite assigned elsewhere. After unrolling time, CIRC is an acyclic graph with recurrently generated parent relations. It differs from chain conditions in fan-in, the number of active nodes, content exposure, depth, and temporal interaction. A standalone circulation arm is reasonable, but it cannot isolate a circulation-specific effect without a matched two-input noncirculating control. Keep it exploratory or omit it from the initial confirmatory paper.

H6 says a classifier trained on prompt variants 1 and 2 will be evaluated on variants 3 and 4, but prompt variants are sampled independently at every node. A long chain may contain both variant pools, so the split is undefined unless every node in a trial is assigned to the same training or holdout prompt family. Specify a trial-level assignment rule and hold out entire prompt templates, worlds, and producer families without constructing inadvertently overlapping training and test chains. The secondary producer is a transfer test of the complete generation regime, not just classifier performance, and its prompts may need separate semantic fidelity checks.

The instruction “continuous prose, 5 to 8 sentences” cannot also be a universally matched instruction for a short social-media post if genre is crossed later. Within each genre, output constraints should be the same across topologies. Across genres, length and format are deliberately changed and must be treated as separate randomized factors. Template families must stay semantically matched across graph conditions, not merely superficially uniform.

# 5\. Study 2: do the conditions remove single-message cues?

The claim that “no single-message cue can separate the structures” is plausible under an exchangeability theorem, not guaranteed by requiring one rewrite. For each observation drawn uniformly from a set, IND, DEP, and MIX can have the same marginal text distribution if source observations are drawn from the same conditional distribution given world and access regime, rewrite prompts and decoding are identically distributed, branch position is exchangeable, and the producer/selection pipeline exposes no graph label. DEP then creates correlation among the three outputs by sharing an ancestor, while IND does not. MIX creates dependence in one pair. The evidence lives in the joint distribution, as intended.

The actual design needs to establish these premises. The same world is reused, and full versus partial observer access changes the distribution of source messages. Concealment instructions may alter content, length, or phrasing, especially if they mention multiple apparent sources. The MIX condition can create an ordering cue if its independent message always occupies a specified slot. If branch-indexed node prompts, source identifiers, shared context, generated name references, or differential retry rules vary by structure, a single text can leak its class. The proper falsification test is a blind one-message classifier on randomly sampled members of each set, evaluated separately within access and concealment cells and under world and prompt holdouts. Report the single-message AUC/confusion matrix before interpreting set-level gains. Ensure that branch ordering is randomly permuted and hidden from both methods.

A stronger analysis is incremental information: compare the set classifier with a classifier that aggregates only the three independent single-message scores, and then add relational features. If the latter improve prediction under held-out worlds, the gain can be attributed to information in relationships among messages under the implementation. Do not equate a set-level classification result automatically with detection of shared ancestry. Distinguish pairwise dependence labels, set-level topology labels, and the presence of shared content attributable to the event itself. Three messages generated with the same producer model can agree on characteristic errors without a common message ancestor.

The world-anchored shared-deviation baseline is useful, but “excess over independence” needs an estimated null conditional on world, proposition difficulty, model, prompt family, and observation-access regime. Independent model calls may make the same stereotyped error. The world-free version needs its own same-event independent baseline trained without access to held-out worlds. Otherwise “excess similarity” can silently encode knowledge of the test-world distribution. Pairwise records are dependent within triads, trial replicates, and worlds. World-level clustering is necessary, but a simple mixed model with only one world random intercept may underrepresent the dependence induced by set and branch reuse.

H8 and H9 are too deterministic. Full access can reduce information diversity, but independent observers may still differ in interpretation. Partial access can produce low overlap even among outputs with shared ancestry after stochastic rewriting. Concealment can suppress source markers, yet may also cause paraphrase, remove quotes, or change claim selection, thus altering shared-idiosyncrasy measures. H9’s asserted invariance of inheritance-based detection under concealment must be treated as an empirical hypothesis and tested for indirect effects on content. An attribution-removal postprocessing control, which strips source markers without rewriting factual content, would isolate the effect of losing attribution information more cleanly than a generative concealment prompt alone.

# 6\. Measurement, inference, and scale

## 6.1 Measurement should follow the actual information channels

Replacing the old semantic/formal/trace taxonomy with formal, attributional, propositional, and relational measurements is more coherent for the redesigned question. However, the four groups are not mutually exclusive latent constructs. Hedges and evidential terms can encode epistemic meaning, not simply attribution. Named entities may be part of propositional content. Relational shared-deviation scores combine paired semantic comparison and a baseline model. Describe them as transparent feature families with operational definitions, and test incremental predictive value and robustness, rather than claiming clean construct separation from the naming itself. The proposed external sentence-embedding reference is useful, but it should not become a dominant comparator that substitutes another representation for the construct test.

Proposition preservation of approximately 0.96 in the original run is a reason to consider richer worlds and more informative deviations, but the new 20-proposition worlds must avoid manufacturing divergence solely to make inference easy. Distinguish natural sampling/rewriting variability from injected marker-like errors. When numeric details are used, predefine whether approximate paraphrases count as preservation, qualification, alteration, or error. The matcher must be validated on human-labeled cases sampled across all graph and genre conditions, otherwise classifier performance can inherit the matcher’s biases.

## 6.2 World grouping and inferential targets

Sixty synthetically constructed worlds provide more independent event units than four held-out worlds, and 10-fold world-grouped cross-validation is a useful stability design. Yet the worlds are drawn under the same schema and may share a world-generator style, so uncertainty is conditional on that population and producer configuration. Four replicates per Study 1 cell and three per Study 2 cell are precision choices rather than evidence-independent guarantees. Use pilot-estimated variance components to simulate precision or power for the named contrasts, explicitly including worlds, condition-by-world interactions, prompt variants, and within-world generation. If world folds are used both for selecting models and reporting outcomes, use nested world-grouped selection or a final untouched world set.

World-clustered bootstrap intervals from out-of-fold predictions describe variability among the sampled worlds under one training procedure. They do not automatically include variability from model selection, refitting, or new producer families. Specify whether the estimand is fixed-trained-model prediction on new worlds or performance of a learning pipeline retrained on new worlds. A single 10-fold cross-validation estimate answers the latter only approximately. Pairwise dependence outcomes also require uncertainty accounting for shared sets and repeated pairwise edges. Report conditional sample sizes by world, genre, access, and ancestry category.

## 6.3 Recomputing the planning estimates

The arithmetic underlying the main budget is correct if one producer call, one reader call, and feature extraction collectively average nine seconds per call equivalent. Study 1 has 60 worlds × 15 conditions × 4 replications \= 3,600 terminal cases. Counting D0 \= 1; C1/C2/C3/C5 \= 2/3/4/6; SI2/SI3 \= 3/4; SD3 \= 5; SI3-R1/SD3-R1 \= 5/6; SI3-R3/SD3-R3 \= 7/8; CIRC \= 9; NC-IND/NC-DEP \= 3/4 gives exactly 70 producer calls per world-replicate and 16,800 total. Its 14,400 reader calls imply 4 reader evaluations per terminal case: two reader models crossed with zero-shot/few-shot. Study 2 has 60 × (3 structures × 2 access regimes × 2 attribution regimes) × 3 replicates \= 2,160 sets. IND, DEP, and MIX require 6, 4, and 5 producer calls per set respectively, yielding 10,800 calls. Its 4,320 reader calls imply 2 evaluations per set, and that asymmetry with Study 1 is not explained.

At nine seconds each, (16,800 \+ 14,400) × 9 / 3,600 \= 78.0 hours; 16,800 × 9 / 3,600 \= 42.0 hours for the second Study 1 producer; (10,800 \+ 4,320) × 9 / 3,600 \= 37.8 hours for Study 2\. Total serial time is 157.8 hours, or 6.58 continuous days. These values check exactly. They are not an empirically supported schedule for the redesigned conditions. The nine-second average came from a different prompt/context distribution, and local hardware throughput varies by model, quantization, input length, output length, batching, concurrency, cache behavior, generation failures, and retries. Study 1’s nine-call circulation and multi-input synthesis may run substantially slower than simple one-call observation. The second producer family also need not share the original model’s throughput.

The extension costs are material. Tripling all three budget lines for three genres would imply approximately 473.4 hours, or 19.7 continuous days, before any random-graph sweep, although reducing replicate counts would lower that total. “200 graphs per world block” does not define whether 200 means overall or per world. If it means 200 graphs for each of 60 worlds with four replications, the sweep adds 48,000 terminal cases. At five producer calls each and nine seconds per call, this alone adds 600 hours, or 25 additional days, before readers and second-family generation. At ten calls it adds 1,200 hours, or 50 days. If the 200 graphs are a global sample reused across worlds, state that precisely and budget the realized call-count distribution. The claim that the full extensions remain a matter of “weeks, not months” is therefore unsupported without deciding the sweep size, reuse, replication, and genre crossing.

Replace the fixed nine-second estimate with a pilot log of input tokens, output tokens, per-stage latency, calls per case, failed calls, and wall-clock utilization. Budget each node function and producer family separately. Distinguish total compute-hours, wall-clock hours under feasible concurrency, and human time needed for world review, annotation, validation, and writing. “No API cost” is accurate only as a billing statement for local inference, not as zero computational or labor cost.

# 7\. Field-anchor corpora and citations

## 7.1 METER: valid resource, imperfect label for the proposed set task

The METER corpus is documented by Gaizauskas et al. (2001) and Clough et al. (2002). Its source documents are Press Association newswire stories and its candidate newspaper stories concern the same events. Global labels indicate wholly derived, partially derived, or non-derived relative to the Press Association source. Those labels are appropriate for a PA-source-versus-newspaper reuse task. They do not establish which newspaper articles share a different common ancestor, whether two non-derived articles are independent of each other, or whether a partly derived article also used other sources. “Non-derived from PA” is not “independent of all other reports.” METER can support paired reuse validation and perhaps event-based sets with additional independently verified source labels, but it is not a ready-made IND/DEP/MIX ground truth.

I verified the 2001 conference citation and the 2002 journalistic text-reuse research descriptions. The original METER paper says the resource was available free for research, but I could not establish a presently functioning authorized download of the full corpus or an explicit redistribution license. Access and permitted processing must be checked with the rights holder before the dataset is promised as a field anchor. Also, testing a synthetic-trained world-free model “without retraining” is a severe cross-genre and cross-generator stress test, not the appropriate sole measure of method validity. Report zero-adaptation transfer if feasible, then compare transparent field calibration or retraining in a strictly separated evaluation design.

## 7.2 Artificial manuscript traditions: strongest ancestry anchor, restricted reuse

The Heinrichi and Notre Besoin artificial traditions exist. Roos and Heikkilä (2009) evaluated methods with known copying histories, and the Computer-Assisted Stemmatology Challenge displays hand-copied witnesses, a correct-stemma resource, and downloads (Computer-Assisted Stemmatology Challenge, n.d.). This is the closest of Claude’s three suggestions to known source ancestry across sets of human-produced texts. It is not a naturally occurring communication field study, and its texts, copied by scribes under an experiment, differ from short reports of current events. Consequently, it is best described as independent human-production validation, not field generalization to news communication.

The challenge’s public page states that its datasets were supplied for challenge use and that further use requires explicit permission from organizers and original providers. Downloadability does not establish permission for this study. The same issue applies to the Notre Besoin material. Verify permissions for full texts and ground-truth structures before extracting features. If permission is obtained, specify whether the evaluation is pairwise common-ancestor detection, branch-distance estimation, or full stemma reconstruction. A tree dataset may not directly instantiate the three-class IND/DEP/MIX task without additional label construction.

## 7.3 Sumner et al. (2014): verified citation, weaker lineage label

Sumner et al. (2014, BMJ, doi:10.1136/bmj.g7015) examined 462 university press releases and 668 associated news articles, linked to peer-reviewed studies, and coded exaggerations in scientific claims. The paper and publication details are verified. It establishes a dataset for comparing claims across research papers, releases, and coverage. It does not generally prove that every news article directly copied the press release, or that two news reports lacking the same exaggeration were independent. Its inferential target is provenance and transmission of particular claims under observed associations, not exact communication-graph ancestry. It can test claim-mutation measures, but would require new human annotation or document-specific sourcing to test Study 2’s pairwise independence target. The full-text reuse rights and availability of all original articles were not established in this review.

For a field-anchor strategy, first define the target label, then choose the corpus. If the objective is known human-copying lineage, pursue permissioned Heinrichi or equivalent controlled-transmission data. If the objective is source-derived journalism, use METER for its actual PA-to-newspaper reuse labels and do not silently relabel them as inter-newspaper independence. If the objective is persistence of a distorted proposition across media, use the Sumner material with the existing annotation ontology. These are three distinct validity checks, not three interchangeable replications of the same estimand.

# 8\. Where Claude is weaker than my proposal and what to retain

Claude supplies a larger and more coherent program, but loses the economy of a deliberately minimal falsification sequence. My Task A recommendation began with a single terminal-branch negative control, followed by one topology-by-role/genre crossing and a small number of world-level audits. That sequence remains preferable before committing to 60 worlds, 15 plus 12 conditions, two producer families, three genres, and a random-graph extension. The first gate should answer whether nonancestral siblings affect terminal outputs under rigorously matched call conditions. The second should demonstrate whether at least one ancestral graph contrast survives a prompt/genre inversion. The third should test whether a three-message set yields relational information beyond single-message summaries. Only then is a larger factorial design justified.

Claude is stronger in specifying the mathematical distinction between one observed output and a set, its actual message-ancestry audit, the inclusion of a source-sharing mixed condition, and the observation-access manipulation. Those elements should be adopted. I would revise the design in this order: (1) independently verify the compound defect and the five reported ancestry classes against source code and one full lineage record per motif; (2) preflight immutable call manifests and paired negative controls; (3) pilot Study 1 with D0, C1/C3, SI3/SD3, NC-IND/NC-DEP, and a crossed genre/prompt variant; (4) pilot Study 2 with IND/DEP/MIX, single-message leakage probe, and relational incremental-value test; (5) estimate world- and trial-level variance, then set equivalence margins and replication; (6) expand to the full design only after design gates pass. Do not claim in advance that a given branch structure will be identifiable.

A useful conceptual refinement is to report a hierarchy of recoverability: graph interventions outside terminal ancestry, interventions that change ancestry but not content diversity, interventions that change information diversity and therefore terminal content, and lineage information that emerges only from comparing multiple outputs. This avoids treating topology as an undifferentiated object and makes the negative results theoretically interpretable. It also separates a mechanism that changes observable messages from a graph label inferred through unrelated protocol conventions.

I would keep Claude’s TMLR option as a credible venue if the revised contribution concerns learnability and identifiability in language-model call graphs, paired with generalization across prompt pools and producer families. TMLR’s published acceptance criteria emphasize supported claims and relevance to a portion of the machine-learning audience rather than novel algorithms alone (Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2026). A paper focused on evidential dependence in human communication would instead be reviewed more naturally by network and communication scholars. Venue should follow the strongest completed discriminating result, not decide the experiment in advance.
