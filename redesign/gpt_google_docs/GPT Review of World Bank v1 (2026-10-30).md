# GPT Review of World Bank v1 (2026-10-30)

# Review basis and decision

Sources examined: GPT\_Brief\_World\_Bank\_Review\_2026-10-30.md, Worlds\_v1\_Review\_2026-10-30.md, the complete 70-record build/worlds/bank.jsonl, and DRAFT\_PROMPT in build/residue/worldgen.py. I examined every world’s core, both declared conflict pairs, source inventory, and proposition text, including consistency of facts outside the declared conflicts. The automatic checks verify only the shape of the data, numeric content, source inequality within each declared pair, and exact duplicate texts. They do not establish that two claims in a pair are about the same fact, that neither is contradicted by an unpaired fact, or that a source could know what it purports to report.

Decision: REDRAFT WITH A CHANGED PROMPT AND RECHECK. The existing bank should not enter generation unchanged. Nine worlds have objectively misassigned conflict-pair labels, several other pairs compare different denominators or nonexclusive mechanisms, and independent nonconflict propositions sometimes adjudicate a supposedly unresolved alternative. The drafting instruction requests four to six distinct in-world sources, but fifty worlds exceed six, with as many as eleven. Every core is p00 and many event types repeat the same cause-versus-count dispute, sometimes with recurring counts such as eleven versus fourteen. A few targeted edits would repair the most serious examples, but the repetition and source-count discrepancy are generated by the drafting procedure itself. A changed prompt with a source-count check and a semantic conflict check is cheaper and more defensible than manually rewriting many records. Section IV provides exact revised DRAFT\_PROMPT text. Section II provides direct proposition-level repairs for every identified substantive defect so that a targeted-repair alternative is available if redrafting is inconvenient.

# I. Answers to the five questions

1\. Are both pairs genuinely incompatible and balanced?

No. The current automatic validator accepts any two propositions sharing a numeric conflict\_pair label if their source strings differ. It does not check semantic alignment. In W006, W008, W014, W022, W030, W031, W039, W049, and W069, a purported conflict pair contains a cause proposition and an unrelated count or outcome proposition. These are unambiguous coding errors, not close judgments. Other pairs compare a population at an alarm with a later assembly count, two different populations of affected objects, or a physical vulnerability with an initiating event that could coexist. Nonconflict propositions favor one alternative in W002, W003, W004, W049, W057, and W059. Different speakers making contradictory claims are not themselves contradictory facts: the analysis needs the underlying contested propositions, not just the true facts that two people disagreed.

2\. Are facts sufficiently variable in salience and retention probability?

Partly. The worlds contain a useful mixture of central events, times, instrument readings, operational details, safety consequences, and peripheral facts such as maintenance dates. But all seventy have their single core at p00, a full sentence with a precise time, and twenty total propositions. A reader will almost always preserve that core. Similar response schedules, inspection facts, and attendance counts recur across worlds. This is acceptable as some shared task structure, but it limits variation in what observers omit and can yield mostly easy core preservation plus generic omission of peripheral material. More serious are unpaired facts that reveal which side of a conflict is correct. These eliminate the intended opportunity for independent observers to resolve the same uncertainty differently. Cheapest repair: keep the twenty-fact schema, vary the position of the core in the rendered package with a condition-blind shuffled order, and require in the drafting prompt that no unpaired fact resolve either disputed claim.

3\. Can the classifier learn world quirks instead of ancestry?

Within the intended world-grouped folds, the same world cannot appear in both training and held-out evaluation. Thus rote memorization of an individual world cannot directly explain out-of-fold structure accuracy. The broader danger is world-generation regularity shared across folds. For example, every world has exactly two conflicts, one often a cause and one a headcount or damaged-unit count. Several consecutive worlds use eleven/fourteen or similar small integer contrasts. Source counts differ by event type, and source inventories in some worlds are dominated by data records whereas others have many named witnesses. This can influence the observable preservation, omission, and attribution patterns and make a predictor learn the drafting family’s dispute grammar rather than robust effects of ancestry. Because every structure is generated within each world, these world properties are not themselves direct condition labels. They instead moderate signal strength and can create model-by-event-type interactions. Cheapest repair: demand diverse dispute types and counts across the five siblings of each event type, enforce proper conflict alignments, avoid identical count templates, and report performance by event type and source/quantity density. Do not change the world-grouped evaluation.

4\. Real organizations, places, persons, or refusal-prone content?

The events are presented as fictional and the locations and institutions are largely constructed names (Harrow Basin, Calder Reach, Pellwick, etc.). I did not identify an unambiguous real institution, identifiable real individual, or actual incident from the provided records. Some given and family names may coincide with those of real people, which cannot be ruled out by reading alone. There is no reason to assert that these are real referents. Gas leaks, fires, derailments, patient incidents, chemicals, and food contamination are ordinary event-reporting subjects rather than requests for harmful instructions. A refusal is possible with any provider, but no bank item obviously requires one. The drafting log’s abandoned “laboratory exposure” type indicates sensitivity to the model’s chosen wording rather than a defect in the accepted airport-baggage replacement. Use clearly invented organizations and place names, avoid famous or uniquely identifying proper nouns, and retain the ordinary incident-report framing.

5\. Are sources plausible, diverse, and informative?

Many are plausible, and having a mixture of direct observers, logs, instruments, and later inspection reports is useful for inherited attribution analysis. Several assignments are not credible: an engine-temperature gauge is made to report fuel level (W000 p14); a ticketing manifest reports injuries (W044 p09) or line-closure hours (W042 p19); a shift roster reports a manager’s arrival (W067 p11); a floor leak sensor reports room air temperature (W067 p10); a building-management alarm log measures public web-service downtime (W069 p18); a building plan states how long a mezzanine was closed (W014 p19). These are not neutral noise. They could cause producers to discount certain facts or invent plausible replacement sources, obscuring inheritance. Moreover the drafting prompt specifies four to six distinct source identities, yet only twenty of the seventy worlds have six or fewer. The cheapest consistent repair is to ask the drafter to define one explicit 4–6-name source inventory before the propositions and assign every proposition to a source that could observe or document it, with the program validating source count and membership. The study’s source-inventory model and in-world attribution field already support this. This is a worldgen quality change, not a change to any structure or analysis.

# III. Cross-world patterns and prediction implications

The bank's strongest recurring patterns are visible without training a classifier. All 70 worlds contain exactly 20 facts, the core is p00 in all 70, and all 70 have two labeled conflict pairs. The uniform 20-fact design is intentional and useful for matched structural comparisons, but p00's constant role and the routine use of a causal-dispute-plus-count-dispute template are accidental drafting artifacts. For example, eleven-versus-fourteen worker or vehicle counts recur across warehouse fires, chemical spills, bridges, rail, hospital and other event types. Distinct worlds also reuse fictional geographical stems such as Harrow, Calder, and Marrowfield. These motifs can generalize across training and test worlds as properties of the \*world generator\*. They cannot by themselves identify the structure when each world appears in every structure, but may amplify or attenuate particular trace features and make generalization to differently written events worse.

Source inventory size is also heterogeneous in a patterned way. The requested four-to-six range is violated in 50 of 70\. W009 and W014 use eleven sources each. Event types sometimes rely heavily on institutional logs and sensors and sometimes on actor statements. That variation is potentially useful, but the Sources line and token pressure can behave differently with eleven sources versus five, and sources that cannot directly know their facts invite hallucinated repair. The minimum fix is an enforceable closed source inventory and credible source-fact pairing. Keep a distributional report of source count, named-actor proportion, numeric-fact proportion, and world/event type. The fact that each world receives all conditions means these are world-level moderators rather than direct condition labels.

There is no evidence of a systematic one-to-one link between a particular world and a generating condition in the bank itself. The principal threat of “learning worlds” would occur only if the builder accidentally placed descendants of one world in different training and test folds or included a world/source ID in text-only features. The existing world-grouped folds and feature-access registry are the appropriate safeguards. The bank problem is different: overly formulaic conflict types and mistaken labels can prevent the intended \*causal contrast\* from being a meaningful test of inherited conflict handling, even with perfect world-level splitting.

# IV. Exact replacement for worldgen.py DRAFT\_PROMPT

Replace the current DRAFT\_PROMPT string with the following complete string. Keep the existing event\_type and siblings substitution fields and JSON keys, so parse\_world and the rest of the pipeline need no change to data shape.

DRAFT\_PROMPT \= """Write one fictional event record as JSON for a controlled study of how information changes as reports are passed between writers.

Event type: {event\_type}. Make this event distinct from these already drafted events of the same type: {siblings}. Change the setting, immediate circumstances, affected objects, event details, and disputed quantities. Do not repeatedly use the same types of disagreements or numbers.

Return ONLY one JSON object with:

\- "event": a one-sentence description of the central event.

\- "propositions": exactly 20 objects, each with "text" (one standalone factual sentence), "source" (one source name), "core" (true for exactly one central-event proposition and false otherwise), and "conflict" (0 for exactly two propositions, 1 for exactly two others, and null for all remaining propositions).

Requirements:

1\. The single core proposition states the central event and its time, without declaring which disputed cause, count, or explanation is correct. Do not always put the core proposition first.

2\. Use exactly FOUR TO SIX distinct, explicitly named in-world sources across the entire record. Choose this closed set before writing the facts. Every "source" field must exactly match one of these names. Mix appropriate human observations, institutional records, and instruments. Each source must plausibly know or document the fact attributed to it. A sensor cannot independently establish a causal diagnosis, a roster cannot document an arrival, and a ticket manifest cannot establish injuries or repair durations.

3\. Create exactly TWO genuinely incompatible pairs. Each pair has exactly two members, with different source names. Both members must concern the SAME referent, measurement definition, population, place, and time or reporting window, and they must give incompatible substantive claims. A careful writer cannot report both underlying claims as true. For example, two exact counts of the same people present at the same moment can disagree. Do NOT contrast people at an alarm with people later at an assembly point, total system inventory with items in one queue, or two causes that can operate together. If contrasting causal explanations, make them explicitly mutually exclusive competing sole causes.

4\. Do not place any other proposition, including the core, that confirms one disputed claim or makes the other impossible. Background evidence may be informative but must not conclusively adjudicate either pair.

5\. Include several numeric details and at least four propositions containing numbers, times, durations, or measurements. Mix highly salient, moderately useful, and peripheral but relevant facts. The nonconflict facts should be distinct and informative, not repetitive boilerplate or paraphrases of one another.

6\. Keep the chronology, quantities, locations, and source claims internally consistent except for the two deliberately disputed pairs. Use plausible times, measurements, personnel roles, and operational actions. The facts should be usable in an incident report without special instructions, redactions, or hidden assumptions.

7\. Use fictional institution, person, and place names and no identifiable actual incident. Do not include instructions for causing harm. Ordinary fictional reporting about accidents, outages, failures, illness, or releases is allowed.

8\. Return only JSON. Do not add commentary, markdown fences, or fields not listed above."""

The requested changes are to the generation prompt and quality checks for world content. They do not alter the number of worlds, event types, proposition count, two-conflict structure, source matching objective, or any communication topology.

# V. Minimum acceptance checks on a regenerated bank

The existing exact schema checks should remain. Add an exact source-inventory count of four through six and case-normalized source string equality, without making two different witnesses accidentally count as one. Explicitly test that each conflict label occurs exactly twice and that the paired facts are about the same referent and measurement window, with pairwise semantic review before accepting the bank. Test no unpaired proposition conclusively resolves a pair. For sources, check whether the claimed reporter could have observed the fact. These semantic tests cannot reliably be replaced with counting digits or exact string comparisons. A human reviewer can inspect the four dispute propositions and their relevant contextual facts for each of 70 worlds, which is a bounded task using the existing review format.

After regeneration, show a compact diagnostic by world: its two opposing statements and their source names, the 4–6-source inventory, core statement, and any external facts plausibly settling either dispute. Report each event type's source-count distribution and types of disagreements, especially the frequency of two exact count disputes or cause/count repetition. Keep verification worlds identified as in the current allocation only if their world IDs are retained, otherwise maintain the intended ten verification IDs through the existing assignment system. Do not pass a regeneration merely because all JSON fields parse.

# VI. Decision and scope

The current bank is not suitable as written. The documented mechanical pair errors alone would contaminate matcher training and inherited-deviation analyses. The recurring ambiguities and source-inventory violations make extensive manual editing unattractive. Redraft with the exact revised prompt, then inspect the regenerated records using the substantive tests above. If regeneration is not possible, the explicit fixes in Section II and an audited source-inventory pass provide a repair route, but that path demands more manual judgment and leaves the formulaic drafting patterns largely intact.

This recommendation is about the quality of the source events, not about preregistration, a change in the study's structures, or a new theoretical claim. It protects the contrast between input-independent versus shared ancestry by ensuring that observers encounter genuine unresolved disagreements and that source attribution can be inherited or lost coherently.
