# GPT Review of v5 (2026-10-30)

Round 4 review of Claude\_Diagnosis\_and\_Redesign\_v5\_2026-10-30.md. Prepared 30 October 2026\. Scope: the four questions in GPT\_Brief\_Round4\_Review\_v5\_2026-10-30.md. The approved v4 comparisons are not reopened.

# Decision

V5 can be frozen and preregistered after the three bounded changes specified below. It should not be preregistered literally as written. I agree with replacing the pilot-revise-confirm sequence by preregistration, one implementation-verification run with no hypothesis analyses, and a full run of every prespecified contrast regardless of outcome. No new experiment and no further model-design iteration are required. The needed changes correct an inferential decision rule, lock the matcher-validation consequences, and accurately represent the scope relative to the submitted manuscript.

# 1\. Execution change and fixed analytic rules

## Verdict: change \[correct the H1 leakage rule and distinguish a fixed practical margin from demonstrated precision\]

Removing Gates 3 and 4 does not impair causal identification. The assignment procedures, matched node functions, source-package rules, and intervention-defined contrasts remain those accepted in v4. V5 instead declines to choose sample size or retain contrasts on the basis of early treatment-effect estimates. That is compatible with a fixed-design experiment. Predeclaring a full run also prevents the scientific target from drifting toward whichever contrast happens to produce the largest verification effect.

The proposed H1 and H7 AUC range of 0.43 to 0.57, H10 AUC-difference band of ±0.05, H11 lower ratio bound of 0.25, three replications, and F1 single-input rule are fixed numerical conventions. Their being fixed is not by itself justification for their scientific importance or attainable precision. In particular, v5 says the H1 margin follows partly from “expected precision at 180 messages per condition” and that AUC 0.57 is below every positive contrast the design treats as recoverable. Expected precision determines whether a confidence interval can support an equivalence decision, not the smallest substantively consequential leak. Since positive structural effects are unknown before generation, the assertion about every positive contrast is not established. An AUC of 0.57 may be comparable to a small positive ancestral contrast. Keep ±0.07 as an openly stipulated practical tolerance for the prespecified classifier, rather than claiming that sample size or unknown positive effects validate it.

There is a concrete logical error requiring correction. H1 currently says that if the confidence interval excludes AUC 0.57, “the result is reported as a leak.” A chance-centered interval such as 0.46 to 0.54 excludes 0.57 but is evidence against a large upper-tail leak, not evidence for one. Predeclare the following exhaustive rule for each H1 comparison: equivalent at the stipulated tolerance only if the entire two-sided 90% world-cluster interval lies within \[0.43, 0.57\]; detectable leakage beyond tolerance only if the entire interval lies above 0.57 or below 0.43, with label orientation and inversion policy specified before fitting; otherwise inconclusive. If the classifier is always oriented so larger AUC is better, the lower-tail case is replaced by its corresponding inverted-score criterion. Never call an interval that merely excludes 0.57 a leak.

Apply the same interpretive separation to H7 within each access × attribution × prompt-family stratum: failure to demonstrate equivalence can mean limited precision, not detected singleton leakage. H7 already continues to H8 regardless of its result, which is correct. An equivocal H7 result restricts the strength of the claim that singleton information was absent, while the second H8 baseline still tests incremental relational information. A world-held-out classifier that returns near-chance discrimination does not prove that all possible classifiers would be unable to recover the label.

The above change fixes the preregistered decision rule while leaving the fixed numerical margins and contrast structure unchanged. H10’s ±0.05 and H11’s quarter-retention threshold are likewise prespecified operational thresholds, which can be reported without retrospectively claiming that empirical calibration justified them. For H11, the already specified denominator-stability condition is retained.

# 2\. Leakage from the verification run

## Verdict: change \[freeze the proposition matcher and annotation decision rule, with a prespecified failure disposition that cannot select analyses\]

The verification architecture is nearly sufficient. The ten reserved worlds are disjoint from the 60 confirmatory worlds. The new dispatcher is audited for graph correspondence, routing, exact prompt and seed transmission, execution schedules, package rules, metadata parsing, and retries. V5 authorizes only two prospective design branches: F1 is dropped if its one-input synthesis outputs fail the fixed 50-case preflight rule, and a non-deterministic schedule is excluded after the prescribed attempt to repair and rerun it. Recording low observer diversity without altering decoding parameters also respects the fixed-design objective. Predeclared bug fixes to bring code into correspondence with the locked specification are not equivalent to redesign, provided their nature and verification reruns are logged.

The matcher introduces a third potential adaptation route. Part VI proposes a blinded, condition-stratified annotation sample from verification outputs, requires at least 0.85 agreement for each of four judgments in every condition, and says propositional features cannot enter an analysis unless the relevant validation succeeds. If the verification result determines which condition-specific feature families are included in the confirmatory models, the analysis specification changes after observing generated material, notwithstanding the prohibition on calculating hypothesis metrics. It may also produce incomparable feature sets across treatment conditions. The criterion’s unqualified percent agreement can look satisfactory when omissions or alterations are rare, even if the matcher misses the rare outcomes that would carry the ancestry signal.

Required fixed rule: before generating verification data, lock the proposition-matcher code, label definitions, blinded annotation sample-selection procedure, unit and denominator for each judgment, class-prevalence reporting, and the pass/fail criterion. If 0.85 is retained, evaluate it on a class-balanced or class-sensitive basis rather than overall accuracy that can be dominated by unchanged propositions. State how an unobserved label or inadequate sample size is marked as unvalidated. Do not train or revise the matcher using these verification labels. Predeclare identical confirmatory model specifications for all conditions, including a non-propositional specification as the primary fallback if validation fails. A failed matcher check may limit the substantive inference about inherited omissions and errors, but it must not trigger unplanned condition-specific feature selection, new training data, a changed hypothesis, or a revision of the design.

The annotation can still be carried out while full generation runs if it is purely an audit whose outcome cannot alter feature definitions or analysis. If the matcher fails, the locked non-propositional analyses are reported and the propositional results are explicitly labeled unvalidated, with the relevant inheritance-mechanism claim withheld rather than patched. This separates technical verification from using verification outcomes to choose the estimand. It also preserves Eric’s requirement that the experiment run once in full.

# 3\. Scope relative to the submitted manuscript

## Verdict: change \[correct the fidelity-calibration row and explicitly characterize the replacement as a new, narrower empirical claim\]

I read main\_manuscript\_npj\_complexity\_v2.docx as well as v5’s Part XIII. In the submitted manuscript, the main experiment was eight-motif classification with a surface-form classifier and a prompted reader that received message content and, at richer levels, rendered trace information. Figure 4 and its Results section explicitly analyze the observability sweep at L1, L3, L5, and L6. The original manuscript also reports a candidate-source origin posterior, an independence judgment, and the reader’s fidelity posterior. The trace sweep was part of the stated residue-method-alignment account, not an incidental robustness check. A resubmission cannot silently preserve that paper’s empirical conclusion while deleting this channel.

The omission itself is defensible for the revised scientific target. The original collection narrative at high observability encoded route length and intermediary types, creating a direct path from label-bearing protocol metadata to the reader’s answer. The new question is whether message text, or relations among a set of messages, carries traces of actual ancestry when node instructions are matched. Adding an L1-to-L6 metadata-validity factorial to the new experiment would answer a separate question about reader use of provenance cues and would partly restore the original identification problem. A message-only prompted reader is already present. No metadata-only follow-up cell or candidate-source set is necessary for this paper. The original trace findings can be documented in the protocol-confound account, not carried over as an independent result about message residue.

The scope map does contain one misleading equivalence. It says “Calibration and the reader’s fidelity confidence” are kept as Brier score and log loss, while admitting that the fidelity posterior is not collected. Brier score and log loss measure calibration of the structure predictions for which probabilities are elicited. They do not measure the original reader’s confidence that the message faithfully preserved its source, nor its correlation with source-content preservation. Replace the row with: “Calibration of structural predictions is retained, assessed with Brier score and log loss. The separate fidelity-confidence posterior and its calibration or correlation with source-content preservation are dropped.”

Part XIII should also state that the reworked paper is a replacement empirical argument, not a direct confirmatory replication of the rejected eight-motif accuracy figures, the original reader-plus-trace comparison, or the original Fig. 4\. The old classifier accuracies and compound defect may appear as a diagnosis of protocol confounding only, with explicit provenance from the deposited records. The new manuscript will require a rewritten abstract, claims, figures, and Methods to match the new observation units and controls. This correction concerns the truth of the scope description, not the addition of another experiment. An original reviewer may legitimately expect an explanation of where the trace channel went, and Part XIII can supply it.

# 4\. Precision at 60 worlds and three replications

## Verdict: agree

I do not recommend cutting a planned contrast solely because 60 worlds and three replications might yield an inconclusive interval. The experiment is finite, predeclared, and structurally informative even when a positive classifier does not distinguish conditions or a negative control cannot prove a tight equivalence claim. The analysis already treats the world as the primary independent unit, clusters intervals accordingly, and preserves matched trials and common-source dependencies. No power calculation in the available documents demonstrates that a particular contrast is incapable of producing interpretable estimates at the fixed scale. Neither does the design warrant a guarantee that small effects will be detected.

The precision caution is most acute for H7’s stratified equivalence claim. With 180 sets per structure within an access × attribution arm, random allocation between prompt families leaves roughly 90 per structure within each family. For two independent samples of about 90 observations each, a rough null AUC standard error is 0.043, yielding a 90% interval half-width near 0.071 even before accounting for world clustering. Actual paired and clustered uncertainty may differ, and the exact result depends on the prespecified estimator, but this calculation explains why a \[0.43, 0.57\] within-stratum equivalence conclusion may often be unattainable even if true AUC is 0.50. That is a limitation of equivalence precision, not grounds to delete H7 or Study 2\. H7 should be allowed to return “inconclusive” rather than being translated into a detected singleton cue.

H1 has about 180 messages per condition across 60 worlds, so its negative-control equivalence test also depends on actual world-cluster variance rather than the total number of generated messages alone. H10’s ±0.05 AUC-difference equivalence may likewise be difficult in a small number of world clusters. H11’s lower confidence bound exceeding 0.25 of above-chance anchored performance is a demanding decision rule when the ratio is noisy. Those outcomes should be reported without selective expansion, deletion, or threshold adjustment. The full-data run has evidentiary value in estimating the size and uncertainty of each effect. Lack of demonstrated equivalence must not be reported as evidence of non-equivalence.

The planned call arithmetic is internally consistent: verification Study 1 uses 1,400 producer calls across the two producers and its second-genre subset, replay requires 2,600, and verification Study 2 requires 480, for 4,480 verification producer calls. Full Study 1 primary generation requires 10,980 producer calls, the second-genre subset 3,240, the second producer 10,980, and Study 2 8,640. Reader work adds 6,120, 1,440, and 6,480 calls respectively. At the provisional nine seconds per call, the full run is about 120 serial hours, before failures and the expected larger synthesis latency. I found no arithmetic basis for dropping a contrast.

# Freeze/preregistration verdict

Decision: v5 can be frozen and preregistered after the exact three changes specified under Items 1–3. It should not be frozen as written because H1 contains a false leak decision rule, the matcher can otherwise select analysis features based on verification outcomes, and the scope table misstates the survival of fidelity confidence. The H1 and matcher corrections protect inferential interpretation, while the scope correction protects correspondence between the old and new papers. Item 4 is accepted without changing sample size or dropping any contrast.

No new experiment, pilot-based parameter setting, adaptive stopping rule, or revision based on hypothesis outcomes is proposed. The permitted F1 and schedule branches remain intact. Before full generation, the project should complete the code and source-lineage verification, lock and preregister the corrected protocol and feature algorithms, run verification solely as an implementation audit, and execute every prespecified contrast at full scale regardless of its performance. That sequence is compatible with a single, precommitted empirical study.
