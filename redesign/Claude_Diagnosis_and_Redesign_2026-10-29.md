## Part I. Diagnosis

### What the original experiment measured

The original design assigned each production motif its own prompt templates at the node that produced the terminal message. In five motifs, the property that defines the motif lies in branches that are not ancestors of the terminal message. Independent corroboration, dependent repetition, common-source laundering, and clustered reinforcement each generate three parallel messages and observe one of them. The other two never influence the observed message. Seen from the message itself, the generating process is only its chain of ancestor calls, and the eight motifs collapse into five ancestry classes: one call on the world facts, a source call followed by one, three, or five rewrites, and a three-input synthesis. Within each class, the messages differ only in the prompt template that produced them, and the 14 features separate them on that basis alone. Single direct and independent corroboration are separated with 100% accuracy under leave-one-world-out cross-validation, although both are a single call on the same world facts.

The compound motif was not composed. The composition function computes the laundering stage's output and never passes it on, so every compound terminal message is an ordinary three-hop chain relay generated from the world facts. Compound and three-hop chain relay are one distribution under two labels.

The classifier therefore recovered generation protocols, not production structure. The reviewer saw this from the manuscript, and the code shows it is complete rather than partial.

### Why reframing cannot repair it

Three problems sit beneath the confound, and they determine the redesign.

The first is the observation unit. The paper's motivating question is whether agreement among messages reflects independent origins. Independence is a relation among messages. A single terminal message cannot carry information about branches that never reached it. The only exception is when the generation protocol differs by branch structure, which is the confound itself. The question as posed is unidentifiable from the data the design collects.

The second is the absence of a structural null. Nothing in the design states what a message should not be able to reveal. Without that, a high recovery rate cannot be distinguished from leakage through protocol, prompt, length instruction, or implementation error. The compound defect went undetected for the same reason. No check compared the realized lineage of each message with the specified graph.

The third is that the constructs had no operational counterpart. Semantic, formal, and trace residue were defined conceptually, but the classifier used a fixed feature list that does not map onto them. Length and lexical features alone account for 61.6% accuracy, against 63.8% for all 14.

The statistical concerns are real but secondary. The headline accuracy is stable across worlds (63.8%, world-cluster interval 61.7 to 65.7%). The per-motif figures, on which the mechanism narratives rest, move by about 20 points across splits.

### What the original apparatus contributes

Several components are sound and can be reused: the world schema with conflicting proposition pairs, the lineage logging, the embedding-based proposition matcher, the residue extractor, and local generation at no API cost. The original 16,800 producer calls and 4,660 reader calls ran from 2026-05-15 to 2026-05-17, about 9 seconds per call including feature extraction. The original run also remains useful as a demonstration. It shows that when protocols differ across motifs, motif recovery benchmarks can report high accuracy for structural properties that the observed artifact cannot contain.

## Part II. Redesigned question

The redesign replaces the single question "can motif class be recovered from a terminal message" with two questions that match their observation units.

**Question 1, single message.** Which properties of its own production history can one message carry? The answer is bounded in principle. A message generated by its ancestor calls is conditionally independent of every non-ancestral part of the graph, given those ancestors. Only properties of the ancestral subgraph are identifiable. The empirical question is how much of that subgraph leaves measurable residue: depth (the number of rewrites), fan-in (the number of combined inputs), whether combined inputs were independent of one another, and how quickly each signal decays under later rewriting.

**Question 2, set of messages.** Given several messages that agree about one event, can their dependence structure be recovered, and from what evidence? Here independence is a property of the observed data. The identifying principle is the one used in textual criticism and phylogenetics. Messages that share an ancestor inherit its idiosyncrasies, such as omissions, alterations, choices among conflicting claims, and phrasing. Independent messages share only what they took from the world. Dependence is evidenced by shared deviations from the world, not by agreement with it.

The two questions connect directly to the original motivation. The paper can now say precisely why agreement is weak evidence of independence (a single report cannot reveal whether other reports were independent). It can also say what evidence does bear on independence (inherited idiosyncrasy across reports) and under what conditions that evidence exists (when independent observers select, err, or resolve conflicts independently).

## Part III. Design principles

1. **Protocol matched across topology.** Every node performs one of three functions: observe the world, rewrite an incoming message, or synthesize several incoming messages. Each function has one pool of prompt variants used in every topology. The variant for each node is drawn uniformly at random, independently of the condition, and recorded. Topology is then the only factor that differs systematically across conditions.
2. **Matched output constraints.** All three functions share the same length and format instruction (continuous prose, 5 to 8 sentences, no headers, lists, titles, or sign-offs). Length differences can then arise only through the process itself, not through instructions that differ by motif.
3. **Ancestry-matched negative controls.** For every topology defined by non-ancestral branches, the design includes a control condition with the same ancestry and no siblings. Prespecified equivalence between the two is the test that the pipeline does not leak protocol.
4. **Protocol factors crossed, not bundled.** Attribution policy (neutral or concealing) is assigned at random to rewrite nodes, independently of topology. Concealment is what distinguishes laundering from dependent repetition. Crossing it lets topology and concealment be estimated separately.
5. **Lineage audit before analysis.** An automated check verifies, for every trial, that each non-source message's prompt contains its parents' texts, and that the realized ancestry of the terminal message equals the specified ancestry. Trials that fail are excluded and reported. This check would have caught the compound defect.
6. **Constructs mapped to measures in advance.** Each feature is assigned to one residue class before data collection, and group-level ablations are a primary analysis.
7. **The world as the unit of inference.** Worlds are crossed with all conditions, all evaluation uses world-grouped splits, and all intervals are world-clustered.

## Part IV. Study 1: what a single message carries

### Conditions

All observer nodes in Study 1 receive a random 70% subset of the world's propositions, always including the core-event proposition. Each observer resolves the world's conflicting proposition pair independently, under its own sampling. This gives independent observers independent content, which the synthesis conditions require. A partial-access condition is also more faithful to independent observation than giving every observer identical facts.

| ID | Condition | Terminal ancestry | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| D0 | Direct: observe, terminal | One observation | Depth 0 reference |
| C1, C2, C3, C5 | Chain: observe, then k rewrites | Depth k | Depth recoverability |
| SI2, SI3 | Synthesis of k independent observations | Fan-in k, independent inputs | Fan-in recoverability |
| SD3 | Synthesis of 3 rewrites of one observation | Fan-in 3, common-source inputs | Input independence from one message |
| SI3-R1, SD3-R1 | SI3 or SD3 followed by 1 rewrite | Fan-in 3 at depth 1 | Decay of the fan-in and independence signals |
| SI3-R3, SD3-R3 | SI3 or SD3 followed by 3 rewrites | Fan-in 3 at depth 3 | Decay of the fan-in and independence signals |
| CIRC | Three nodes, two rounds, each rewrite conditioned on the other two nodes' previous messages | Recurrent, recombinant | Circulation as genuine structure |
| NC-IND | Three independent observations, one observed | One observation (equals D0) | Negative control |
| NC-DEP | One observation, three rewrites, one observed | Depth 1 (equals C1) | Negative control |

That is 15 conditions. SD3 against SI3 is the single-message version of the independence question that the design can answer, because the synthesized message is the ancestor-dependent product of its inputs. The decay conditions measure how many downstream rewrites erase that evidence.

### Hypotheses

- **H1, negative controls.** NC-IND is indistinguishable from D0, and NC-DEP is indistinguishable from C1. The test is binary classification under world-grouped cross-validation, with equivalence declared if the world-clustered 90% interval for AUC lies within 0.45 to 0.55. If either control fails, the pipeline leaks protocol and the main analyses are not interpreted until the source is found.
- **H2, depth.** Depth is recoverable above chance, and the propositional drift from the world (omission, alteration, qualification) increases with depth. The test is ordinal regression of depth on features, with drift metrics by depth.
- **H3, fan-in.** Synthesis conditions are distinguishable from chain conditions of equal call count.
- **H4, input independence.** SI3 is distinguishable from SD3. The predicted mechanism is that independent inputs carry different proposition subsets and conflict resolutions, so their synthesis contains more qualified claims and more explicitly conflicting claims than a synthesis of three rewrites of one observation.
- **H5, decay.** The SI3 versus SD3 discrimination declines with downstream rewrites. The estimand is AUC at depths 0, 1, and 3 after synthesis.
- **H6, protocol invariance.** A topology classifier trained on prompt variants 1 and 2 retains its accuracy on variants 3 and 4. A classifier trained on one producer family retains it on another. A large loss would mean the classifier still depends on protocol.

## Part V. Study 2: what a set of messages carries

### Conditions

Every observed message in Study 2 is exactly one rewrite from an observation, so no single-message cue can separate the structures. Only the relations among messages differ.

| ID | Structure | Observed set |
|---|---|---|
| IND | Three observations, each rewritten once | Three messages with no shared ancestor |
| DEP | One observation rewritten three times | Three messages sharing one ancestor |
| MIX | One observation rewritten twice, plus a second observation rewritten once | Two messages sharing an ancestor, one independent |

These three structures are crossed with observer access (full access to all propositions, or a random 70% subset with independent conflict resolution) and with attribution policy on the rewrites (neutral or concealing). That gives 12 conditions. DEP with concealment is common-source laundering in its proper form: several agreeing messages, one concealed ancestor, and no attribution cues.

### Tasks and methods

The tasks are set classification (IND, DEP, or MIX) and pairwise dependence detection, meaning whether two messages share an ancestor.

- **World-anchored shared deviation.** For each pair, the method computes the excess of shared omissions, shared alterations, and shared conflict resolutions over the rate expected if the two messages deviated from the world independently. It uses the existing proposition matcher. It is an upper reference, because a field analyst does not know the world.
- **World-free shared idiosyncrasy.** For each pair, the method computes the overlap of rare word sequences, the similarity of sentence order, and the embedding similarity in excess of the similarity expected between independent reports of the same event. This is the field-relevant method.
- **Supervised classifier.** A classifier on the pairwise features, under world-grouped cross-validation.
- **Prompted reader.** A language model sees the three messages and judges which share a source, zero-shot and with labeled examples from training worlds.

### Hypotheses

- **H7.** Pairwise dependence is detectable above chance by the world-anchored method under partial access.
- **H8.** Detection is weaker under full access than under partial access, because independent observers with identical facts converge. Independence is evidenced only to the extent that independent sources select or err independently.
- **H9.** Concealment does not reduce detection by the shared-deviation methods, because it removes attribution cues and leaves inherited content in place. Concealment does reduce detection by the prompted reader, to the extent the reader relies on attribution cues. This is the formal statement of why laundering works on readers who judge sources by attribution.
- **H10.** The world-free method recovers a measurable fraction of the world-anchored method's performance. The size of that fraction is the practical result.

## Part VI. Measurement

Features are assigned to residue classes before data collection.

| Class | Features | Requires world? |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | Token count, sentence count, mean sentence length, type-token ratio, mean word length, punctuation density | No |
| Attributional | Source markers, evidential markers, hedges, uncertainty markers | No |
| Propositional | Preservation, omission, alteration, qualification, unsupported addition, conflict-pair resolution, proposition order | Yes |
| Relational (Study 2) | Shared deviations, rare word-sequence overlap, order similarity, excess embedding similarity | Partly |

The semantic class of the original paper becomes the propositional class, which the matcher measures directly against the world. The trace channel is dropped from the primary design. Its manipulation affected only the prompted reader, and it diverted attention from the structural question. A generic sentence-embedding classifier is reported beside the construct-mapped groups as a reference for what an unconstrained text classifier achieves.

The method comparison is no longer the central contrast. Where readers and classifiers are compared, they receive the same information (message text only) and comparable supervision (labeled examples from training worlds supplied to the reader). The remaining difference between in-context examples and fitted parameters is stated as such.

## Part VII. Generation and scale

**Worlds.** The design uses 60 worlds: the existing 20 plus 40 new worlds generated under the same schema. Each new world has at least one conflicting proposition pair, and event types are distributed across domains so that no domain is tied to a condition. Eric reviews the new worlds before generation.

**Producers and readers.** The primary producer is llama3.1:8b, as in the original run. A second producer family is used for the H6 transfer test. Readers come from a different family than the producer in the primary analysis. All models run locally.

**Replication.** Study 1 uses 4 replicates per condition per world, and Study 2 uses 3.

**Planning estimate**, derived from the original throughput of about 9 seconds per call. These are estimates, not measurements.

| Component | Units | Producer calls | Reader calls | Approximate time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Study 1, primary producer | 3,600 messages | 16,800 | 14,400 (2 readers, zero-shot and few-shot) | 78 hours |
| Study 1, second producer (H6) | 3,600 messages | 16,800 | none required | 42 hours |
| Study 2 | 2,160 sets | 10,800 | 4,320 | 38 hours |

The total is roughly one week of machine time with no API cost. The extensions in Part X would add to this. Crossing three genres would roughly triple Study 1 and Study 2 unless replicates were reduced to keep the totals fixed. The random-graph sweep would add an amount proportional to its size. Even with the extensions, the full program would be measured in weeks of machine time, not months. A pilot on 10 worlds precedes the full run. It tests H1 (the leak check), confirms the lineage audit, and supplies the variance estimates used to confirm the replicate counts.

## Part VIII. Analysis plan

- All evaluation uses world-grouped cross-validation, with 10 folds of 6 worlds.
- Accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, multiclass Brier score, and log loss are reported with world-cluster bootstrap intervals (5,000 resamples).
- Contrasts between conditions (H3 to H5, H8, H9) are estimated as differences in AUC or accuracy, using world-cluster bootstrap intervals and mixed-effects logistic models with world random effects.
- The negative controls use the equivalence criterion in H1.
- Only prespecified subgroup analyses are interpreted. Every subgroup percentage is reported with its count and interval.
- Hypotheses, conditions, features, margins, and exclusion rules are preregistered before the pilot. Pilot data are not used for confirmatory tests.

## Part IX. What the resulting paper claims

If the predictions hold, the paper claims four things, all within language-model pipelines. A single message carries information about its own ancestral subgraph and none about its siblings. Depth and fan-in leave measurable residue, and so does whether a synthesis drew on independent inputs, with a measurable rate of decay under later rewriting. The independence of agreeing messages is identifiable from shared deviations across the set, to the degree that independent sources select or err independently. Concealment of attribution defeats attribution-based judgment but not inheritance-based evidence. The original run appears as a demonstration that protocol-confounded designs overstate structural recovery.

If the predictions fail, the failures are informative. Failed negative controls would indicate leakage, which would itself need explaining before any structural claim. A null result for H4 or H7 would bound what synthesized or agreeing artifacts can reveal under faithful transmission. Proposition preservation averaged 0.96 in the original run, so faithful transmission may leave little inherited idiosyncrasy. That possibility is why observer access is a manipulated factor rather than an assumption.

The scope remains machine agents. Rewriting by language models is not human retelling, and transfer to human communication requires independent evidence. The world-anchored methods need a known world and serve as references. The world-free methods are the ones relevant to field use.

## Part X. Alternative structures, messages, and data

The design above keeps the original genre and named structures where they serve the question. Several departures would serve it better, and I recommend three of them.

### Structures

**Named motifs versus a parametric graph space.** The eight original motifs were chosen for coverage of real phenomena, and the names invited the call graphs to be built to match the labels. An alternative is to sample random directed acyclic production graphs and regress recoverability on properties of the terminal message's ancestral subgraph: depth, fan-in, the number of distinct root observations, the number of paths from the roots, and the position of synthesis along the path. Named conditions communicate well, but they test a few points in a space whose dimensions are not separately varied. **Recommendation:** keep the named conditions in Study 1 for interpretability and add a random-graph sweep (for example, 200 graphs per world block, depth up to 5, fan-in up to 4). The sweep would estimate how each ancestral property contributes to recoverability, and it would protect against the result depending on the chosen motifs.

**Fan-out.** Fan-out is the defining feature of most of the original coordination motifs, and it has no single-message signature by construction. It belongs only in Study 2, where its consequence (several messages with a shared ancestor) is observed.

### Messages

**Genre.** Operational incident reports are a narrow and stylistically flat genre, and language models write them in a uniform register. This limits the idiosyncrasy available for inheritance and ties the results to one document type. **Recommendation:** cross genre with topology using two or three genres (incident report, news brief, and short social post), with prompt pools per genre and per node function. Topology effects that hold across genres are stronger evidence than effects in one genre. Genre-by-topology interaction is itself informative, because short posts may preserve less structure than reports.

**World richness.** Original worlds have roughly 8 to 10 propositions, and mean preservation was 0.96. That leaves little room for heritable variation. **Recommendation:** enlarge the worlds to about 20 propositions, including several numeric details (times, counts, durations) and two conflicting pairs. Numbers are useful inheritance markers because rewriting perturbs them in idiosyncratic ways ("8 minutes" becoming "about 10 minutes"), and descendants carry the perturbation. Partial access over a larger proposition set also gives independent observers more distinct selections.

### Data with known lineage outside the simulation

The redesign is still entirely synthetic. The strongest available improvement is a field anchor: human-produced texts whose derivation is known. Three kinds of resource exist and should be verified before use.

- **Journalistic text reuse.** The METER corpus (Gaizauskas, Clough, and colleagues, around 2001) labels British newspaper articles as wholly derived, partially derived, or not derived from Press Association newswire copy. It is direct ground truth for the Study 2 question in human-written news.
- **Artificial manuscript traditions.** Stemmatology benchmarks, in which volunteers hand-copied a text along a known copying tree, were built to evaluate methods of reconstructing the tree from copies (for example, the Heinrichi and Notre Besoin traditions associated with Roos, Heikkilä, Baret, and colleagues). They test shared-error inference on human copying.
- **Press releases and resulting news.** Studies linking university press releases to the news coverage they generated (for example, Sumner and colleagues, 2014) give known source-to-derivative pairs. Large quotation-tracking corpora give derivation chains with mutation.

**Recommendation:** add one field-anchored validation, most practically on journalistic reuse. Apply the world-free shared-idiosyncrasy method (Study 2) to the human corpus without retraining and report how much of its synthetic performance carries over. This converts the paper's transfer limitation from a stated caveat into a measured boundary, and it does not require a new field study. The corpora's availability, licensing, and exact labels must be verified first. The citations above are from memory and must be checked against their sources before they enter any document.

### What I would not change

The world-held-fixed logic is the design's main strength and should stay. Local models should also stay. They keep the cost at zero and make the full design repeatable, and frontier models can be added later as a robustness reader on a subset if a venue expects them.
