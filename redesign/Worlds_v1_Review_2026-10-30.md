## How a world is used

An observer receives a world's facts, or an assigned share of them, each with its in-world source, and writes the first report. Every later message descends from those reports. Each world has exactly one core fact (the central event), several facts with numbers, and two conflicting pairs: two facts that cannot both be true, from different sources. How messages resolve those conflicts, and which facts they drop or change, is the main trace of a message's history.

## Questions for reviewers

1. Is each conflicting pair a genuine incompatibility, such that a careful writer could not report both as true, and is neither member obviously the right one?
2. Is any fact so salient or so mundane that every writer will keep it, or drop it, regardless of the message's history? A few of each are fine, but a world made only of these carries little trace.
3. Do the five worlds of an event type differ enough from one another, and do the fourteen types differ enough, that a classifier cannot learn a world's quirks instead of structure?
4. Does any world contain real organizations, people, or places, or content a model might refuse to write about?
5. Are the sources plausible for the facts they are attached to, and varied enough that a Sources line carries information?
