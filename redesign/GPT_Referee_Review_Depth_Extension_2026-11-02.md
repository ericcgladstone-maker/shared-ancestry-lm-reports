# Independent referee review: TACL manuscript and deeper-rewriting extension

**Date:** November 2, 2026  
**Manuscript:** *Detecting Shared Ancestry Among Language-Model Reports*  
**Disposition:** **Major revision. TACL remains a defensible first venue, but the extension does not yet resolve the significance objection.**

## Review basis and scope

I read `redesign/GPT_Brief_Depth_Extension_Review_2026-11-02.md` before the other materials, then compared `build/runs/depth/PLAN.md`, `build/run_depth.py`, `build/analyze_depth.py`, `build/runs/depth/analysis.md`, the November 2 entries in `build/LOG.md`, and the current `tacl/main.tex` and compiled `tacl/main.pdf`. I inspected the PDF's figure, section placement, references, and appendices. I also inspected `build/runs/depth/sets.jsonl` programmatically rather than accepting the manuscript's account of key sharing. I checked the appendix rule against TACL's February 16, 2024 announcement and current submission instructions. I did not regenerate the model outputs or rerun the 39-minute analysis. The numerical results below are the recorded results, with the source code and saved set-level construction checked independently.

The extension addresses the manuscript's most important prior limitation, inference from a single rewrite of a shared parent. Its central new observation is that binary relational AUC remains 0.855 after five rewrites although the pooled mean/max baseline falls to 0.577. This is relevant to NLP. It is not, however, evidence that relational information becomes intrinsically purer with depth, and the three qualitative readings fixed by the extension plan were not met in their specified forms. This discrepancy between a promising numerical result and its interpretation is the chief revision issue.

## 1. Plan fidelity and the audit of implementation

**Generation: substantially compliant.** The plan specifies 60 existing worlds, three replicates per world, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, full access, the neutral arm, and the three matched structures. It specifies four new rewrite calls on each of five distinct depth-1 terminal reports per block. `run_depth.py` implements those choices. Its step-2 through step-5 templates are drawn deterministically from the block's assigned family using a seed that includes world, replicate, report position, and step. Every call key includes the position, depth, and parent key. Shared-parent descendants therefore reuse a stored output rather than independently rewriting ostensibly shared text. Generation order among the three structures is shuffled within block. The depth-1 inputs are retrieved from the full-run store, not newly generated. The configured run cap is $1.50 on top of the provider ledger.

The saved `sets.jsonl` contains exactly **2,700 set rows**, namely 180 world/replicate blocks, three structures, and five depths. I checked all **900 block-by-depth groups**. The keys and text agree with the specified sharing relationships in every group: position 1 is shared by all three structures, the Y structure's second terminal is shared with out-star, and its third with independent. Positions 2 and 3 do not coincide between independent and out-star. There were no counterexamples in these checks. The recorded run made **3,600 new calls**, with no recorded errors and a Google cost of approximately **$0.587**. The run configuration's start time is **14:22:25 UTC** on November 2, following the plan file's 14:22:00 UTC modification time. This is evidence of temporal priority for the plan, although file timestamps by themselves are not cryptographic preregistration.

**Analysis: substantially compliant.** `analyze_depth.py` uses depths 1, 2, 3, and 5. It employs the project's canonical feature, out-of-fold learner, five-fold world assignment, rarity-by-outer-fold, own-world removal, and world-cluster uncertainty routines. Depth 4 is generated but not analyzed, as planned. It computes the pooled and relational AUCs, their paired gain, the nine label-unfitted scores, relational-minus-selected-score paired AUC difference, three-structure macro AUCs, and the two single-report controls. The selected score is constructed against unlabeled outer-training-world text, and its AUC is evaluated on held-out worlds. “Unfitted” consequently means no fitting to structure labels, not independence from a reference corpus. The depth-1 verification reports pooled 0.6989, relational 0.9077, and selected score 0.8631, matching the full run at reported precision.

**Reporting: one meaningful departure.** The plan says that all nine unfitted statistics are to be presented in an appendix table **across the analyzed depths**. The generated `analysis.md` contains that four-depth table, but Appendix B of the manuscript contains the nine-statistic table for the original one-rewrite analysis across producers, not their trajectories at depths 1, 2, 3, and 5. Figure 5 contains the selected score's trajectory but not the other eight. Moreover, the planned paired differences at the intermediate depths (0.052 and 0.049) are absent from the paper. This is a reporting shortfall rather than an implementation failure. It matters because the plan explicitly sought to distinguish persistence of simple overlap from an emerging advantage for combining features. The missing appendix table would let readers assess whether another unfitted statistic behaves differently across depth.

## 2. Matched marginal distributions at depth

**The distributional equality is preserved by the generating design, conditional on the design's stated stochastic assumptions.** At each position, the initial observation has the same prompt family, input record, producer, sampling regime, and rewrite role across structures. Every additional depth uses the same position- and depth-indexed template distribution and the same stateless model. If the parent report for that position has the same marginal distribution in all three structures, applying the same rewrite transition preserves that marginal at the next depth. This is an induction argument, not an inference from the classifier's chance-level performance. Importantly, the sharing pattern determines *dependencies between* the positions, which is why set-level discrimination remains possible.

The stored-key audit also rules out a different implementation error: a shared text being regenerated separately in one structure, or an independent text being inadvertently reused at a position that should not be shared. It does **not** prove identical empirical token distributions from a finite sample, rule out provider drift or order effects, or validate the model as a general stochastic kernel outside the tested calls. At each depth the out-star and independent sets are not merely assigned the same source facts. Their corresponding terminal positions also have the same ancestral generation procedure, differing in whether those upstream realizations are shared across positions.

The unshared-text single-report results are: feature learner 0.527, 0.536, 0.546, and 0.547 at depths 1, 2, 3, and 5, with 90% intervals; embedding learner 0.490, 0.473, 0.500, and 0.493. The feature intervals at depths 3 and 5 are [0.519, 0.571] and [0.520, 0.574], so they narrowly fail the prespecified **equivalence** criterion. Neither interval establishes positive discrimination under the plan's rule. The manuscript reports this correctly, without converting “inconclusive” into “equivalent.” The direction of the point estimates warrants transparency, but another mechanism need not be invented to explain them.

The plan's sentence that a single-report result outside the equivalence band would mean that “the matched-marginal construction has failed” is logically too strong. Even a significant single-report result could indicate random variation, nonstationary generation, model selection, or an implementation artifact, rather than a mathematical failure of the intended construction. The plan's *operational check* is useful, but its proposed diagnostic implication is not a theorem. This issue does not invalidate the present depth results, since no single-report interval lies wholly outside the band.

## 3. Adjudication of the four planned readings

| Planned reading | Specified criterion | Observation | Referee judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decay | Relational AUC **and gain** fall with depth | Relational AUC 0.908 → 0.855, but gain 0.209 → 0.278 | **Not met as specified.** One component declines and the other increases. |
| Divergence | Relational-minus-unfitted-score paired difference grows and excludes zero | Differences 0.045, 0.052, 0.049, 0.059, all with intervals excluding zero | **Not met.** A positive advantage exists at each depth, but growth is not demonstrated. |
| Persistence | Depth-5 relational AUC falls within the depth-1 relational AUC interval | 0.855 versus depth-1 interval [0.875, 0.937] | **Not met under the plan's explicit criterion.** Discrimination remains high, which is a different, weaker claim. |
| Single-report check | No outcome demonstrating discriminability beyond the chance band | Embedding equivalent at all depths; feature equivalence at 1–2, inconclusive at 3 and 5 | **No affirmative contrary result.** Marginal construction remains credible but the feature learner does not affirm equivalence at 3 or 5. |

Section 4.4 currently says separation “declined slowly” and that pairwise similarities “retain most of theirs.” These are defensible descriptions of the **observed AUC levels**, if qualified as descriptive. It must explicitly say that the strict persistence criterion was not satisfied, rather than allowing the reader to infer that the plan's persistence prediction succeeded. The text correctly notes that the trained-model advantage over the selected score did not grow detectably. The increase in pooled-versus-relational gain is not evidence that either model improved. Both declined, but pooled AUC declined more.

**The phrase “more purely relational” in the Discussion is not supported as an inferential conclusion.** The design already makes every structure's *single-report marginal* the same at each depth. All predictive information about structure is therefore a property of the **joint** distribution from depth 1 onward. A mean/max summary is itself a statistic of the joint set, even if it contains no explicitly computed pairwise similarity. What grows is the AUC difference between two feature representations, not the proportion of ancestry information that is ontologically relational. The decline in pooled AUC from 0.699 to 0.577, compared with the smaller decline in relational AUC from 0.908 to 0.855, supports the narrower statement: *At five rewrites, the tested pairwise features retain substantially more discriminative performance than the selected mean/max summaries.* Neither feature set identifies a channel of linguistic transmission, and no stronger pooled or permutation-invariant alternative was tested.

The result is therefore interesting for **robustness under paraphrase**, not for identifying a progressively purer mechanism. Nor does the growing numerical gain establish a statistically detectable *change in the gap across depths*. The reported intervals compare models within each depth and are conditional world-cluster intervals on fixed predictions. A paired interval for the change from depth 1 to 5, using shared worlds, was not reported. The separate within-depth intervals should not be treated as such a test.

## 4. The selected overlap score and paired comparison

The sequence is sufficiently documented for a conditional prospective comparison: nine unfitted statistics were examined **after** the original depth-1 results, maximum rare-sequence overlap was selected, and the depth plan then named that statistic and the paired comparison before new deeper texts were generated. The plan was not written before the depth-1 data or the score selection. The manuscript's Section 4.4 accurately says the score was selected in a post hoc check and the comparison was fixed before deeper texts existed. Section 4.1 and Appendix B also identify the original check as post hoc.

**One correction is necessary.** The interval at **depth 1** for relational minus score, 0.045 [0.016, 0.074], is a post-selection estimate on the data used to select the benchmark. It should not be grouped without qualification with the planned depth-2, depth-3, and depth-5 comparisons. Those later comparisons were specified before *their new terminal texts* existed, but they share worlds, prompts, and depth-1 ancestors with the selected data. They therefore constitute a planned follow-on test conditional on the earlier score selection, not an independent validation on new worlds. Intervals also do not account for searching among nine candidate scores. Describing the score as “unfitted” is fine so long as the paper continues to say that rarity is built from unlabeled training texts.

The observation that the advantage remains around five AUC points is not an emerging trained-model breakthrough. The score itself retains AUC 0.796 after five rewrites. The paired gap is 0.059 [0.024, 0.096], compared with 0.045 at depth 1, but the paper correctly says that an increase has not been established. Statistical uncertainty for this *between-depth change* is not supplied. The selected binary task should not be substituted for tests of locating the shared pair or identifying the five exact configurations.

## 5. TACL significance after the extension

**Assessment: improved, still borderline.** The previous submission's most vulnerable generality claim was that detection had only been studied after one rewrite. The new chain experiment demonstrates substantial persistence in **absolute discrimination** to five rewrites, with relational AUC 0.855 and a simple overlap score at 0.796. That observation makes the contribution more substantial than one-step paraphrase overlap. The controlled identical-fact comparison, same-position marginals, multiple producer families at depth 1, and limited but informative configuration recovery are also independent sources of inferential leverage.

Nevertheless, an NLP referee could reasonably argue that this remains a demonstration on 60 synthetic event records, one report genre, a small template library, within-producer homogeneous sets, and one producer at depth greater than 1. The strong untrained rare-overlap baseline, 0.863 at depth 1 and 0.796 at depth 5 for Gemini, reduces the possible algorithmic contribution. The five-rewrite pipeline applies the same kind of instruction repeatedly and does not test mixed producers, compression, synthesis between steps, or adversarial rewording. Source reuse against identical facts remains the central phenomenon. The paper does not show when the distinction becomes impossible or why semantic and order features succeed beyond lexical overlap.

I would **retain TACL for a first submission after substantive reporting corrections**, rather than change venues because the depth experiment failed to meet its stricter readings. TACL assesses substantial original empirical work and permits evaluation research. But acceptance is uncertain, and prose polishing cannot remove the remaining significance risk. A reviewer would not be justified in treating the extension as proof of general network genealogy recovery or robust transfer to deployed systems. If speed or probability of publication dominates disciplinary reach, Scientific Reports remains the more conservative venue. The depth evidence improves the case for TACL, rather than establishing that its contribution threshold has been cleared.

## 6. TACL appendix-policy compliance

I applied the **February 16, 2024 policy**, effective March 1, 2024, rather than the superseded sentence in the 2021 template. TACL permits **10 original content pages**, up to **5 pages of replication detail** (Category 1), and up to **3 pages of complementary tables and figures** (Category 2). Appendices follow the references in one submission PDF and are unreviewed. Free-standing interpretive prose is disallowed outside replication detail (TACL, 2024).

The current 13-page PDF places the Introduction through reproducibility statement on pages 1–9, references across pages 9–11, the methodological appendix (A) beginning on page 11, and complementary tables and figures (B) on pages 12–13. Appendix A's instructions, storage, rarity construction, five-configuration formula, reader sampling, and depth-generation paragraph are legitimate **replication details**. Appendix B's tables and figures qualify as **complementary results** and contain no substantive free-standing narrative. The document therefore appears compliant with the separate page limits. The roughly 9.1 content-page count is below 10. Formal compliance should still be checked using TACL's submission template and the actual final PDF.

One substantive qualification matters. The **decisive evidence for the new inference** should not be located only in an unreviewed appendix. Figure 5 and the core AUCs are in main text, which is appropriate. However, the full four-depth nine-score comparison promised by the plan is absent, and the within-depth paired score differences at depths 2 and 3 are absent from the text. These are needed to assess the divergence reading, whether or not TACL would review the appendix. Main text should at least report the four paired differences and say that no increase is established. Appendix B should carry the full nine-by-four score table if retained. There is room for it under the Category 2 allowance, subject to typesetting.

## 7. Outside-reader edits and remaining prose issues

The November 2 08:50 log describes textual changes responding to a PDF-only outside reader. The current draft implements the material clarifications: it defines observation and rewrite, identifies the primary producer, states the shared stored-text positions, differentiates binary AUC from three-structure and five-configuration tasks, labels the unshared-text check as post hoc, explains the equivalence decision, and avoids claiming that a nonsignificant source-stripping comparison proves equality. Figure 4 now says “Assigned Y.” The depth extension does not reverse these fixes. The Discussion still distinguishes shared upstream production from independently acquired evidence.

Three matters remain relevant to the prose and inference:

1. **Introduction, paragraph 1:** “Three rewrites of one earlier report ... add nothing beyond that report and reproduce its errors and omissions” is too absolute. A rewriting model can change, introduce, omit, or correct textual assertions. The defensible point is that rewrites are **not independently gathered corroboration**, not that downstream text is guaranteed to contain exactly the parent's information or errors. This can be corrected without changing the motivation.
2. **Section 4.4 and Discussion:** “the information available to per-report summaries declines toward chance,” “the pairwise similarities retain most of theirs,” and “more purely relational” should be aligned with the estimand. These are AUC trajectories for specified, fitted feature representations, not direct measurements of Shannon information or an identified mechanism. “Toward chance” describes the observed trend, but 0.577 is not established to be equivalent to 0.5. The Discussion's “more purely relational” is particularly misleading.
3. **Abstract:** the planned depth extension is correctly labeled as planned and restricted to the primary producer. However, “the relational model stays above the unfitted score at every depth” places the selected depth-1 comparison alongside the genuinely prospectively specified deeper comparisons without noting the conditional nature of the selection. The abstract need not reproduce the full chronology, but Section 4.4 should make the distinction explicit. The abstract is also long and densely numerical, an editorial issue rather than an established violation of a TACL abstract-word limit.

The Discussion's application to corroboration remains an **illustrative implication**, not evidence of factual independence. That distinction has been preserved appropriately. No additional question of code/data release was evaluated, as excluded by the brief.

## Required changes for a submission-ready revision

**Major:** Replace the “more purely relational” mechanism-like inference with a representation-specific AUC comparison. Explicitly adjudicate the plan's decay, divergence, and strict persistence readings, rather than quietly substituting a weaker persistence narrative. Restore the planned nine-statistic four-depth appendix table or identify the reporting departure, and put all four within-depth paired comparisons in the main text. Separate depth-1 post-selection uncertainty from the planned follow-on score comparison at deeper levels.

**Minor but material:** Narrow the opening sentence about rewrites reproducing errors. State that the depth-3 and depth-5 single-report tests are inconclusive under the fixed equivalence rule, without suggesting failure of distributional matching. Identify all between-depth changes as descriptive unless accompanied by a paired across-depth interval. The current appendices appear policy-compliant and need no wholesale restructuring.

**Final disposition:** **Major revision, with continued TACL consideration.** The new depth run is a credible and reproducibly documented extension of the matched design. Its numbers strengthen the scope of the empirical observation. They do not support the strongest interpretive sentence in the Discussion and do not satisfy the plan's a priori decay, divergence, or strict persistence patterns. A reviewer should evaluate the study on what it actually establishes: discrimination survives five same-producer paraphrases in a fixed synthetic setting, while the diagnostic advantage of specified relational features over specified mean/max summaries widens descriptively.
