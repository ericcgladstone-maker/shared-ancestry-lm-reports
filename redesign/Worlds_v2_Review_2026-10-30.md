# World Bank, Version 2: For Review

Generated by Claude, 2026-10-30, from `build/worlds/bank.jsonl`. Version 1 is archived in `build/worlds/v1/` and reviewed in `GPT Review of World Bank v1 (2026-10-30)`. Seventy fictional events, five per event type, drafted by Claude Sonnet 5.5 and checked by GPT-6.1 Terra.

## What changed from version 1

- The v1 schema marked conflicts with a 0/1 field, which the drafter read as member order in nine worlds, pairing a cause with a count. Each dispute is now an explicit object holding its two claims, with a kind and a short statement of what is in dispute, so pair membership cannot be mislabeled.
- Each world declares a closed list of four to six sources, and every sentence's source must be on it. This is checked mechanically, as are 15 background facts, two disputes of different kinds, distinct sources within each dispute, no duplicate sentences, and at least four sentences with numbers.
- The drafting prompt follows GPT's replacement text, with these changes: the explicit dispute objects, a dispute kind, a list of invented names already used in other worlds that the drafter must not reuse, and the reasons for rejection when a draft is redrafted.
- Every draft that passes the mechanical checks is read by a checker from a different model family (GPT-6.1 Terra at high reasoning effort). For every sentence it judges whether the source could know it and whether it favors a dispute claim. It also judges whether each dispute's claims share a referent, are incompatible, and are balanced, and it looks for inconsistencies and real referents. Any defect sends the draft back with the reasons, up to four attempts. A world with no clean attempt keeps its attempt with the fewest defects and is flagged below.
- Sentence order is shuffled with a seed derived from the world ID, so the core and the disputes do not sit at fixed positions.
- Twenty-four worlds had no draft that passed the checker within four attempts. Each was repaired in place: the drafter received its best draft and the checker's defects, changed only the sentences named, and the checker re-read the result (`build/repair_worlds.py`, logged in `worlds/repair_log.jsonl`). Twenty passed. Claude edited the remaining four by hand, along with W011, whose time dispute set two devices' records against each other, which the checker had passed. Every hand edit is logged with its before and after text in `worlds/hand_edits.jsonl`, and the checker re-read each edited world. Repaired and hand-edited worlds are marked below.

## Questions for reviewers

1. Is each dispute a genuine incompatibility about one referent, such that a careful writer could not report both claims as true, and is neither claim obviously right?
2. Does any background fact or the core settle a dispute?
3. Could every source observe or document what is attributed to it?
4. Do the worlds vary enough, within and across event types, that a classifier cannot learn the drafting template instead of structure?
5. Does any world contain real organizations, people, places, or incidents, or content a model might refuse to write about?
