## Proposed title and one-sentence thesis

**Working title:** *Can Shared Ancestry Be Recovered from Language-Model Reports? Controlled Tests of Textual Dependence*.

Alternate, if the paper must emphasize output-only observability: *Tracing Shared Ancestry from Uninstrumented Language-Model Outputs*.

**Thesis, supported within the tested study design:** When separately presented reports are produced under matched marginal conditions, information about whether they descend from a common source can remain in relations among their texts. The estimand is incremental prediction using explicit cross-message relations over a baseline that has access to all individual-message features. This is not equivalent to proving that isolated messages have exactly zero structural information, reconstructing an arbitrary hidden graph, or identifying the literal ancestral text.

## Claim types and reporting discipline

A **design property** is fixed by the generator, matching rule, and intervention and needs implementation verification, not an inferential significance test. A **primary estimate** is the specified contrast from the evaluation procedure, with its canonical point estimate and inference method. A **secondary analysis** evaluates an additional contrast or sensitivity without the status of the primary test. An **implementation check** verifies whether the intended pipeline, randomization, isolation, or feature construction operated. An **interpretation** links those observations to a limited substantive inference and must remain conditional on competing explanations.

The same conceptual hypothesis may need multiple ledger entries if it combines a design assertion, statistical estimate, and interpretation. In particular, the statement that single-message marginals are matched **by construction** does not entail that every realized classifier is equivalent to chance. Likewise, a relational gain over a pooled baseline does not prove that inherited deviations, rather than pairwise lexical or semantic dependence, provide the gain.

## Article architecture and evidence ledger

### 1. Introduction: the dependence problem

An apparently corroborating collection can contain descendants of one prior report rather than independent observations. A claim of independent corroboration requires knowledge of the dependence structure, which may not accompany the final reports. Existing source tracing often presumes that candidate upstream texts are available. Instrumented agent tracing can preserve agent identities or generation-time signatures. This paper tests what information remains when the objects supplied to the predictor are final text outputs only. Present the specific inferential target as distinguishing the **observed output-set genealogy**, not reconstructing unrestricted networks from arbitrary messages.

### 2. Design and methods before Results

The paper should give the reader the random assignment and the measurement constraints before reporting discrimination. Depict the three-member structures **IND** (independent roots), **DEP** (common root, out-star), and **Y/MIX** (one related pair, one separate root). Every observed report is produced via a matched single rewrite after its observation ancestor. Under **full access**, observation roots receive the same event record. In the separate **partial-access** arm, structures also differ in package sharing, so its contrast does not identify pure inherited production. Generation slots are not supplied as metadata. **Classifier sets were held in generation order**, although their features are permutation-symmetric and an all-six-order refitting audit established invariance. **Prompted readers alone received randomized presentation order.** Stored upstream outputs are intentionally reused across conditions, making world-grouped splitting necessary.
